### Top 10 transferable techniques (explainer), ranked

Ranked by how many outliers use it, how strongly it separates outliers from controls, and how cheap it is in code (Remotion + our explainer kit).

1. **Second-person line 1.** The viewer is the diver, the victim or the shrunken protagonist from the first sentence. In Shorts, read the title back as "When a X ... you" at frame 0. (fGcgWaswMns, 6mRR91AbxjQ, XFFjxTllpoA, QxM2Hu70tVk, Tvqq_1GAPOQ)
2. **Every number drawn as a familiar object**, on screen and animated: bus, 6-7 elephants, coin next to the jellyfish, "you holding your breath for 0:15". Never a number that is only spoken. (fGcgWaswMns, eWu92oXtxsw, fKizW6NlnoA, jw6bS-WL4F4)
3. **Plant-and-payoff open loop plus a bookend.** Plant a sensory detail in the hook and pay it off at 30-60% ("that knocking was you being scanned"). The last line rephrases the opening rule. (fGcgWaswMns, qc5nK6_9VBs, jw6bS-WL4F4, W9m89Z35Aho)
4. **A visible progress spine.** Use depth zones that light up, a collection silhouette grid, numbered CHALLENGER gates or a period ruler, so viewers can see how much is left. (mzeN0BXR4e0, QRcaiIwyym0, Tvqq_1GAPOQ, fKizW6NlnoA)
5. **Question card, then a one-word answer, as the chapter turn.** For example "WHAT DOES IT EAT?" then "SQUID."; "NO LIGHT. SO HOW DOES IT HUNT?" then "SOUND." It re-hooks every 20-60 s. (fGcgWaswMns, W9m89Z35Aho)
6. **"Your options" survival menu.** Each option is struck through with a joke, ending on a red verdict line. This turns facts into a game the viewer plays. (fGcgWaswMns, QRcaiIwyym0; kit: `explainer/Options`)
7. **Subversion every round.** Name the answer the viewer expects (or list the usual suspects), then reveal the real one. (fKizW6NlnoA, eWu92oXtxsw, jw6bS-WL4F4, qc5nK6_9VBs)
8. **Counters and meters for stats.** Use a dB bar, depth counter, count-up bite force or isotype grid, so the number moves while it is said. (fGcgWaswMns, eWu92oXtxsw, mzeN0BXR4e0; kit: `Stat`, `DepthMeter`, `SpeedBars`)
9. **Shorts mechanics.** Use 2-4 word caption cards (about 2.8 s), 230-280 wpm TTS, a glow on the body part being named, a surface view that morphs into a cutaway, and a last line that loops back into the first. (6mRR91AbxjQ, XffxtLGdXUQ, Ky0XPonYDcc)
10. **The thumbnail adds a new claim.** Use 2-4 words in white with one red word ("IT HEARS YOU.."), a tiny human with a red arrow, and the monster at a much larger scale. For Shorts, frame 0 is the thumbnail, so open on the most legible threat image. (fGcgWaswMns, 2819-mQEA9M, mzeN0BXR4e0)

### Top 10 transferable techniques (horror), ranked

1. **Threat, reassurance, knock, inside 12 s.** False safety is set up and inverted before a viewer can swipe away. (7654526047005723937, 7659677578365537569)
2. **The phone is the storytelling engine.** Group chat with names and unit numbers, a lock-screen notification as the cold open, an incoming call, a 1% battery. It's cheap to render, readable while muted, and one narrator can voice every character. (7654526047005723937, 7659677578365537569, 7666044652910316833, Xv32FAAUI-0; kit: `HandPhone`, `FeaturePhone`)
3. **Plant a rule in the first 3 s and pay it off at the climax.** Each rule has a trigger, a consequence and a countermeasure, and a minor rule gets broken early as foreshadowing. (7664930759395888416, 7702661541224566049, 7692268145888873760, BciSW_2VMZg)
4. **Compromised authority and trust ladder.** The cop is fake, the chat is compromised, an ally's message gets visibly deleted or muted, and every helper becomes a suspect. (7654526047005723937, 7659677578365537569)
5. **A re-hook every 15-25 s:** a new anomaly, a counted clue or a moving number ("41 members, now 42"). (7654526047005723937, 7659677578365537569, CQKPC9MlciE)
6. **End mid-sentence.** No outro and no CTA; the cut is the hook for part 2. (7702661541224566049, 7692268145888873760, x--ui6_mAxE)
7. **A diegetic second voice for dramatic irony:** floating comments (danmaku), a speaking-object burst next to the phone or radio, or the phone giving orders. (7654526047005723937, KJfJKo400b0)
8. **Concrete numbers and sensory specifics as credibility:** 47 km to the exit, plate J1574, room 802, 1% battery, "a song plays when they strike". (7702661541224566049, 7659677578365537569)
9. **Limited-animation economy.** Use 2-4 characters with expression swaps (normal, dot-pupil horror eyes, narrowed), held poses, hard cuts and lots of inserts. Show off-screen deaths through reaction close-ups. (7654526047005723937, 7694491037988818208)
10. **Frame 0 as the cover:** a readable threat image plus a burned-in caption sentence that's cut mid-phrase. The picture sits in a band with a small white caption above it (refs use about a 43% picture band; the pilot may go full-bleed but must keep the caption above the action). (7654526047005723937, 7659677578365537569, 7702661541224566049)
