# The Warship That Sank in Its Own Harbour (proposed narration)

Changes against v2, each tied to STORY_SCRIPT_SPEC.md: one-sentence hook (24 -> 21 words, 6.2 -> ~5.5 s); numbers
15 -> 4; facts ~14 -> ~8 (dates, tonnage, distance, death toll, carvings and the 1961 epilogue cut); two quoted character lines
(the admiral, the king's letters) and three dry jokes; four escalation steps; mechanism 56 -> ~40 words; the last line
is the payoff callback to the hook (no museum epilogue, video ends on the word).

## Narration

```narration
[sfx: footsteps] Thirty sailors ran across a brand-new warship until she nearly rolled over, and the navy sent her to sea anyway.

[sfx: whoosh] The admiral stops the test and mutters, "If only the king were here."

[sfx: whoosh] But the king is off at war, and he wants the mightiest warship afloat.

[sfx: impact] So the Vasa gets two decks of heavy bronze cannons, stacked high above the water.

[sfx: knock] Nobody wants to tell the king his masterpiece wobbles. His letters keep arriving: "Get her to sea."

[sfx: crowd] On launch day, a huge crowd watches her sails go up.

[sfx: whoosh] One light gust leans her over. She shakes it off, and the crowd keeps waving.

[sfx: whoosh_long] Then a second gust tips her further, and her open lower gun ports dip under the water.

[sfx: bubbles] The sea pours in, and in front of the whole city, the Vasa sinks inside her own harbour.

[sfx: knock] At the hearing, one builder's best explanation is, "Only God knows." Nobody is punished. The man who ordered the cannons was the king.

[sfx: stinger] You see, a ship stays upright like a bottle with sand in the bottom: heavy low, light on top.

[sfx: impact] The Vasa was built upside down, with tons of bronze up high and too little stone below.

[sfx: click] And more stone would only have pushed those gun ports down to the waterline.

[sfx: footsteps] So when thirty sailors nearly rolled her over at the dock, the Vasa was showing them exactly how she would sink.
```

## Facts (all from the v1 fact list in episodes/story-vasa/script.md)

- The stability test (thirty men, stopped after three runs), the admiral's "wished the king were at home", the king at
  war urging her to sea, two gun decks of bronze guns, the crowd, gust / rights / second gust / lower
  ports under, sank in Stockholm harbour, the inquiry's "Only God knows" and no one found guilty, too little ballast,
  more ballast would have brought the ports to the waterline.
- New wording to check: "inside her own harbour" (she sank about 1,300 m out, in Stockholm harbour);
  "The man who ordered all those cannons was the king" (he set the armament in 1626; the inquiry blamed no one).
- Dramatised: the admiral's line is a paraphrase of testimony; "Get her to sea" paraphrases the king's letters.
