# The Ancient Shield That Heard an Army Underground (draft)

Persian siege, walls that hold (9-18 s); four escalations: the attacks stop, the smith's walk, days of dull thuds, the
ring (18-41 s); pivot "You see" at ~66 %; mechanism in ~40 words (dull over solid earth, humming over a hollow, dig

```narration
[sfx: impact] With an army outside the walls, the city's bronze-smith spent every day laying his shield face-down in the dirt.

[sfx: whoosh] The guards on the wall think the siege has finally cracked him.

[sfx: whoosh_long] Then one week the attacks just stop, and the Persian camp goes strangely quiet.

[sfx: knock] "Lost something?" a guard laughs. "The enemy's out there, not down there."

[sfx: knock] For days, nothing. Just a dull thud, wherever he puts it.

[sfx: stinger] You see, the Persians had stopped attacking the wall because they were digging under it.

[sfx: impact] Over solid ground, a bronze shield just thuds. But over a hollow tunnel, every pick striking below makes it hum like a bell.

[sfx: whoosh] So wherever it rang, the defenders dug straight down and broke into the tunnel from above.

[sfx: underwater_rumble] An army spent months digging in total silence, and a shield lying in the dirt heard every swing.
```

## Facts used (all from the queue's verified list)

- A Persian army (Aryandes) besieged Barca, in Libya, for months, attacking the walls and digging mines toward them.
- A bronze-worker laid a bronze shield on the ground inside the wall; it was dull over solid ground and rang over a
  tunnel. The defenders countermined where it rang and broke into the tunnel. (Herodotus 4.200; Aeneas Tacticus 37.6-7)
- Date: "about 2,500 years ago" (the queue's safe wording; c. 515-510 BC is contested).
- Dramatised (T7): the guards' jeer, the week the attacks stop, the corner tower, the dust on the bronze.

## Shot notes (S-env)

Persian camp on the plain at dusk; the wall walk and the streets behind it; the smith kneeling, ear to the bronze;

earth (still) vs over the tunnel (shivering, rings drawn in the dust); defenders breaking down into the tunnel; end on
