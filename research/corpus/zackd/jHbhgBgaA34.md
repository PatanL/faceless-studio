## 2. Escalation ladder

The ladder is **time**, not attempts. Each rung jumps forward in life while the bad habit stays fixed, and the cost grows from discomfort to loss:

1. **Teen, symptom ignored** (0:00-0:06). A small ache, glowing neck, waved off. Cost: a twinge.
2. **College** (0:06). One shot, one noun. Cost: none shown; the habit just continues.
3. **Desk job, years** (0:07-0:11). Calendar flips 2021-2026, stubble, receding hairline. Cost: the posture is now permanent.
4. **Awareness + fake fix** (0:11-0:16). He sees the bad line in his own reflection and does one overdone straighten-up that collapses within a second. Cost: the excuse is now conscious.
5. **More years** (0:16-0:19). Grey beard, head nearly on the keyboard. Cost: the body has changed shape.
6. **Grandpa, the moment that matters** (0:19-0:27). The grandkids call and he can't turn to see them. Cost: love and connection, the highest stake. Pain icon callback at 0:26.6 (74%).
7. **Rule + second case** (0:27.6-0:36). Hard cut to a new kid and a new habit (candy, teeth) that compresses the whole arc into 8 s and ends on the pain hit.

Pattern: one habit, held constant, against a fast-moving clock. The image of each rung changes the body a little (hair, beard, hunch), so aging works as a progress bar. The stakes switch from physical to emotional at the top rung, then the moral generalises and proves itself on a second example.

## 3. Twist and payoff mechanics

- **The stakes jump genre at the peak.** For 19 s the cost is physical (an ache, a hunch). The payoff is social: grandkids calling and a grandparent who can't look at them. The pain line (0:26.6) comes after the emotional hit, so the pain reads as the reason for the loss, not just as pain.
- **First-second callback.** The red neck glow and lightning bolts at 0:00.5 return at 0:26.6 on the old body. The same icon on two ages of one face is the "before/after" without any split screen.
- **Phrase callbacks bridge the swap.** "tell yourself" (0:13.6, 0:28.8), "fix it" (0:15.2, 0:29.9) and "until" (0:18.7, 0:33.5) each appear twice. The second story reuses the first story's words, so the viewer feels the pattern before the narrator explains it.
- **Pattern transfer instead of a twist.** At 77% a new character with a different problem (sweets, decayed teeth) replays the whole arc in 8 s: grin, cavity visible, shrug, more candy, bite, pain. This turns a posture video into a video about procrastination and gives a second payoff in the last 3 s. (It may also tease another upload on teeth; not verified.)
- **Sting on the last word.** The final sentence is built so the last syllable ("is") lands with the macro bite into the cavity at 0:34.5. The video cuts 0.7 s later on the wince, with no breath for resolution.
- **The mirror motif.** He sees his posture in the dark monitor (0:11); the kid grins at himself in a bathroom mirror (0:27.7). In both cases "telling yourself" is shown as literally looking at yourself.
- **Loop:** weak. The last frame (a kid in green wincing) does not match the first (a young man in lilac on a bed). The replay hook is the open question "which habit is mine?", not a visual loop.

## 6. Visual grammar notes

- **Characters:** semi-realistic stylised CG (game-cinematic detail). One protagonist aged across four stages (teen, young adult, middle age, grandpa) using the **same wardrobe colour** (lilac/lavender top) and recurring plaid pyjama pants as the identity anchor, while hair, beard and glasses change. The grandkids are small, simple and cheerful. The second-act boy wears a **saturated green hoodie**, a complementary colour that flags "different person" instantly.
- **Narration person vs camera:** "you" narration, but shot in third person. Rule found across the corpus: use first-person POV when the story is about what "you" see and do in a trap (`p832whe8hrI.md`); use third person when the story is about what happens **to** "you" over time, because the face has to age on screen.
- **Palette:** every interior is a saturated cobalt/royal blue (bedroom, office, living room, bathroom) with lilac on the protagonist. Warm accents (lamp, recliner tan, orange slippers) only in home scenes. **Red-orange glow + red bolts** = pain (used exactly twice, as bookends of story one). Red on the reflection line = bad posture; yellow = the alternative line. Daylight green outside the office window = youth; grey-white = later.
- **Lighting:** soft key from the front, cool blue fill, warm practical lamps at home, screen glow on the face in the phone and monitor shots. The night living room is darker than the bright offices.
- **Materials:** plush hoodie fabric, leather recliner with specular highlights, glossy desk plastic, skin with pores and wrinkles that increase with age, glossy decayed teeth in macro. The brand is printed on the desk (0:08) as a prop, not as an overlay.
- **Camera language:** eye-level medium shots with slow push-ins carry most lines; close-ups for each body-part noun (neck, phone, head, teeth); one extreme macro at the sting. Transitions are whip-blurs between life stages and short cross-dissolves inside a montage. One black frame marks the act break.
- **Time devices:** a wall calendar flipping year by year, progressive aging of one face, changing light through a window, a hard jump to old age. No on-screen dates other than the calendar prop.
- **Cut rhythm:** a new image on most caption chunks (about 1-1.5 s) in story one; under 1 s per shot in the closing transfer. Montage shots hold longer but change content inside the shot.
- **Captions:** 1-4 words per chunk, white bold sans, lowercase except sentence starts, soft dark drop shadow, no box, no highlight colour, centred at about 78% of frame height. They change on spoken word groups (about every 0.5-1 s) and keep contractions ("isn't", "you'll", "you're").
- **Pain and sound get icons, nothing else does:** red bolts for pain, concentric rings for a voice. No arrows, labels or diagrams in this one.
