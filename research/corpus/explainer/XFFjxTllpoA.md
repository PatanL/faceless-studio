### Escalation ladder

1. Tingling lips (10 min): mild, felt.
2. Numb mouth, drooling: loss of control, embarrassing.
3. Nausea, body tries to purge: the body fights back.
4. Mechanism aside -> slow paralysis: the cause is named, so the dread grows.
5. Can't talk, dizzy, headache (30-40 min): communication lost (joke at the top).
6. "It gets worse" -> breathing fails: life-threatening.
7. Death, told as two jokes (praying, phone alarm).
8. Speed: all of it in a few hours.
9. Reality flip: people choose to eat it; one chef mistake kills.
10. Dose the size of a pinhead.
11. It still happens every year.

The ladder climbs by body system (mouth -> stomach -> muscles -> speech -> lungs -> consciousness) and by clock time. After the death peak it climbs again on a new axis: from "hypothetical" to "real, and still happening".

### How it ends

No CTA, no subscribe line, no "comment below". It ends on a 13-word metaphor stinger plus a present-tense recurring fact, over a comic fish image. The video stops at 0:58-0:59, straight after the last word, so the Short loops back to the hazard-sign title card. That makes replays likely and cuts swipe-away during an outro.

### Pacing and device notes for the PLAYBOOK

- Very dense: about 288 wpm, about 14 words per sentence, no pauses. On-screen captions carry the comprehension, so the voice can run faster than a long-form narrator.
- "You" or "your" appears in 12 of 18 lines. The whole body is in the conditional ("you would...").
- Numbers: 4 (30 people, 1,200x, 10 min, 30-40 min) plus 2 non-numeric scale images (a few hours, pinhead). All of them land in the hook or in the first 40% of the body, except the pinhead.
- Humour is the retention engine in the second half: every death-adjacent line is turned into a joke or euphemism ("one hell of a headache", praying, phone alarm, "curtains", newspaper, "guppy"). Death is never stated plainly.
- One authority beat only (the toxin name), kept to under 4 s.
- Production is minimal: 8 stock photos, 1 green-screen creator, bold captions. The creator's corner-jumps are the main motion. For our code-only kit, the equivalent is a static illustrated background per beat, a mascot or "YOU" figure that jumps sides, and a caption block per sentence.

## 3. Transferable skeleton (no original wording)

Template for a 45-60 s vertical "What if you [did a dangerous thing to/with ANIMAL]" Short. Percentages are of total runtime. At about 250-290 wpm with full captions, 55 s is about 240-270 words. At a calmer 180-200 wpm, plan 170-190 words or 60-65 s.

1. **Title restatement, 0-5% (12-16 words, about 3 s).** Say the title as a "here's what happens if you..." promise in the second person. Visual: a title card styled as a warning sign or hazard label, with the host/mascot already on screen.
2. **Stakes, 5-12% (15-20 words).** Two numbers back the superlative: one counts victims or units of damage, the other is a multiple of something the viewer already fears. Add a small-vs-huge contrast (small creature, big toll). Show the creature on screen; you can hold back its name.
3. **Timed ladder, 12-45% (4-5 rungs, 3-4 s each).** Start "first" with a clock stamp. Each rung is "then [a worse body effect]", moving from a mild felt sensation to loss of control to the body fighting back. Insert one 4-second mechanism aside (the real scientific name plus a plain-language verb), paid off in the very next line. Put a second clock stamp near the end of this block, plus a list of three that ends on a comic understatement.
4. **Ironic re-hook at about 45% (4-7 words).** A false-reassurance line saying it gets worse. Change the background on the next word.
5. **Climax, 45-70%.** The most vital body system fails. Say the death only through 2 jokes or euphemisms tied to everyday objects or habits, never as a flat statement. Close with a final clock stamp that makes the speed shocking (loop A closes).
6. **Reality twist, 70-85%.** A self-correcting tone break ("fun fact, no, scary fact") reveals that the hypothetical really happens on purpose (food, sport, tradition, job). State the safety rule that makes it survivable, then break it with one small human error.
7. **Scale payoff, 85-92%.** One "the deadly amount is this small" comparison pointed at an on-screen object ("that [tiny thing]").
8. **Stinger, 92-100% (10-15 words).** A gambling or game metaphor plus a present-tense "it still happens every [period]" fact. Over a comic image of the creature. Cut on the last word; no CTA, so the loop goes back to the title card.

Pacing constants: one sentence per caption block (about 3 s); a new rung or fact every 3-4 s; a background change every 6-8 s; a host/mascot position jump every 3-6 s; an explicit re-hook at about 45% and about 73%; a joke every 4-6 s after the midpoint and almost none before it; 3 clock stamps that escalate from minutes to hours; 3-4 numbers, mostly in the first 40%; "you" in two-thirds of lines.
