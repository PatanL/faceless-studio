## 3. Transferable skeleton (no original wording)

Template: **"Don't do this common habit, and the reasons are not what you think"** (warning-title list Short). Length 45-55 s, about 130-160 words, 7-9 picture changes.

1. **False-stakes hook (0-6 s, about 12%).** Open on a myth, superstition or exaggerated threat aimed at "you" about the habit in the title. Picture: someone doing the habit.
2. **Fake-out (1 s, 2-3 words).** Immediately take the myth back with a wink. Own caption block, a beat of silence.
3. **Real command (2-3 s).** "But really," restate the title's command plainly. The viewer now has a rule with no reason.
4. **Promise (1 s, 2-3 words).** An explicit "here's the reason" line. End the hook by about 20% of runtime.
5. **Thesis (3 s).** One line naming the umbrella reason in general terms (the object's role in a system).
6. **You-vs-them contrast, reason 1 (10-12 s).** First half: what the object means to the viewer, made small and a little funny. Second half on a "but": what it means to an animal or system, with a resizing metaphor (a trinket as building material, a crumb as a meal). Follow with one plain fact that backs the metaphor. Hard cut on the "but", and point at the animal on screen.
7. **Guilt / agreement question (4-5 s).** A rhetorical question that asks the viewer to side with the cute victim. This is the mid-video re-hook.
8. **Reason 2, quick (4-5 s).** "Also" plus a second, shorter nature reason with two named examples.
9. **Personal-risk pivot, reason 3 (4 s).** Turn the stakes onto the viewer with a playful idiom: it can get you into trouble. New tone, new graphic (sign, stamp, badge).
10. **Proof (4 s).** One named place where the rule is strict.
11. **Fair caveat close (6-8 s, last 15%).** A second named place where it is mostly allowed, with one condition and a closing imperative ("just check for X"). No CTA, no recap; stop on the warning so the loop to the myth line plays as a joke.

Rules to carry over:
- The spoken hook can run long (about 20% of runtime) if it holds three quick turns: myth, take-back, real command.
- Go from abstract nature stakes to a cute animal with a face, then to the viewer's own risk. Save the personal risk for last.
- One resizing metaphor per Short, followed at once by the literal fact.
- Write the narration to the picture: name what is on screen ("this", "that") so every sentence earns a cut.
- Keep the legal or factual claims checkable and hedged where they vary by place. This one overstates the UK rule; ours should name a specific, verifiable rule instead.
