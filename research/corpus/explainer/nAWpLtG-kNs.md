### Escalation ladder

Two ladders run at once, and the friction between them is the engine:

1. **Environment ladder (rising, mostly steady):** air goes from none (0%) to 5-10% to 15% to 15-20% to 30-35% (overshoot) to normal; then fire becomes possible (Silurian, 4:13); fish become catchable (Devonian); fruit arrives (Paleogene, 12:41); fruit and nuts (Pliocene). Each era names a "first time" milestone. The viewer feels progress toward "livable".
2. **Predator ladder (rising, then human):** harmless alien (Anomalocaris) → 6 m nautiloid (stay shallow) → 2 m sea scorpions → a sea scorpion in your fishing water → giant bugs that won't hurt you → gorgonopsids that you can't outrun → (extinction) → land crocodile → 30-33 ft theropods → Quetzalcoatlus (scarier than T. rex) → Titanoboa, terror birds → saber-tooths that hunt hominins → **other humans with tools**.

The **verdict sequence** that results is a sawtooth: Hours, Hours, Days, Days, Weeks, **Months**, Weeks, **Hours (crash)**, Weeks, Weeks, Months, Months, Most of a year, **A year or more (peak)**, Most of a year. There are three breaks in the climb: Permian (first drop, 50% runtime), Early Triassic (crash to the bottom with "less than hours", 54-62%), and Pleistocene (the most familiar era scores below the peak, 92-99%). Each break is announced by a "but" or a "finally" so the viewer feels the turn.

### How it ends

The peak is declared one era early ("the pinnacle... not going to get better", 14:45). The final era looks like the easy win (we survived it), gets a myth-bust (it wasn't all frozen), two monsters, then a twist that the predator is another human with tools (callback to the "no tools" rule). Then a fast verdict, a 6-word answer to the opening question, the worst era named as a counterweight, and a hard cut on the finished tier list. There is no CTA. The finished board is the shareable, comment-bait image ("Why is the Cretaceous only Months?").

### Pacing and device notes for the PLAYBOOK

- 165 wpm overall; short spoken lines (4-12 words) with many 1-3 word punches ("You're cooked." "Peak danger." "S tier.").
- "You" about 6 per minute, plus about 2 per minute host "I" opinions ("I'd say", "I'll be honest", "I don't even know"). The host voice gives the rulings a human judge, which makes viewers argue with them in comments.
- **Fixed per-segment checklist.** Every era is audited on the same 6 items in roughly the same order: AIR (O2 %) → TEMP → WATER → FIRE → FOOD → PREDATORS → VERDICT. After the viewer learns it (about era 5), the checklist is compressed to one line, which speeds up the second half.
- **Numbers:** each era has 1-3 numbers (O2 %, length in feet/meters, weight, calories, years, area), usually followed by an everyday comparison (Everest, crab, dog, pig, coyote, giraffe, axe, modern 21%).
- **Running catchphrase** ("you're toast" / "cooked") at the 3 worst verdicts (0:39, 2:34, 9:49) works as an audience in-joke.
- **Strategy talk** ("stay in shallow water", "stay in fresh water", "go north", "just avoid them") makes the viewer a player, and the video repeatedly punishes the strategy (freshwater Jaekelopterus, 5:49).
- **Gags about every 1.5-2 min**, often meta about the format ("my list doesn't go that low", "bottom seat here").
- Visual rhythm: about one cut every 5 s; real paleoart/CGI for creatures; white text callouts for numbers; silhouette scale charts with a human for every major predator; crude stick-figure "YOU" for comic beats; the tier list after each verdict (about 15 appearances, roughly 12% of screen time).

## 3. Transferable skeleton (no original wording)

Template for a "how long would YOU last in every [ZONE / ERA / PLACE / DEPTH]" ranked explainer, 8-16 min. It maps directly onto our ocean kit as "how long you'd last at every ocean depth / in every ocean / next to every sea creature". Percentages are of total runtime. At 165 wpm, 10 min is about 1,650 words; 12-15 items at 30-90 s each.

1. **Premise hook, 0-3.5% (80-100 words, fast, about 180 wpm).** One second-person "imagine" sentence that drops the viewer into a random item of the set (stuck, stranded, sinking). Ask the core ranking question. Fire 3-4 quick rhetorical teasers, each naming one recognizable item with one vivid image; hold the most famous one for last in the list. Raise the stakes ("is any of them survivable at all?"). State the challenge rules (just you, no gear) so verdicts are comparable, and plant one rule you will break at the end. Close with a 2-4 word "let's see". Visual: 5-6 fast creature/location clips, then the empty scoreboard.
2. **Scoreboard reveal, about 3.5%.** Show the empty ranking board (rows = survival time units from worst to best, colored red to green) before item 1. It stays the visual loop for the whole video.
3. **Item blocks, 3.5-99% (12-15 blocks, 30-90 s each; front-load longer blocks, compress later ones).** Each block repeats the same ritual:
   - **Title card** (item name + one identifying number, such as a date range or depth) and a one-line tease or reaction ("peak danger", "we'll need to run").
   - **Map / where-am-I** beat (map, depth gauge or locator), shrinking to one line once the format is known.
   - **Fixed checklist** in fixed order (for ocean: pressure, light, temperature, oxygen/air, food, predators). Turn each number into an everyday comparison. Pair good news with a "but".
   - **One milestone** per item ("first place on the list where X is possible") so the viewer feels progress.
   - **Predator rung:** name it, give 1-2 size/weight numbers, show a silhouette scale chart against a human, and either dismiss it with a viewer strategy (stay shallow, stay near the surface) or punish the strategy (the threat lives exactly where you planned to be).
   - **Verdict:** a survival-time ruling, said in the host's own voice with a reason, then the thumbnail moves into a scoreboard row. Use one recurring catchphrase for the worst rulings.
   - Spoken re-hook about every 20-30 s ("but", micro-question answered in 1-3 s, "here's the interesting part"); a visual gag or stinger about every 60-90 s.
4. **Verdict curve: make it a sawtooth, not a line.** Rising overall, with (a) a first drop at about 50% after the first big high, (b) a crash to the bottom (or "below the scale") at about 55-60% for the most catastrophic item, with a surprise-survivor fact inside it, (c) the top verdict at about 90%, said aloud as "it won't get better than this".
5. **Pay off the hook teasers in order of fame** at about 35%, 60%, 75% and 90%. Hold the most familiar or most expected item for the final block.
6. **Final-item twist, 92-99%.** The item everyone expects to win scores below the peak. Use a myth-vs-reality beat, then reveal the deadliest threat as one that breaks the opening rule (for example, the one thing in that zone that has the gear you don't). Fast verdict.
7. **Close, about 1% (10-15 words).** Answer the opening question in one line, name the worst item as a counterweight, and hold the finished scoreboard on screen for 5+ s. Optional: no CTA at all. The finished, debatable ranking does the comment-bait work. If a CTA is used, make it "which one would you pick?" over the final board.
