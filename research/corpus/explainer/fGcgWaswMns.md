### Escalation ladder

The threat rises one rung at a time and each rung is "worse" than the last:
size -> weapon (teeth) -> beats monsters (squid) -> goes where you can't (2 km, 1 hr) -> senses you in the dark (sonar, loudest animal) -> thinks and talks about you (largest brain, codas) -> has done it to humans on purpose (two ship sinkings) -> **you vs it: every escape option fails because of an earlier rung** -> verdict "nothing you could do" (7:01, 70%).

The options sequence reuses rungs 1, 3, 4 and 4 again as the reason each option fails. The facts the viewer has learned become the weapon against them, which is why the climax works.

After the peak, the threat is lowered 3 times: it doesn't care (twist) -> we were the danger (villain swap) -> it is good for the planet (payoff). Each step is still framed as a surprise ("here's the surprising part", "the real danger has always gone the other way", "in ways most people never hear about").

### How it ends

Bookend to the cold-open rule (same diver graphic), concession plus "but somewhere out there..." with three callbacks (depth, brain, sound), a humbling joke, then a 12 s CTA: comment-topic request + "I do actually read them" + end-screen pointer. There is no subscribe request and no mid-roll sponsor.

### Pacing and device notes for the PLAYBOOK

- 168 wpm, average sentence 12 words. The longest sentences (30-40 words) are the research/history facts. One- to three-word sentences are the punches and pivots.
- "You/your" appears 40 times (about 4 per minute). Second-person lines cluster in the hook (0:00-0:29), the "picture it again" midpoint (4:43) and the options climax (6:02-7:01).
- Number devices: every raw number is followed by an everyday comparison within 1 sentence (16 m -> city bus; 40 t -> 6-7 elephants; tooth -> your hand; 1 hr -> your 1 min in a pool; 8 kg -> bowling ball; a whale's interest in you -> your interest in a fly).
- Authority is always qualified ("officially", "measured", "researchers in 2024", "some scientists suggested... never proven", "one widely cited estimate", "one study estimated"). The narrator says once that a claim is unproven, which makes the rest more believable.
- Visual rhythm: a new caption or graphic state about every 2-4 s. Each beat gets one recurring motif (scale bar; counter; meter; sonar wireframe; document + stamp; numbered list with strike-throughs; icon grid draining). Red text is used only for the punch word of a beat (KNOWS WHERE YOU ARE, SQUID., YES., RAMMED, SUNK, COMPLAINT, MONSTER).

## 3. Transferable skeleton (no original wording)

Template for an 8-10 min "why you'd never want to meet [ANIMAL]" explainer. Percentages are of total runtime. At 168 wpm, 10 min is about 1,650 words.

1. **Cold open, 0-5% (60-75 words, about 150 wpm).** Put the viewer in a calm, specific place in the second person. List 2-3 common dangers and dismiss each one, ending on a one-line "safe rule". Break the rule with a 3-4 word turn line. Give one unexplained physical sensation or clue (loop A, close at about 35%). Name the creature last and say it already has the upper hand. Visual: one held "YOU" shot with small tick/cross captions, then the creature's first appearance on the reveal word.
2. **Reframe, 5-10%.** "Most people think the dangerous one is [famous rival]." Concede the rival is real, then pivot: our creature is in another class. Stack 3 superlative credentials, the last one a teaser of a real event (loop B, close at about 50%). Keep one credential for the final payoff.
3. **Promise, about 10%.** One sentence restating the title as what the video will deliver (loop C, close at about 70%).
4. **Escalation ladder, 10-70% (6-7 rungs of 30-65 s each).** Order: body/size -> weapon -> what it beats -> where it goes that you can't -> how it senses you -> how smart it is -> proof it has hurt people. Each rung has:
   - an entry: a rhetorical question answered in 1-3 words, a "here's the part that's worse" tease, or an "and it's not only that" escalator;
   - 1-2 hard numbers, each followed by an everyday or you-vs-it comparison;
   - one qualified authority line (dated study or "scientists measured"), with one honest "never proven" hedge somewhere in the ladder;
   - a 1-3 word punch line, plus a comic deflation about every 60-75 s;
   - one recurring graphic motif per rung (scale bar, counter, meter, sensor view, map, document stamp).
   Close loop A at the senses rung with a callback ("remember the sensation from the start?"). At about 47%, re-run the cold open with everything learned so far ("picture it again"). Close loop B as a short told story with date and place, add a second case, then a perspective-flip joke that plants sympathy for the creature.
5. **Interactive climax, 60-70%.** "What would you do?" Show a numbered list of 3-4 escape options. Kill each one with a callback to an earlier rung plus a metaphor or joke, striking it through on screen. Verdict line: nothing you could do (closes loop C).
6. **Twist, 70-78%.** "Easy to assume it's a monster, but here's the surprise": it barely notices humans (0% diet chart, a scale-of-interest analogy that makes the viewer the small thing). Add one strange-behaviour detour to keep curiosity up after the climax.
7. **Villain swap, 78-88%.** The real danger runs the other way: the history of human harm with 2-3 numbers (population before vs after, peak kill rate), then "it stopped, but the problems didn't", today's 3 threats, one gut-punch image, and a callback joke to lift the tone.
8. **Payoff, 88-95%.** "It holds the ecosystem together in a way few people know": a 3-step cause-effect chain ending in one big positive number. Restate the opening superlative credential as a paradox with its gentle role.
9. **Bookend, 95-98%.** Restate the opening "safe rule", concede it is mostly true, then end on an eerie present-tense image that uses 3 callbacks, followed by a light humbling joke.
10. **CTA, 98-100% (10-15 s).** Ask viewers to comment the next creature, say you read comments, and point to the end-screen video.

Pacing constants: about 165-170 wpm; a re-hook about every 30 s; a 1-3 word punch line about every 60-90 s; a joke about every 60-75 s; 3-4 long loops opened in the first minute and closed at roughly 35/50/70/98%; a graphic state change every 2-4 s; red text only for each beat's punch word.
