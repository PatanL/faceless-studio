## 3. Transferable skeleton (no original wording)

Template: **"Underdog clash list"** (third-person vignette compilation built on a broken saying). Length 4-8 min. For a voice-led version: about 600-1,200 words at about 160 wpm, one vignette every 25-35 s, 12-17 vignettes.

1. **Saying set up and broken (0-10 s, about 2%).** Quote a well-known saying about strength or danger, then say the video will show it is wrong in two ways (wrong animal, wrong weapon). No channel intro.
2. **Two proof vignettes (about 10%).** Open on a small intruder-vs-defender clip framed as a property dispute, then one even-matched clash among the hero species. After each, one "why it won" fact and two exact numbers (body weight, weapon weight), then one "why it isn't hurt" mechanism.
3. **Short pivot + title event (about 13-17%).** A 3-5 word line saying size isn't everything. Tell the title event in four steps: sympathetic bigger victim, the bigger animal attacks first, the small one strikes back, result in a 2-3 word sting. Pause on it.
4. **Story-ending vignette + soft science (about 17-30%).** A gang-up fight with a named place, ended by an emotional twist (the loser makes peace), backed by a behavior-science fact. Then one vignette in a new terrain with a terrain-adaptation fact.
5. **"But not every..." pivot + species switch (about 30%).** Switch to a related underdog species against a much larger animal. Use one comic beat where the giant refuses to fight. Add one behavior-season fact that a later vignette will use again.
6. **Stat cluster (about 40%).** Open a vignette on its number (impact force), turn it into an everyday comparison (sports tackle), then a short spoken aside to the viewer, then a second number (speed).
7. **Oddest-opponent vignette (about 43%).** The hero species against a machine. A second "why it isn't hurt" mechanism (a different body part).
8. **Category-flip pivot (about 55%).** A short line that flips an assumption the video has made so far (sex, age or breed). Then a slapstick vignette with a very specific place name.
9. **Heavyweight contrast block (about 60-75%).** Bring in the big animals the opening saying named, fighting each other, so the hero species' wins look bigger. Repeat the machine-opponent joke one level up and the gang-up outcome shape once more. End the block with a winner that lacks the weapon the saying named (a callback to the hook's second claim).
10. **Boxing-idiom pivot + finale (about 79-97%).** Underdog-vs-giant vignettes in a gentler, playful tone. A third species gets the same "built to absorb impact" fact. The last opponent is a human, the top of the opponent ladder.
11. **Close (last 3%).** A short sign-off. For us: one line that returns to the broken saying with a new version, instead of a bare CTA.

Rules to carry over:
- Every vignette opens with a scene line (who, where, what is at stake) and gets at most one or two fact lines. Never two vignettes in a row with no number or mechanism.
- Hold the title event back to about 15% of runtime: after the first-minute drop-off point, before the 20% mark.
- Pivot lines are short idioms (4-9 words) placed between vignettes about every 60 s, each switching one category (species, sex, opponent type, place).
- Repeat devices one level up (car, then a named car; why-not-hurt fact across three species; gang-up shape twice) instead of repeating wording.
- Name a place on most vignettes; specific places make generated clips feel documented.
- Check every stat. The source's force, speed and "concussion proof" lines are loose or overstated, and its death claim has no source. Ours must cite or soften.
- Front-load the shock, end on play.
