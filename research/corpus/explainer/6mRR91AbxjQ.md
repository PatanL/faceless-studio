Visual style seen on the sheet: photoreal 3D. A black hairy horsefly stands on a horizon-flat orange "skin floor" against a flat sky-blue gradient, so a tiny insect reads as a monster ("macro as landscape"). The mouthparts are rendered as glossy golden blades. The part being named glows yellow (stylets, saw teeth, the vessel, the hollow tube). The camera moves continuously through one world and morphs from the skin surface into a pink tissue cross-section instead of hard-cutting. Captions are small white bold sans, 2-4 words per card, at about 78% of frame height, changing every 0.5-1 s. 24 shots in 27.6 s, average shot about 1.15 s.

Fact note for our own scripts: a horsefly has six cutting/piercing mouthparts (2 mandibles, 2 maxillae, the hypopharynx that carries saliva, and the labrum). The script names five and skips the labrum; the "spongy tip" it ends on is the labellum, which is a separate pad, not one of the six. Its saliva does carry anticoagulants. So the "six tools" count is a hook number that the body only roughly pays off. Viewers do not notice because the enumeration rhythm ("two... two more... next... its tip") feels complete. Our scripts must still check every count they promise.

Shape: hook + stakes 20%, escalation ladder 70% (with the gore peak at 57-65%), reveal/payoff 10%. There is no twist, no CTA and no outro. The whole video is one mechanism told in order, with the "why" withheld until the last clause.

### Hook

- Hook line: 5 words, 1.6 s of speech (0:00.2-0:01.8). It is the title, word for word, in second person.
- Full hook including the inventory: 13 words in 5.4 s (about 144 wpm, the slowest part of the video).
- First "you" at word 5 (0:01.6). First number ("six") at 0:02.4. Subject named in the first 3 words and shown in the first frame.
- Visual hook: three scale changes in 2 s (macro fly, whole man with fly as a speck, fly again), then a dive under the fly into a fan of golden blades by 0:02. The viewer sees the weapon before hearing what it does.

### Re-hook interval

- There are no explicit verbal re-hooks ("but wait", "it gets worse"). The counted enumeration does the job: "two of them" (0:05.4), "two more" (0:09.5), "next" (0:18.1), "its spongy tip" (0:24.9). That is a new tool every 4-8 s (mean about 6.5 s).
- Under that, a new analogy or a worse body verb lands every 2-4 s: needle 0:03, slice/scissors 0:08-0:09, saw 0:11.6, tear 0:14, burst 0:16, bleed 0:17.5, spit 0:19.9, keeps flowing 0:23.8, laps 0:26.3.
- Visual changes: 24 shots, a new framing about every 1.15 s; a big world change (surface -> cross-section -> surface -> close object -> surface) every 5-8 s.
- Grammar is the hidden re-hook: seven open conjunctions ("so", "while", "to", "until", "which", "so", "as") mean almost every clause is grammatically unfinished, so the ear waits for the next one. Only two full stops occur after the hook (0:18.1 and 0:26.9).

### Escalation ladder

1. Presses (needle-like tools touch the skin): contact, a threat.
2. Slices open (scissors): the skin is cut.
3. Saws (teeth, saw): a harsher tool, and the motion is repeated.
4. Tears open the vessels: the damage goes deeper, under the skin.
5. Burst, bleed: the gore peak at 57-65%.
6. Pumps in spit: a new axis, disgust instead of pain.
7. The cut cannot seal, blood keeps flowing: the body's defence is switched off.
8. Laps it up: the reveal of purpose; the viewer is food.

The ladder climbs on two axes at once: depth (surface -> tissue -> vessel -> bloodstream) and kind of violation (pain -> gore -> contamination -> being eaten). Each step uses a tool analogy one notch more brutal or more gross than the last (needle, scissors, saw, pump, sponge).

### How it ends

On the reveal, not after it. The last clause is the payoff, the last word lands at 0:26.9, and a 0.7 s silent pull-back returns to the opening composition. No CTA, no "follow for more", no stinger joke. The ending is the loop: the viewer replays to catch what they missed, which counts as extra watch time.

### Pacing and device notes for the PLAYBOOK

- Very short and very dense: 79 words in 27.6 s, 177 wpm, only 3 sentences (avg 26 words), one of them 40 words long. At the Shorts length this is the "one breath" pattern: no pauses, no short punch lines, no jokes.
- "You/your" appears 5 times in 79 words (in 5 of 12 clauses), all at the body-horror moments: bites you, your skin, slice you open, your blood vessels, you start to bleed. After 0:18 the script drops "you" and talks about "the cut" and "the blood", which makes the end feel clinical.
- Numbers: one count ("six"), then the pair counts that tick it off ("two", "two more"). No measurement numbers, no superlatives, no authority claims.
- One everyday-tool analogy per mechanism, and at least one analogy is shown literally on screen (blades become steel saw blades for about 1 s).
- Visual system for our code-only kit (2D/2.5D, no photoreal 3D): a skin "floor" plane with perspective and a flat sky gradient (macro as landscape); one quick wide shot with a human silhouette for the reverse scale shock; a surface-to-cross-section morph (clip-path wipe) instead of a cut; a yellow glow highlight on the part being named, bound to the caption word; small 2-4 word captions that do not compete with the visual; last frame = first frame.

## 3. Transferable skeleton (no original wording)

Template for a 25-35 s vertical "How [CREATURE] [hurts/feeds on/attacks] you" mechanism Short. It fits any creature with a multi-part weapon or a step-by-step attack (stinger, beak, tentacle, toothed mouth, venom delivery). Percentages are of runtime. At about 175-185 wpm, 28 s is about 80 words. For our 60-90 s Short slot, either run two creatures back to back in this shape, or keep one creature and extend the inventory to 5-7 named parts with a twist and stinger after the reveal (as in the XFFjxTllpoA skeleton).

1. **Title as scenario, 0-9% (4-6 words, under 2 s).** Restate the title in the second person as "when X does the thing to you". Visual: the creature huge and centred on a flat-colour backdrop, standing on a stand-in for the viewer's body, then a 0.5 s cut to the full human with the creature tiny on it, then back.
2. **Counted inventory, 9-20% (7-9 words).** One specific count of weapon parts plus one everyday weapon image, ending on "your [body part]". Slower delivery so the number lands. Visual: the camera drops under or into the creature and the parts fan out toward the lens.
3. **Rung 1, 20-35% (12-14 words).** The first pair or part, what its edge or shape is, then a cause-effect clause with a common household tool analogy and a violent verb aimed at "you". Visual: the surface morphs into a cross-section; the parts act out the analogy.
4. **Rung 2, 35-57% (17-20 words).** "And more" escalator: the next pair or part with a harsher detail and a harsher tool analogy, shown literally for about 1 s (the part briefly becomes the tool). Then a motion verb that takes the damage one layer deeper. Keep it inside the same running sentence.
5. **Peak, 57-65% (7-9 words).** The worst physical result, ending on "you" doing something involuntary. The first full stop in over 10 s comes here. Visual: the deepest structure glows, then fails.
6. **Rung 3, new axis, 65-82% (12-14 words).** "Next" plus a part that does something gross or sneaky rather than painful (injects, coats, numbs, dissolves), then a one-clause mechanism that answers a question the viewer has had about this creature in real life. Visual: the part shown end-on against the sky, then a macro of the effect on the surface.
7. **Consequence, 82-90% (5-7 words).** What that mechanism lets keep happening. Visual: the result builds slowly on screen (a growing bead, a spreading stain).
8. **Purpose reveal + loop, 90-100% (6-8 words).** The last part or pad reveals what all of this was for (feeding, laying eggs, storing, carrying away), with the grossest image last. Stop the voice on the reveal word. Hold 0.5-1 s on a pull-back that matches the opening frame exactly. No CTA.

Pacing constants: one clause per caption card (2-4 words, a new card every 0.5-1 s); a new framing every 1-1.5 s; a new named part every 4-8 s; a new analogy or harsher verb every 2-4 s; join clauses with cause-effect conjunctions so the script runs as 3 sentences; a gore or intensity peak at about 60%; "you/your" on every pain beat in the first 65%, then a clinical third person for the end; one counted number in the hook, no other statistics; a yellow highlight on whatever part is being named; verify the promised count against real anatomy before writing.
