Fact note for our own scripts: several claims are pop-science simplifications. Jaekelopterus is from the Early Devonian, not the Silurian (other large eurypterids were Silurian). Kronosaurus is Early Cretaceous, not Jurassic. Recent work (2023) puts Dunkleosteus at about 3.4-4 m, well short of a school bus, and the "8,000 psi" bite figure is a loose popular conversion. Cameroceras lengths of 6 m+ are contested. Helicoprion's tooth whorl did not rotate on its own; the jaw motion did the slicing. Livyatan is about 13.5-17.5 m and overlapped with Megalodon in the Miocene, which is fair. "Widely considered by paleontologists the most dangerous ocean" has no source. The Cambrian "Everest air" line overstates how certain we are about Cambrian oxygen levels. The zero-fatal-wild-orca-attacks line is accurate. The video works because each era has one clear lethal mechanism; ours must keep that clarity and check every number.

### Pacing and device notes for the PLAYBOOK

- 179 wpm, average sentence 13.7 words. Long sentences (25-42 words) are kill sequences and mechanisms; the punches are 2-4 words.
- Every era has the same 5-part cell: entry (date/name + "this brings us to") -> creature build-up (name, gloss, everyday comparison) -> "the scariest part is..." escalator or VIS pointer -> what it does to you (2P) -> survival verdict + joke. Repetition of the cell makes 17 minutes easy to follow.

## 3. Transferable skeleton (no original wording)

Template for a 15-18 min "how long would you survive in every [era / place / condition]" explainer (eras of a planet, layers of the ocean, planets of the solar system, historical battles, climates). Percentages are of total runtime; at about 175 wpm, 17 min is about 3,000 words.

1. **Hook, 0-3.5% (90-110 words).** Two-word imperative, then the whole premise in one second-person sentence (you are put into [setting] at [time/place]). Name what the viewer expects ("you'd assume [the obvious danger]"), grant it ("and sometimes you'd be right"), then a list of 3 future monsters/dangers with one vivid detail each (each is paid off later in order). Pivot: the rules change by [era/zone]. End on two extremes: one stage where you are untouchable, one where you die almost at once (do not say which).
2. **Rung 1, 3.5-12% (about 90 s).** Start with the earliest/mildest stage. Build its top predator up as terrifying, then reverse it ("then you notice it's tiny"). Add one real-science reason why. Then reveal the true killer is the environment, which pays off the "die at once" tease. Give the first survival time and close on a dry joke.
3. **Upgrade + rung 2, 12-21%.** Give the viewer gear that removes the last killer ("since that's no fun, take [tool]"). The new danger is that you are now food. Explain where the predator waits and why your position gives you away, then a 3-step death the viewer can picture, plus an on-screen "it looks like this" pointer. Updated survival time. Finish by voicing the viewer's obvious escape plan as a question ("so you'd go to [safe place], right?").
4. **Rung 3, 21-27% (short, under 60 s).** The next stage answers that question: put the viewer in the safe place, say "you think you're safe" in a short line, then a reversal. "Think bigger. Even bigger." before the size number. Verdict: there is nowhere to hide.
5. **Rung 4, 27-40% (longest early rung).** A bigger predator. Size with an everyday comparison, then "but that isn't the scariest part" -> its special weapon, explained as a small "why it evolved that way" story, a tool comparison and a VIS pointer. Survival time drops. Then a fair-test upgrade: bring the modern safety tool. State the result first ("you still lose"), say "here's why", explain how the tool normally works, why this animal beats it, one estimated number, and a food joke.
6. **Rungs 5-6, 40-51% (short, about 60 s each).** Change the axis to keep the middle moving: one rung on strangeness (two odd designs, each with a tool comparison, verdict "a gamble"), one rung on logic (a mass-extinction reset, then a rule that removes your last safe zone; restate it 3 ways).
7. **Rung 7, 51-63% (long).** "The peak of [group]": size, VIS pointer, then "the most dangerous part was how it moved" with a short mechanism lesson. Survival time in seconds, told as a first-person moment. Then a doubt loop: "would it even bother with you?" -> a long self-deprecating comedy run -> "Unfortunately, no." -> why your own behaviour gives you away. Dark punchline.
8. **Rung 8 = peak, 63-73%.** "If you somehow made it, congratulations": the stage called the worst ever. Pay off the biggest hook monster, add a second predator with one strange true fossil/evidence fact, then "this is as bad as it gets" with an "if A doesn't get you, B will" line. Last fair-test upgrade, result first ("spoiler"), mechanism, comic kill image.
9. **Rung 9, 73-83%.** Things get too big to care about you: you are insignificant, death by accident or as collateral damage from giants fighting. Pop-culture comparison (you're an extra in someone else's movie). This starts lowering the threat.
10. **Twist, 83-91%.** "Finally, today." Name the viewer's real modern fears in "we" voice (movies, deep water), then the reversal: now is the safest it has been. Prove it with one hard zero and one honest caveat played as a joke. Then a 3-line "you wouldn't be..." recap using the earlier comic images, and the final survival verdict (the slowest, least dramatic death in the video).
11. **Reflection and thesis, 91-97%.** "So why does it still feel scary?" Contrast what humans are built for with what the setting rewards; 3 animal abilities (one a callback) vs 3 short "we can't" lines; end on a short thesis line and a callback to the opening time span.
12. **CTA, 97-100% (25-35 s).** Personal joke, ask viewers to comment the next topic, say you read them, plug a related video on the end screen.

Pacing constants: about 175-180 wpm; a new stage every 60-140 s (mean about 90 s); a re-hook line every 30 s; one survival verdict per stage; a joke about every 60 s; an on-screen "looks like this" pointer every 2-3 min; a "would [tool] save you?" test about every 3 min in the first two-thirds; 1-4 word punch lines after long sentences; second person throughout the ladder, "we" for the twist and ending.
