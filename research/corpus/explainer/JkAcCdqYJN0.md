### Hook

- Line 1 is the whole hook: **14 words, about 5 s**, second person, restating the title as an action happening to the viewer ("you're getting swallowed") with a scope claim ("every animal") and a qualifier that seeds the twist ("can actually").
- The cold open is 48 words / 20 s: hook (5 s), host intro with a joke (9 s), and the scoring rule (stopwatch) (5 s). The first numbered entry starts at 0:20.
- No cliffhanger in the open; the retention engine is the format promise (8 entries counting down, a score per entry, the title question "which one actually can?").

### Re-hook interval

- **Macro:** a new "Number N" every 52-78 s (mean 62 s). Each entry ends on a "time inside" stamp, which is the beat that tells viewers "next one coming".
- **Micro:** inside each entry there is a turn every 10-20 s, usually marked by a signpost phrase: "But, don't get cocky" (0:51), "Then it hits a small problem" (1:34), "Oh, and" (1:59), "And this one isn't a what if" (2:08), "and get this" (2:32), "So, can you?" (3:19), "Fun fact" (3:53), "Here's the weird part" (4:44), "But here's the twist" (5:57), "But wait" (7:06). Average gap between explicit re-hook phrases is about 40-45 s, with jokes/punch lines filling every 8-12 s between them.
- **Seam hook:** most entries end on a bonus weird fact or button joke right before the next number (eyeball teeth, plane crash survivor, croc tears, echidna vomit, cannibal mating), so there is no dead air between entries.

### Escalation ladder

The ladder climbs on two axes at once:

1. **How far you get in (and whether it is real):** #8 head only -> #7 in the mouth 2 s, coughed out -> #6 *real cases*, 30-40 s in the mouth -> #5 kills you but in pieces -> #4 tries, may vomit you back -> #3 full swallow is physically possible, but zero confirmed cases -> #2 the only whale that could, legend debunked, "could but never has" -> #1 *really has*, six documented cases.
   The "throat is the bottleneck" motif repeats (#7 coin, #6 fist, #1 shoulders) so the viewer learns the test and can feel #2 and #1 pass it.
2. **Tone:** slapstick (pelican) -> gross-out (croc acid) -> narrator fear (headband, refuses to time) -> darkness (sperm whale stomach, pitch black, dive) -> serious (stopwatch away, muted palette, "real people"). The comedy is withdrawn exactly when the answer arrives, which makes the climax feel weighty.

Inside each entry the mini-ladder is: superlative stat -> 2P scenario -> obstacle/mechanism -> real dated anecdote -> weird bonus fact -> verdict stamp.

### How it ends

Climax answers the title at 8:26 (92%), then a 21 s rapid recap that reuses each entry's verdict line as a one-phrase label (the verdicts were written to be recapped), then a kicker that opens one last tiny loop ("one animal here will never eat you") and closes it on the mascot, a callback to the mascot's costume, and a CTA framed as a joke threat that bookends the first entry. No "thanks for watching", no sponsor, no next-video plug in the transcript.

### Pacing and device notes for the PLAYBOOK

- 145 wpm, slower than the serious Depth Theory style (168): comedy narration leaves room after punch lines. Short lines (1-3 words) appear about every 30 s.
- Second person is used in every scenario, but the narrator is a character ("I"), which gives a second voice and allows asides ("Even I can only hold my breath...").
- Every number gets an everyday anchor: coin, fist, bench-pressing a pickup, 5-minute breath-hold.
- Dated real events (2006, 1981, 2021, 2025, 2007, 2022, 1891, Jan 2026, since 2017) appear in every entry. They act as authority and as recency hooks.
- Myth-vs-fact flips: anaconda (crush/suffocate -> blood flow), Bartley legend, "jaw unhinges". One per long-form third.
- Visual grammar: white bg + mascot for facts, full-colour scene for 2P scenarios, interior (pink/magenta) shots for "inside", kicker word per stat, coloured section card per number, palette desaturates for the serious climax.

## 3. Transferable skeleton (no original wording)

Format: a numbered countdown of N threats (N = 6-8 for 8-10 min; 3-4 for a Short) that all answer one yes/no test stated in the title, with a twist in the title that only the last entry passes. Narrated by a character-host who keeps a running score.

1. **Cold open (0-4%, ~45-50 words).**
   - Line 1 (≤15 words, ≤5 s): restate the title as something happening to "you" right now, with a scope claim (every/all) and a qualifier hinting that most will fail the test.
   - Host intro (≤10 s): a character-narrator with a personal stake in the topic and one joke.
   - Scoring rule (≤5 s): the host announces the single metric they'll stamp on every entry (time, distance, survival odds, damage).
2. **Entry block, repeated N times (each 9-14% of runtime; entries get slightly longer as the stakes rise; the final entry is shorter and denser).**
   1. Number + name + one superlative stat, with a kicker word on screen.
   2. Second stat with an everyday-object comparison.
   3. "You" scenario in present tense: 3-5 short actions, each one an animation beat, in a full-colour scene.
   4. Obstacle or mechanism reveal that answers the title test for this entry (repeat the same obstacle type across entries so viewers learn the test).
   5. A signposted turn ("but", "here's the strange part", "wait") into a real, dated anecdote or study.
   6. A bonus weird fact placed at the end so the seam into the next entry is a hook, not a pause.
   7. Verdict stamp: the score in one short line, written so it can be reused word-for-word in the recap. Use joke verdicts ("depends on X") for middle entries.
3. **Ladder rules.**
   - Order entries by how close they come to passing the test: can't -> briefly -> real but partial -> fully possible but never recorded -> passes.
   - Put one "it actually happened" entry early (around entry 3) to raise stakes from hypothetical to real.
   - Put the host in danger in one late entry (they appear on the menu), then have the host break their own scoring routine (refuse, walk away). Breaking a learned pattern is a re-hook.
   - Put the strongest "maybe this is the one" fake-out on the second-to-last entry (a legend), then debunk it inside 15 s and state the could/has distinction that sets up #1.
   - The final entry drops the running gag on purpose, switches to a sober tone and a muted palette, explains the mechanism in one myth-bust, and lands the documented answer at ~90-94% runtime, echoing the title's wording.
4. **Recap (3-4%).** Rapid-fire list of every entry name + its verdict line.
5. **Kicker + CTA (2-3%).** Open and close one last tiny loop about the host (a "safe" twist), add a callback to a visual detail planted at the start, and make the subscribe ask a joke that bookends the first entry.

Pacing targets: 140-150 wpm for a comic host (160-170 if played straight); macro re-hook (new entry) every 50-80 s; a signposted turn every 30-45 s; a punch line or short line every 8-12 s; at least one dated real-world fact per entry; one myth-vs-fact flip per third of the runtime.
