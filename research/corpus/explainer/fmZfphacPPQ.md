Fact note for our own scripts: several claims are pop-science exaggerations. Breath-hold divers get a "blood shift" (blood pools in chest vessels to protect the lungs), which is not lungs "filling with blood". Blood gases becoming toxic (narcosis, oxygen toxicity) applies to someone **breathing compressed gas**, which contradicts the swim-down framing. A human body is mostly water and would not "implode" at 3,000 m; air spaces would have crushed long before. The format works because it sounds exact, but our scripts must check every number and mechanism.

Rough macro split: hook 9%, escalation body 73%, payoff 18%. No CTA, no title card, no outro.

### Hook

- Length: 9 words, about 3 s (0:00-0:03). Including the first symptom, the opening rung is 21 words in about 8 s.
- Form: second-person conditional ("if you" + action) + the first, deliberately small number with a minimiser. It does not restate the title; it starts the experiment immediately.
- The question in the title ("Could you survive...?") is the open loop; the script never asks a question aloud.

### Re-hook interval

- Each new depth number is a re-hook: 0:00 (10 m), 0:10 (100 m), 0:17 (1,000 m), 0:27 (3,000 m). Intervals 10 s, 7 s, 10 s: **a new number about every 9 s**.
- Between numbers, a new symptom lands every 3-6 s (0:04, 0:11, 0:21, 0:26, 0:31).
- Visual cuts every 1-3 s; the two x-ray cutaways (0:12, 0:21) are pattern interrupts placed right after a number re-hook.
- Every rung transition is bridged by a conjunction ("and by the time", "and if you", "and any part", "at around"), so the script never offers a sentence end until the final word.

The ladder climbs on two axes at once: the number grows (x10, x10, x3) and the threat moves from **outside** (pressure on the ears) to **inside** (lungs, then blood chemistry) to **whole body** (implosion). The third rung is the longest (about 12 s) and carries the stakes comparison plus two symptoms; it is the "middle sag" killer.

### How it ends

On the payoff itself: a 4-word final clause ending on a finality word ("instantly"). No moral, no "subscribe", no question to the audience. The final image (body on the seabed) cuts back to the calm surface opening when the Short loops, so the end works as a rewatch trigger. The channel watermark appears once, 3 s before the end, not as an outro card.

## 3. Transferable skeleton (no original wording)

Use for any "how far / how much / how long could you survive X" Short (depth, altitude, temperature, speed, G-force, radiation, time without sleep). Target 30-35 s, about 90-100 words at 165-180 wpm, one continuous sentence.

1. **Premise + small first number (0-3 s, 8-10 words).** Second-person conditional: "if you [did the action] to only [small value]". The minimiser tells the viewer that trouble starts early. Do not restate the title; start the experiment.
2. **Rung 1 symptom (3-8 s).** A mild sensation nearly everyone has felt in a smaller form. Name the villain force once. Picture: the character reacting (hands to head, wince).
3. **Bridge + rung 2 (8-14 s).** "and by the time you got to" -> x10 value. Show a famous landmark of about that size next to the character (do not say it). Symptom: an internal organ change, plus a one-clause "to stop it from [failure word]" reason. Plant that failure word. Picture: x-ray/cutaway of the organ.
4. **Bridge + rung 3 (14-20 s).** "and if you [hedge word: somehow / against all odds] kept going past" -> another x10 value. Add a stakes line: "where even [a machine built for this] starts to fail". Picture: the character beside that machine for scale.
5. **Rung 3 symptoms (20-26 s).** First the threat moves inside (your own body chemistry turns against you), then the planted failure word comes back as something now happening to part of the body. Picture: second cutaway, then a visual break-up of the character.
6. **Bridge + final number (26-28 s).** "at about" -> the last value (a smaller jump, x2-x3, so it lands as the end of the line). Picture: the tallest landmark of the video, seen top to bottom.
7. **Payoff stinger (28-33 s, 4-8 words).** The failure word at full scale, ending on a finality word ("instantly", "in seconds"). Cut on the last word. No CTA, no outro; make the last frame contrast with the first so the loop invites a rewatch.

Rules of thumb from this video:
- One spoken number per rung, about every 9 s; a symptom every 3-6 s; a picture change every 1-3 s.
- Join rungs with "and..." bridges so there is no sentence end before the payoff.
- Pair every number with an on-screen landmark of matching size; say at most one comparison out loud (the machine), show the rest.
- Move the threat outside -> inside -> whole body while the numbers grow.
- Plant a failure word early as something avoided, then pay it off twice, bigger each time.
- Check the science: this reference overstates its mechanisms, and ours must not.
