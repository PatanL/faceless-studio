### Escalation ladder

1. **Depth:** surface, 200 m (twilight), 1 km to 4 km (midnight), 4 km bottom (abyss), 6-11 km (hadal). Each rung is announced with a number, a zone name and the cross-section diagram (on screen at about 100 s and 366 s).
2. **Hostility:** less light, then no light / near freezing / "hundreds of times" the pressure, then 4 °C mud desert with scarce food, then "a thousand times" the pressure, "instantly fatal".
3. **Strangeness of adaptation:** transparency, glow-to-hide, a red light only the fish can see, ultra-black skin, parasitic male fusion (gross-out peak at 07:49, 44%), gigantism, a 400-year shark, life on chemicals with no sunlight.
4. **Scope:** single animals, then a mass migration, then whole ecosystems on a carcass, then the planet (plains bigger than all land biomes; trench deeper than Everest), then other worlds (Europa/Enceladus).
5. **Human link:** WWII sonar, plastic in trench amphipods, fewer people in the trench than on the moon.

### Per-chapter internal template (repeats 4 times)

entry (depth number, zone name, diagram) → hostility negation list ("no X, no Y, only Z") → "And yet" reversal → 3-5 creatures, each as name + one extreme fact + either a mechanism reveal or a mystery with 2 hypotheses → one non-creature aside (history, whale-fall succession, pollution, space) → recap anaphora triplet ("where..., where..., where...") → bridge line that promises more strangeness below.

### Visuals per beat (from the contact sheet)

- Cold open: green ROV evidence clip, black hold, creepiest-creature montage. Stakes: Mars rover and galaxy cutaways.
- Baseline: bright sunlit reef, the only saturated warm palette in the video. From there the palette darkens chapter by chapter: blue, black, mud green, vent orange.
- Chapter entries: the flat 2D cross-section diagram with the new band lit (the one bespoke graphic), then a submersible floodlight descent shot.
- Examples: long holds (8-20 s) on one hero creature against black. Explanations use a simple two-panel comparison (counter-illumination) or archival props (sonar echogram, a "Male" label on the anglerfish).
- Big-scale lines: a 3D terrain render and Google Earth for the trench, planet renders for Europa/Enceladus.
- Finale: surface horizon reset, then a fast best-of montage of earlier hero creatures under the rhetorical questions. Fade to black.

### How it ends

No CTA and no subscribe ask. The finale (976-1060 s, 7.8%) restates that the ocean is mostly unexplored and asks 3 unanswered "why" questions as a countdown (gigantism, invisibility, then bioluminescence as "the greatest mystery of all"), over a recap montage. It closes on a 3-part emotional button (secrets, worth protecting, worth discovering) and fades out. The open ending pays off the thumbnail's "we can't explain" claim, so leaving the questions unresolved is the payoff itself.

### Notes for our scripts

- The stats contradict each other: "<5% of the deep sea explored" at 00:50 and ">80% of the ocean unexplored" at 16:21. Keep our numbers consistent and sourced.
- The voice is "we" (explorers together) rather than "you". The Depth Theory style we copy uses "you vs. X". The descent-ladder structure works with either: for a "you" version, the stick-figure YOU descends and each rung adds a "you would..." survival line.
- Its slow 121 wpm with music breaths works at 18 min of real footage. For 8-10 min code-only videos with illustrated creatures, keep the structure but tighten the breaths. The visual novelty per second is lower, so aim for about 140-150 wpm and fewer music-only holds.
