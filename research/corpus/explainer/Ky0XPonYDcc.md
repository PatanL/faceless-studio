## 2. Structure summary

Beat sequence with share of the 40 s runtime:

| Beat | Time | Seconds | % of runtime |
|---|---|---|---|
| HOOK (title + comment sticker + "explain the story" line) | 0:00-0:01.5 | 1.5 | 4% |
| SETUP (world context, animal character, behaviour rule) | 0:01.5-0:13 | 11.5 | 29% |
| TURN 1 + STAKES (rule fixed, why it matters) | 0:13-0:19 | 6 | 15% |
| TURN 2 / PROBLEM (rule broken again) | 0:19-0:26 | 7 | 18% |
| REHOOK (micro-cliffhanger) | 0:26-0:28 | 2 | 5% |
| REVEAL + PAYOFF (absurd solution, all threads tied) | 0:28-0:33 | 5 | 12% |
| VERDICT (step-out frame + ranked laugh line) | 0:33-0:40 | 7 | 17% |

- **Hook length:** 8 spoken words, about 1.5 s. But the real hook is visual and textual. The title ("You could've FaceTimed an Eel") and the comment sticker ("THEY FACE TIMED THE EELS") state the absurd outcome before a word is spoken. The spoken line only promises an explanation. This is an outcome-first hook: say the strange result, then tell how it happened.
- **Re-hook interval:** about every 6-7 s, always on a connective at the start of a sentence: "but eventually" (0:13), "which was good because" (0:17), "but of course" (0:19), "which is how" (0:26), "but" (0:36). The script reads as one run-on chain of "but"/"which" links, with no full stop where a viewer could leave. Background image changes are at 0:07, 0:13, 0:28 and 0:35 (about every 7-15 s). They are slower than the verbal re-hooks, and the cuts fall on beat changes.
- **Open loops:** one main loop. L1 "Why would anyone FaceTime an eel?" opens at 0:00 (title + sticker + "explain the story") and closes at 0:28 (the zoo asked people to FaceTime the eels), about 70% through. There is a small nested loop at 0:26-0:28 ("they got an idea" leads to the idea). A soft loop also opens at 0:13: the shyness is fixed and the viewer expects a catch. It closes at 0:19 with "but of course".
- **Escalation ladder:** (1) a general, relatable fact: animals missed visitors during lockdown. (2) A specific odd character: eels with social anxiety. (3) A behaviour rule: they hide when people come near. (4) The rule is fixed by daily exposure, a false resolution. (5) The rule breaks again because of the lockdown, a setback with a cost to science. (6) An absurd institutional solution: an official zoo asks the public to video-call fish. (7) A meta-ranking against every other weird thing that year. Each rung is a little more absurd than the last while staying factual. The comedy comes from treating the eels' emotions as serious.
- **How it ends:** a two-line verdict that steps out of the story ("that year was weird") and then ranks this story at the top, restating the premise in new words. No CTA, no subscribe ask. The last line repeats the title idea (FaceTime + eels), so a loop back to frame 0 with the "THEY FACE TIMED THE EELS" sticker feels seamless. That favours rewatches on a 40 s Short.
- **Pacing note for us:** 288 wpm is far above our long-form explainer pace (Depth Theory about 168 wpm). This is a talking-head comedic Short read at full speed. For a Kokoro/Piper voice, aim for about 220-250 wpm in a 35-45 s Short and keep the same chain-connective rhythm; TTS sounds rushed above about 260.
- **Visual lesson:** the video gets 8.6M views with 5 still images and one presenter. Retention comes from the script chain and from cutting the background picture on beat changes (hidden tank for "bury themselves", tablets image for the reveal). In code, one stage per beat with a hard cut on each "but" is enough.

## 3. Transferable skeleton (no original wording)

Template: **"Absurd-but-true animal headline, told backwards"**. Length 35-45 s, about 120-180 words, 5-6 background states.

1. **Outcome-first hook (0-2 s, about 4%).** Title/on-screen text states the strange true outcome as a fact ("[Institution] did [absurd human thing] for [animal]"). The first spoken line only promises the explanation ("let me explain how this happened"). It is optionally framed as a reply to a viewer comment. Visual: the animal in its normal state plus the text card.
2. **Shared-context anchor (2-5 s).** Place it in a moment or event the audience already knows, in one clause, using a word like "obviously" so it needs no setup.
3. **Animal as character (5-10 s).** Give the animal a human emotional label (a disorder, a personality trait, a feeling), with the exact species and real location as credibility.
4. **Behaviour rule (10-13 s).** One sentence: when X happens, the animal does Y. Picture the rule.
5. **False resolution (13-17 s, "but eventually").** Something fixes the rule, and the animal "gets better".
6. **Practical stakes (17-19 s, "which was good because").** Why the fix matters to humans (research, care, money, safety).
7. **Reversal (19-26 s, "but of course").** The context from step 2 undoes the fix. Call back the rule from step 4 word-for-idea. Cut the background on the "but".
8. **Micro-cliffhanger (about 1 s, a 4-7 word line).** "So they came up with a plan" type beat, alone on screen.
9. **Reveal (about 70% mark).** The absurd real solution, stated deadpan with official language. Cut to the most surprising visual of the video.
10. **Payoff tie-up (one sentence).** Restate the solution's purpose with callbacks to the emotion (step 3, slightly raised) and the stakes (step 6).
11. **Step-out verdict (last 15-20%).** Line A: zoom out to the era or category ("that year/this field has had strange stories"). Line B: rank this one at the top while restating the premise in new words that echo the title. No CTA, end on the title idea so the loop to frame 0 is seamless.

Rules to carry over:
- Chain every sentence with a connective ("but", "which", "so", "because"), and allow no sentence-final pause before the reveal.
- One main open loop, closed at about 70%. One false resolution before the real reversal.
- Raise the human-emotion label once (mild, then strong, then a synonym) for a running joke.
- Background image changes only on beat changes, about 4-5 per 40 s. Captions show whole sentences, top-centre.
- Only use true, checkable stories. The "absurd but real" tension is the hook and depends on it being factual.
