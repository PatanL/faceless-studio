Fact note for our own scripts: the hook compares the viewer's **height** with the bat's **wingspan**; the golden-crowned flying fox (Acerodon jubatus, Philippines only, IUCN Endangered) has a wingspan up to about 1.5-1.7 m but a body under about 30 cm and about 1.2 kg. Megabats (Pteropodidae) live across the Old World tropics (Africa, South and Southeast Asia, Australia, Pacific islands), not "everywhere": none in the Americas or Europe. "100% harmless" is wrong for handling: flying foxes carry Hendra, Nipah and Australian bat lyssavirus. Wing-fanning and licking/saliva-spreading to cool down are documented. The pollination/seed-dispersal value is real; "millions of dollars" is unsourced. The title's "Dracula" bait is never true for megabats (the three blood-feeding bats are small American microbats). Tone: contains a bleeped swear and drug jokes ("OD'd", "roid"); not our brand register.

### Hook

- Length: narrated hook is 13 words in about 3.3 s (0:04.2-0:07.5); with the proof sentence, the opening block is 34 words in about 7.5 s. - Form: second-person size check ("if you are under X tall, this animal is bigger than you") + named species + one number + photo proof. It is the strongest part of the script and the only stretch where the viewer is in the frame.

### Control lessons (vs corpus #7-9, same channel)

1. **The viewer leaves the frame after 6 s.** "You" is 1.6% of words here vs 7.8% (PUFFER) and 5.3% (JELLY); GOBLIN (0.78x) was 1.9%. The size-vs-you hook works, but the body never comes back to your body.
2. **The master question is answered at 38%.** Outliers close their spine in the last 10% (PUFFER's timeline, JELLY's "that's not the worst part"); this one defuses the threat early and spends half its runtime on a charm list.
3. **A borrowed 4 s cold open.** The stitch is a strong visual hook, but narration starts at 7% of runtime; all outlier Shorts speak about the subject within 1 s.
4. **What it does well (and still scored 1.38x):** the size-vs-you hook with photo proof, a joke every 3-4 s, the fake-reassurance list, the "if that doesn't win you over" persuasion ladder, and a two-sided reframe label as the ending. These are worth borrowing inside a danger-led frame, not as the frame.

## 3. Transferable skeleton (no original wording)

Use for a "scary-looking but gentle animal" profile Short (manta ray, basking shark, whale shark, giant isopod, oarfish, wolf eel, sunfish), 55-60 s, about 250 words at about 280 wpm. The control shows where this format leaks, so the skeleton keeps the good devices and moves the defuse later.

1. **Size check on the viewer (0-4 s, 10-14 words).** Start narrating at 0 s (no borrowed or silent open). Second-person condition on a body measurement everyone knows about themselves, then the claim that the animal beats it. Show a real-scale photo or diagram as the line lands.
2. **Name + one number + proof (4-10 s).** Name the species, give one size figure, and pre-empt "that's fake" while the scale picture (a person beside the animal) is on screen. Use the honest measurement (say "wingspan"/"length", not height).
3. **Name-origin gag (10-14 s).** One comic simile for why it has that name, matched to a face close-up.
4. **Fake reassurance list (14-22 s).** "Relax, it only lives in [one place]" then add places one per beat until it is "most of the planet"; show a range map on the punchline. Keep the list true to the real range.
5. **Keep the threat alive (22-38 s).** Unlike the control, do not defuse yet. Two or three "but what if you met one" beats that test the scary reputation against the viewer's body (it swims at you, it opens its mouth, it is next to your boat), each answered with a half-reassuring, half-alarming fact. Re-hook every ~5 s.
6. **Turn (about 38-45 s, ~65-75%).** A register switch ("okay, honestly") and the real answer: harmless to you, with the one honest caveat (do not touch / disease / sting), and the gentlest diet or habit as the bathos punchline.
7. **Persuasion ladder (45-53 s).** "If that hasn't won you over..." -> 2-3 escalating reasons (cute young with a picture reveal, a relatable human-like behaviour, why it matters to people), each wrapped in a joke; call the ladder phrase back once.
8. **Reframe verdict (53-58 s, 10-16 words).** A meta "so the takeaway" line, then a compound nickname pairing the monster label with a cute label, ending on the cute word. No CTA; make the last image contrast with the first so the loop invites a rewatch.

Rules of thumb from this control:
- Speak within the first second; a borrowed or silent opening cost this Short 7% of its runtime.
- Keep "you" in the body (target 5%+ of words, half the sentences), not only in the hook.
- Answer the "is it dangerous?" question at 65-75%, not 38%; the list after the answer has no stakes.
- A joke every 3-4 s and a re-hook every ~5 s are the channel norm; they hold attention but cannot replace a rising threat.
- End on a two-sided label that pays off the title's bait word.
- Check every fact: this reference swaps height for wingspan, overstates the range and calls the animal 100% harmless; ours must not.
