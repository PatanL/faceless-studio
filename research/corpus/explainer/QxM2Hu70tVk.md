### Hook

- Length: 22 words, 0:01–0:08 (about 7 s of speech). The first list item is named at 0:08, and real content starts at 0:15 after the scream gag.
- Pattern: address the viewer as the victim, restate the title as a question, deflate it with a joke, start the countdown. There is no channel intro or "in this video". Visually, the frame from 0:06 to 0:08 shows every threat in the video at once, so the thumbnail-style menu is shown up front.

The design has one long countdown loop over the whole video, with many short loops of 2–30 s nested inside it. Most short loops close within the same item, so the viewer never carries more than about 2 open questions at once.

### Escalation ladder

1. **Order is by fame, not lethality.** The least famous trap (pitcher) comes first and the most famous (Venus flytrap) last, announced with "I bet you've all been waiting for this one".
2. **Mechanism complexity rises.** One trick (slippery wax), then a maze, then a vacuum trapdoor (milliseconds), then glue that tightens when you struggle, then a one-way tunnel, then a four-part system with a counter rule.
3. **Prey scale rises inside items.** Bugs, then frogs and rats, then tree-shrew mammals (#5). Larvae, then tadpoles (#4).
4. **Death time rises.** "In a matter of minutes" (sundew), "a slow, painful death" (corkscrew), "over the course of a few days" (flytrap, last).
5. **The gore end-image shifts.** "Nutrient soup" (2:53, 4:10, 5:32 becomes "nutrient poop"), then "skeleton" (4:57, 8:37). The video ends on the skeleton, its clearest image.
6. **Twists keep the ladder fresh.** Each item has one "unlike the others" twist: no enzymes, it's larvae (cobra lily); a bug does the eating (mirid); it doesn't need lures (corkscrew); it doesn't eat many flies (flytrap).

## 3. Transferable skeleton (abstract, no original wording)

Use this for a "What it's like to be [killed/processed] by [category of N threats]" long-form video of 8–9 min at about 155–160 wpm (about 1,350–1,450 words).

1. **Cold open (0–8 s, ≤25 words).** Address the viewer as the victim (shrink, transform or drop them into the scenario). Restate the title as a question, add a one-line joke, and name item #N right away. Visual: one tableau showing every threat in the video. No channel intro.
2. **Item #N (largest block, about 30% of runtime). This sells the format.**
   a. Countdown card, then the category title card, then a 1-second voice gag that previews the death.
   b. Name/etymology fact that ends on a threat word.
   c. "Picture this" switch into second-person POV: lure (sense: smell, sight, curiosity), then false comfort, then "what you don't realize" irony, then a point of no return, then closing exits one at a time, then the death payoff with a body detail.
   d. The victim asks "why?", answered with one science fact phrased as "from you".
   e. Prey scale step-up (small, then surprisingly large).
   f. Weird-fact tangent with a guess-the-image question (2-second loop) and a gross-out or absurd payoff.
   g. A sub-variant with a second, different POV death (maze, confusion), ending on a sentence cut off mid-line that the visual completes.
3. **Mid-roll CTA (about 33% mark, ≤10 s).** In-world joke-threat ask right after a payoff, then a progress marker ("N-1 more to go").
4. **Middle items (#N-1 … #2, about 60–100 s each).** Per item: card, then a cute-vs-deadly or beauty-vs-danger pivot, then a second-person idyll, then a sudden shock (smash cut), then a named mechanism replayed step by step with a time or size number, then a prey list climbing in size, then a staged death payoff using the recurring end-image phrase. Give each item one twist that breaks the pattern (a different killer, no lure, a helper creature) and one comparison joke that ranks it against earlier items. Put one puzzle loop ("something doesn't add up…") near the midpoint and close it within 10 s.
5. **Item #1 (about 20% of runtime): the famous one, held back on purpose.** Say outright that the viewer has been waiting for it. Bust a common myth about it with a number breakdown (pie chart). Then open a visible K-slot tracker ("K things make it deadly") and fill it one slot at a time. Cut one sentence short so the visual (snap, strike) finishes it. Add a side-character gag of a small creature escaping, then point the threat back at "you" ("but bigger prey like you…"). End with the slowest death in the video and the clearest end-image.
6. **Outro (≤12 s).** Ironic one-line recap of the title promise, a next-video CTA that mirrors the mid-roll joke threat, and a ritual sign-off. No summary.

**Pacing rules:** an attention reset about every 14 s ("but" pivot, question, gag, cut). A mascot cutaway every 10–20 s to cover fact lines. A voice gag every 30–60 s. A new item every 65–100 s after the first. Never more than 2 short loops open at once. One running callback phrase for the death image, reused 3–5 times and twisted once. Keep the gore light: cartoon dissolve and skeleton, never realistic.

**Device checklist:** second-person "you" in every death sequence · countdown cards · "picture this" mode switch · false safety before every trap · dramatic irony ("what you don't realize") · one-way/no-exit rules · numbers for speed, time and diet fractions · rhetorical "why/where/which" questions answered within 30 s · sentence cut off and finished by the visual · myth-bust on the most famous item · K-slot tracker for the finale · joke-threat CTAs at about 33% and at the end.
