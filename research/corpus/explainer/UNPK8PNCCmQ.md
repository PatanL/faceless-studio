- **Visual pattern interrupts** come from the material, not from gags: title card, map, scale chart, skull or jaw, board, roughly one every 30-60 s. There are no memes or stick figures (the subject is not "you").

### Escalation ladder

Three things move at once:

1. **Rival ladder (rising by size, then removed):** Anomalocaris 2 ft (too small to be food) → Cameroceras 18-ft shell, 200 lb, mostly shell → sea scorpions 6-7 ft → Dunkleosteus 12 ft, 1 t (a third of an orca) → Paleozoic sharks 6-12 ft (liver meals) → Helicoprion 23 ft (soft-prey eater, harmless) → Cymbospondylus 55 ft / Ichthyotitan 80 ft (giant but drownable prey) and Thalattoarchon 28 ft (orca-sized but solo) → **Pliosaurus 11 t with a T. rex bite, "first that could kill an adult" (63%)** → Mosasaurus hoffmanni 10-14 t, cannibal → Basilosaurus 60 ft and 25-30 ft sharks → **Megalodon 60 t + Livyatan with the largest teeth ever (88-95%)** → nothing (Pleistocene). Almost every rival is sized against the orca ("a third of", "the same length as", "twice the weight of").
2. **Environment ladder:** food goes from nothing → thin → unknown → plenty → the orca's real prey (whales) → the richest ocean; oxygen from half → modern → record high; plus a repeated heat threshold (fine at about 85°F, fatal near 100°F) and a catastrophe at most era ends (Ordovician ice age 85%, Late Devonian anoxia, end-Permian 90%, Cretaceous anoxic event, PETM). The fair-play rule drops the orcas after each catastrophe, so the test is the rivals, not the disasters.
3. **Verdict curve (a sawtooth from the top, not a climb):** Starve → Dead in a few years → 50/50 → **Dominate x3** (37-56%) → **Establish x2** (68-80%, downgrades when real rivals arrive) → Dominate (87%, rebound) → **Establish** (95%, the most famous rival) → Dominate (99%, homecoming). Corpus #2 climbs toward the top and breaks; this one reaches the top early, and its tension comes from the downgrades.

### How it ends

The second-to-last era holds the most famous rival (Megalodon, named in the drop-date line), concedes a lone orca would lose, argues speed and pod, admits "I don't know", adds a real-history fact (orca ancestors lived beside it), and rules Establish. The last era is the real world: the rivals are gone, the hook's claims are called back (nothing threatens an adult; great whites are prey), and the verdict is left unspoken ("we know how this ends") while the finished board appears. The close is a two-question CTA ("agree? disagree?") over the board, 9 s. The debatable rows (Devonian 50/50, Jurassic and Cretaceous Establish) are the comment bait, flagged earlier by the host ("controversial", "a lot of comments", "piss some people off"); the video has about 1,600 comments.

### Pacing and device notes for the PLAYBOOK

- 172 wpm overall; sentences around 13-15 words; one-word or blunt lines kept for reversals.
- **Third-person champion.** The viewer watches a known animal take a test instead of being the one tested. "You" falls to 0.5/min (corpus #2: 5.8/min); the host's "I think / I'll be honest / I don't know" carries the human voice and appears mainly at verdicts.
- **Fixed per-segment ritual:** drop date → WHY-NOW ("first ocean with X" / "when the biggest Y is alive") → fair-play skip of any lethal window ("not earlier, because...") → food check → rival with 2-3 numbers sized against the orca → MATCHUP using a documented modern orca tactic → temperature, oxygen and coastline in one or two lines → catastrophe if any → verdict with a reason → board.
- **Evidence from today's ocean is the core device.** Each matchup cites something orcas really do now (flank attacks on great whites, eating only the liver, herding fish balls and tail-slapping, drowning whales, hunting near shore, echolocation) and transfers it to the ancient rival. That makes the speculative verdicts feel earned.
- **Defined scoring words** set before any verdict, then cited as a rule when a ruling is unpopular ("technically not top dog because they wouldn't go after it").
- **Honesty as a device:** about 11 hedges or self-corrections ("nobody exactly knows", "although this is debated", "well, it kind of does", "most, not the biggest", "I don't know"). Admitting uncertainty makes the firm verdicts more believable and gives commenters something to argue with.
- **Numbers:** about 6 per minute (dates, lengths in feet, tons, °F, % oxygen, % extinction, bite force), usually paired with a comparison (thumb, sardine, tuna, mountain, seal, blue whale, tanks, T. rex, saltwater crocodile, bull orca).
- **Callbacks to the hook** (great whites at 4:17 and 18:52, blue whales at 9:58, "apex predator" at 10:50, "nothing hunts them" at 11:57 and 18:49) tie the 19 minutes to the first 20 seconds.
- **Repeated formulas** become signposts: every opener names the era and a date in one line ("we're in / the orcas are in / the orcas land in [era], around [N] million years ago"), "because this is the first/when...", "but then there are the [rival]", "they're not going to actively hunt them" (the Establish rule).
- Visual rhythm: one cut about every 5-10 s; documentary orca footage for every orca claim; paleoart/CGI and museum objects for every rival; palaeo-globe map in most eras; silhouette scale chart with a person for each major rival; board after verdicts. No stick-figure gags; humor is light and rare (about 3 asides).

## 3. Transferable skeleton (no original wording)

Template for a "Would [MODERN CHAMPION] rule every [PAST OCEAN / ERA / DEPTH ZONE]?" ranked explainer, 12-19 min. For our ocean kit: "Would a great white / sperm whale / giant squid survive every prehistoric sea", or the reverse ("Would [ancient monster] rule today's ocean, zone by zone"). Percentages are of total runtime. At about 170 wpm, 15 min is about 2,550 words; 9-11 items at 45-140 s each.

1. **Champion hook, 0-2% (50-60 words, about 190 wpm).** State the champion's modern title as a flat fact. Give three proofs of it in rising order, the last one unexpected (something from the human world). End on an absolute claim the video will test (nothing preys on it). Ask the title question as a thought experiment: transplant it unchanged into every past setting. Visual: two strong footage shots of the champion.
2. **Rules, 2-4.5% (about 80 words).** Pick the strongest version of the champion and say why (so no verdict can be blamed on a weak choice), on a real reference chart. Fix the numbers (how many, what group size). Define 2 scoring words for the top rows (winning outright vs merely surviving) in one sentence each, plus the death rows. Show the empty board with every item waiting in a pool below it.
3. **Item blocks, 4.5-99% (9-11 blocks; short early, longest in the middle where real rivals appear, compressed at the end).** Each block repeats:
   - **Title card + date**, then a one-line reason for this exact date (the first setting with X, or the moment the biggest Y lived).
   - **Fair-play skip** if part of the setting is instantly lethal: say you are moving the drop past it, so the test is the rivals, not the disaster.
   - **Food check** first (is there enough to feed the group?), with an everyday size comparison.
   - **Rival rung:** the setting's top predator, 2-3 numbers, always sized against the champion (a fraction of, the same as, twice). Scale chart with a person.
   - **Matchup:** one documented behavior of the champion today, transferred to this rival or prey. Name one champion-only advantage early and call it back in a later matchup.
   - Temperature, oxygen and coastline in one or two lines, with one reusable threshold (fine below X, fatal above Y).
   - Catastrophe at the era's end, if any, with one percentage.
   - **Verdict** in the host's voice with a stated reason, then the board update. Flag 2-3 debatable verdicts aloud as controversial.
   - Spoken tease every 30-50 s ("but there's one problem", "something new has arrived", "you're probably asking why the rival doesn't win").
4. **Verdict curve: reach the top early, then threaten it.** Death rows first (setting too empty or too hot), first win at about 35-40%, a run of wins, downgrades at about 65-80% when rivals that could kill an adult appear (announce the first one explicitly as a first), a quick rebound, and the most famous rival held for the second-to-last item (name it in the drop-date line so the viewer anticipates it).
5. **Admit uncertainty where it is real:** 8-12 hedges or self-corrections across the video, plus one honest "I don't know" before the hardest ruling.
6. **Homecoming final item, 95-99%.** The last setting is the real history where the champion did take over. Remove the rivals by callback, repeat the hook's proofs as facts, and leave the verdict unspoken while the finished board appears.
7. **Close, about 1% (20-25 words).** Point at the board, ask the viewer to agree or disagree, and hold the board for 5-7 s. No recap.
