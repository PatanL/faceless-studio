**Key mechanic: the unreliable first-person narrator is the monster.** For 7 minutes the "I" is a scared victim being coached by an online exorcist to spot a fake human among his roommates, using one diagnostic rule from the hook (fake humans mix up front/back and misuse words). At 7:12 the narrator himself commits the word-swap tell, admits he is the leader of the fake humans wearing the narrator's body, and reveals that the whole investigation, including the online post in line 2, was bait to lure the exorcist. A second reveal (8:52) turns the exorcist into his next victim, and a third swerve (9:03-9:22) reframes the monster as an avenger of bullying, ending on a closed, almost comic "The end."

## 4. Transferable skeleton

Abstract form, no original wording. Durations scale to a 3-6 min target; % is of runtime.

1. **Ordinary wrongness (0-1%).** A time stamp and a small repeated noise or habit from someone the hero lives with.
2. **Outside expert (1-2%).** The hero vents in a public channel; a stranger with an expert-sounding handle answers.
3. **Premise claim + diagnostic rule (2-4%).** The stranger names the companion as a disguised predator, states the cost, and quotes one private, checkable quirk as the tell (a rule of the creature). The hero freezes.
4. **Evidence and false close (4-10%).** Two matching memories; an objection answered with a darker claim (the real companion is already gone); the stranger assigns a test; the test finds a mundane explanation; the hero cuts the stranger off.
5. **Re-scare at the opening image (10-17%).** The opening noise repeats, now face to face, with the very object the test looked for. The hero reconnects. The stranger adds lore: a stage, a deadline, a weakness.
6. **Pre-emptive excuse (17-23%).** The suspect offers an innocent explanation before being asked and an invitation that sets up the next scene. Keep it under 25 s.
7. **Prophecy, then fulfilment (23-36%).** The stranger forbids one oddly specific thing; it appears at the next scene; the tell fires visibly; the hero refuses with a quick lie; the suspect leans in and tests him; a third person defuses it.
8. **Counter-accusation midpoint (40-50%).** Alone with the suspect, who accuses the third person using the same clues told from his side. The narrator reasons one clue away. Suspicion moves.
9. **Tell on the new suspect, chase, rescue (50-65%).** The tell fires on the second suspect; a veiled confession; a chase; caught; the stranger rescues in person with a spectacle prop and reveals the threat is wider (an institution silent).
10. **Expert's blind spot + siege (65-77%).** The expert admits he can't detect a well-disguised creature (plant). The pair hide; the scariest image (a crowd at the window), every exit blocked, the narrator's phone "dead".
11. **Narrator commits the tell (77-80%).** The narrator makes the hook's own mistake; the companion catches it; the narrator confesses he is the creature, and its leader.
12. **Everything was staged (80-86%).** Motive revealed (the "victims" wronged the original host); the other suspect was an accomplice; every clue was planted to lure the expert.
13. **Trap closes (86-93%).** The narrator uses the hero's lifeline channel to lure the expert, silences the real victim mid-warning, and lies to the expert while the viewer watches; the expert says he is the last who knows.
14. **Second reveal (93-96%).** "The loss is you." The expert realises too late.
15. **Moral swerve or cut (96-100%).** Either the creature justifies itself with its own rule and the expert is won over (closed "the end"), or, for our Part-1 format, cut on the expert's realisation and hold the swerve for Part 2.

Production notes for our horror kit (code-only): the whole thing runs on three shot types (two-shot in a room, phone-DM half-screen, one prop close-up), plus one crowd-at-the-window image late; reveals need only brow and pupil swaps on two static puppets. Use our own creature, rule, setting and props. The signature device to borrow is the **rule from the hook that finally fires on the narrator**; vary it (a voice in a recording, a reflection, an app's "is this a human?" check) and don't reuse the roommate/exorcist/fake-human premise, the bullying-revenge motive, or any wording.
