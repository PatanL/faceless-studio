2. The authority says it's over (false safety, 0:08)
3. The threat is at **my** door, disguised as help (0:12)
4. Evidence that the authority is lying (0:28)
5. Physical aggression and a call for backup (1:15–1:24)
6. The group publicly names and pressures me (@-tags, 1:35–1:51)
7. They can get keys (1:49)
8. Neighbors are dead and their identities are being used (1:56)
9. Every outside line is cut (2:03)
10. My only ally is silenced (2:21)
11. I was set up a month ago (2:40–2:50)
12. They are physically breaking in (2:59)
13. It's a **crew**, not one killer (3:44)
14. The authority figure never existed (4:00)
15. Last lifeline dies; unknown sender says flee (4:02–4:08)

Each rung either moves the threat **closer** (building, door, inside) or **widens** it (one man, a network, a long-planned setup).

### How it ends
A **hard cliffhanger with no resolution and no spoken CTA.** A resource countdown (battery, "30 seconds") cuts the information off at peak need. A final anonymous notification turns the tension into a new loop. The last line is an **imperative** ("get out"), so the story stops mid-action. It works as part 1 of a series.

### Pacing and device notes for the PLAYBOOK
- **262 wpm** is far above the explainer pace. These reposts run TTS fast and pack ~1,100 words into 4 minutes. Short 3–6 word lines ("I stayed silent.", "This was way too coincidental.") act as breath marks between 12–16 word lines.
- **Chat read-out formula:** `[Name] from [unit].` then `[message]`. Each speaker gets a unit number, which turns the cast into a building map and makes "802" a target label.
- **Number-matching reveal:** plant an odd count on the antagonist, then find the same count in the chat. It is cheap to animate (a close-up, then the chat) and lets the viewer feel smart.
- **Three channels, in order:** door/peephole, then group chat, then a phone call. Each channel confirms the twist independently and ups the stakes.
- **Real vs. fake authority:** the fake authority owns the chat (mutes, deletes, tags). The real authority shows up only at the end, on a failing line.
- **Visual grammar (from the sheet):** about 45% of tiles are the held phone with chat (big hand, gray phone, white bubbles). The rest are the hero at the door, cop close-ups, a dark flashback room, prop close-ups (stickers, burner phone) and a red countdown card. The background is nearly always dim blue or black; the only bright frames are the flashback, which marks the past.

## 3. Transferable skeleton (no original wording)

Target: 60–250 s, 9:16, narrated first person, chat UI as the main visual. Times are % of runtime.

1. **Hook (0–5%)**: one line that places a danger inside the viewer's own home or building. Add one uncanny **signature rule** for the threat (a sign that announces it). Add one short texture line.
2. **False safety (3–5%)**: an official voice in a shared channel (building chat, school group, workplace thread) declares the danger over.
3. **Inciting knock (5–10%)**: the threat arrives at the hero's barrier (door, window, gate) dressed as help or authority. The hero nearly complies, then hesitates.
4. **Two clues (8–10%)**: one breaks a common-knowledge rule ("they always come in pairs"). One is an odd, countable detail on the visitor (N small marks).
5. **Count-match reveal (10–12%)**: the hero checks the shared channel and finds the same number N in it (N people confirmed the all-clear).
6. **Evidence replay (12–22%)**: re-read the channel line by line as `[Name, location]: [message]`. The messages are lie, pretext (a reason to enter homes), then N−1 instant agreeing replies. Each sounds normal but pushes compliance.
7. **Counter-voices (22–28%)**: 1–2 real members doubt it with physical evidence. Nobody answers them.
8. **Escalation at the barrier (28–34%)**: the visitor gets aggressive, gives an ultimatum and calls in "reinforcements" naming the hero's location.
9. **Escalation in the channel (34–45%)**: coordinated alibi, then the hero is @-tagged by peers, then by the top authority. They threaten to bypass the barrier (keys, records).
10. **Deduction (45–49%)**: the hero concludes that the agreeing accounts' owners are dead and the threat is wearing their identities.
11. **Isolation (49–53%)**: outside lines fail in familiar ways. Only the enemy's channel still works.
12. **Lone ally + censorship (53–61%)**: one member warns the hero directly with a vivid physical clue. The authority deletes it and silences everyone.
13. **Low point + backstory (61–72%)**: "this was a setup." A flashback to a too-good-to-be-true deal shows how the hero was targeted (an object or device tampered with long ago).
14. **Siege + improvised tool (72–80%)**: the barrier is under attack. The hero remembers a left-behind object that gives one last lifeline, with a built-in handicap (low battery, one use).
15. **Countdown contact (80–97%)**: the hero reaches the real authority with a visible countdown (N seconds). Ask the opening questions out loud. Answers in order: confirm the trap is deliberate; close the main loop (it's not over); **scale-up twist** (one threat becomes many); **identity twist** (the authority figure who ran the channel doesn't exist).
16. **Cliffhanger (97–100%)**: the lifeline dies mid-answer. One last message arrives from an unknown sender with an imperative order. End with no resolution. Leave 3–5 loops open (the threat's signature, what they want, the ally's fate, who sent the last message).

Pacing rules borrowed: core hook ≤ 25 words / ≤ 6 s; a re-hook every ~8 s, tightening to 3–5 s in the final third; alternate long lines with 3–6 word verdict lines; switch the visual every ~6 s across door, chat and close-up; a countdown in the last 20%; no spoken CTA, the cliffhanger is the CTA.
