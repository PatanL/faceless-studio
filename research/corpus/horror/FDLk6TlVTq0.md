The script craft is not obviously worse: it has a strong tricolon escalation (hand, face, door), a counting rule with a chilling payoff, a lift beat and a bread-on-your-sill reversal, and its threat cadence is actually **tighter** than the outlier's. What differs is packaging and position:
1. **No hook in the first line.** The outlier's line 1 is its title rule; here line 1 is a soft filler ("So, …") plus a baseline of normal pigeons. The anomaly ("gone quiet") lands at 0:30 (9%) and the title rule only at 0:38.
2. **The viewer is a spectator, not a participant.** The outlier's cold open makes you do the act (you wave back) and the rules manage your own mistake. Here you watch birds; the guidance is about not starting something.
3. **An animal threat, not a human double.** Pigeons are mildly uncanny but impersonal; the uncanny human (residents who wave back late) is a side detail in rule 1, not the premise.
4. **A catalogue, not one night.** The outlier pins all five rules to one evening (arrival, chores, a text, a knock, bedtime, morning). Here the rules are loosely spread over days (any time, morning count, coming home, at night), so there is no single plot clock.
5. **Title asks for a thing nobody does** ("don't feed them"), while the outlier's title tells you how to do a thing everyone does ("only wave once").

## 3. Transferable skeleton (no original wording)

What to keep from this control is its **escalation ladder and its devices**; what to avoid is its **opening**. Use with the #17 skeleton for a 5:00–5:30 YouTube 16:9 guidance episode at 135–150 wpm, about 700–780 words, five rules plus a bonus rule.

1. **Cold open, 0–10% (fix of this video's weakness):** line 1 is the title rule. Then show the anomaly within 10 s, with "you" doing something ordinary in it. Do **not** spend 25 s on a normal baseline before the wrongness; if a baseline is needed, cut it to 2–3 lines *after* the anomaly appears ("normally they would...").
2. **Contract, 10–12%:** the number of rules and the tracker, tied to "what happens next".
3. **Rule 1, 12–25%:** the title rule, a triplet of negative fragments ending on the most personal item (plant that item for the last line). Then a **three-rung memory/knowledge ladder** (it learns a body part, then your identity, then where you live), each rung a split line with a pause. Then the consequence seen on other residents, ending on a **one human-uncanny tell with an on-screen measurement tag** (a delay, a wrong count). Move that tell earlier if it is the strongest image.
4. **Rule 2, 25–40%:** a normal-vs-wrong behaviour test (3–4 short normal lines, then the tell), a forbidden natural reaction, the indifference beat, then a slow split-line reveal that the gaze sometimes points at one window, with "if you already did and it's yours" turning it on the viewer. Keep this rule short (it is set-up, not contact).
5. **Rule 3, 40–56%:** a counting or record rule. Forbid writing or photographing (the record disagrees with your eyes). Then the reframe: the count matches something human (people, flats, nights), "for now", and a mirror pair of if/then lines (one more means X, one fewer means Y). **Peak at about 50–58%.**
6. **Rule 4, 56–75% (make this the longest):** the threat crosses your threshold and behaves like a resident (a courtesy gesture, it knows your floor). Forbid sharing an enclosed space; take the long way; it is already there. A trace left at your door the next morning, with a one-word caption hedge undercutting the comfort.
7. **Rule 5, 75–92%:** reverse rule 1: the forbidden act is now being done **from inside your home without you**. Bin the evidence, shut the window, a quiet sound threat with 2-word qualifiers, a time-bound end ("until dawn"). **Main chill at about 80–85%.**
8. **Bonus and inversion, 92–98%:** "one final thing", negate the premise ("it was never about X"), then end on the threat inside *your* space (use the rule-5 implication, which this control left unused), with a callback to the body part planted in rule 1.
9. **End card, 98–100%:** glitch and a numbered file card.

**Devices to reuse (from this video):**
- Field-guide callouts to set a visual baseline (labelled arrows on the creature).
- A three-rung "it remembers..." ladder, one rung per split line.
- A measurement tag on screen for a human-uncanny delay.
- "If you already did, and it's yours..." to turn a general rule on the viewer.
- A count that matches the residents, with mirrored +1 / −1 lines.
- Evidence that disagrees with your eyes (the photo shows another number).
- An animal or object performing a human courtesy (holding a door, waiting by your door).
- "Already there" on arrival.
- A trace token on the doormat; a one-word red caption hedge ("mostly").
- Rule 5 as a reversal of rule 1, done from inside your home.
- Exhibit photo cards and red/white numeric counters as visual re-hooks.

**Pacing targets (corrected by the outlier comparison):**
- Anomaly by 0:10, a re-hook by 0:05 and at least every 8 s in the first 40 s; never a 25 s+ opening without a threat sign.
- 135–150 wpm overall; split lines and 1–3 word punches about every 10–15 s.
- Threat signs every 12–16 s; rule cards every 40–65 s; the longest rule = the closest contact.
- Peaks at about 55% and about 83%, a short release, then a quiet inversion in the last 5%.
