**Key mechanic:** a **listicle used as a confession**. The surface is a helpful, snarky how-to ("rules if your house is haunted") with a dead friend as the worked example, so every rule is a mini re-hook and every example is a rung of a haunting ladder (sounds → anxious dogs → child nightmares → wounds → family leaves → isolation → the challenge → death on camera). Under the surface the narrator keeps dropping **motive plants** that read as jokes on first watch (he calls the victim an idiot, "spoiler alert" he's dead, the wife was "my ex-girlfriend", "what kind of friend steals your girlfriend", "not bitter, at least not as far as the police are concerned"). The **last rule ("rule out everything else first") flips the genre**: each earlier ghost sign gets a mundane, man-made cause in a "maybe he would have found..." list, and the kill on camera becomes a paralyzing poison plus a man in a costume. The ending turns on the viewer: by watching, you are an accessory, and he can trace your IP, so "we wouldn't want your house to become haunted". The supernatural story is fully told and fully scary before it is taken away, so the video pays off twice.

### How it ends

- **Genre flip via the last list item.** Rule 11 ("rule out everything else") contradicts the purpose of the list. It is followed by a four-item "Maybe he would have found..." list that maps one-to-one onto the ghost signs (knocking, nightmares/visions, dogs, paralysis), so the reveal feels earned rather than random.
- The motive is delivered by **three "if only" lines** that slow down and end on the personal grievance.
- The narrator **asks the viewer's question himself** ("is this my confession?"), denies it, then turns the threat outward: reading makes you an accessory, he can trace your IP, and the last line twists the title ("we wouldn't want your house to become haunted").
- **No CTA, no outro talk**: the last spoken line is the sting, then a ~10 s end card.

### Pacing and device notes for the PLAYBOOK

- **146 wpm**, steady between 125 and 176 per minute. The kill scene and the final threat are the slowest minutes (short, pause-split phrases); the advice stretches are the fastest.
- **Rules list as a long-form chassis.** Numbered rules give a 22-minute story a visible progress bar (viewers know "rule 10 = the big one" is near the end) and a re-hook every ~68 s for free. Number the units and pre-label the climax unit ("the big one").
- **Rule → "Nathan didn't" → new sign → escalation closer** is a repeatable 40–90 s unit. Ten of them build the haunting without any dialogue.
- **Two hooks:** a format hook in the second person (0:02) and a death hook from the narrator's relationship to the victim (1:04). "Spoiler alert, he's dead" up front buys a long build, just as the host's spoiler does in `8bo9yZvedUM`.
- **Plant the twist as character comedy.** Every motive clue is first heard as the narrator's snark. The rewatch value comes from this.
- **Experts rule out the mundane** (vet, doctors, police) to build the supernatural reading, and the twist then mocks that very move ("exhaust all other options").
- **Found-footage narration for the kill**: an exact timestamp (2:13 a.m.), present tense, "is seen", a withheld whisper, a look into the camera. It gives a narrated story a visual shot list.
- **Tonal breathers right before the big rungs** (benign ghosts before wounds, an advice-only rule before the exorcism/evidence/challenge run).
- **Ending implicates the viewer**: second-person bookend, accessory logic, a tech threat, and a title callback as the last line.
- Long-form only: the first rule comes at 14% and the death at 77%, both far too late for a Short. For a Short, keep the unit (rule → broken → sign) and the twist-by-last-rule, and cut to 3–4 rules.

- Show each rule as a numbered title card; it is both the chapter marker and the re-hook. Pre-label the climax rule.
- The climax is described as camera footage, so the visuals can be a static "security camera" frame with a timestamp and slow changes (door, hand, figure), never a clear face.
