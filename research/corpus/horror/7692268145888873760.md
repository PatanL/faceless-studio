## 3. Transferable skeleton (no original wording)

Use this for a 2:30–3:00 first-person "rules of a job" horror Part 1, at 250–280 wpm, about 700–780 words.

1. **Hook, 0–6% (about 50 words, 10 s):**
   - A question in second person about a secret code of an unusual job or place.
   - Then 2–3 short imperative rules, one of them a warning against overconfidence.
   - Then one authority line (mentor, years of experience).
   - End on a confession that tonight the narrator broke them all.
   - Visual: establishing wide shot, a flash image of the worst outcome, then the narrator.
2. **Inciting break, 6–13%:**
   - A specific time that echoes a rule.
   - A group of strangers with a precise headcount asks for help, acting a little too desperate.
   - One hedged description ("seemed / looked like").
   - The narrator lets them in, breaking rule 1 in one short sentence.
3. **Threat tag, 13–17%:**
   - The strangers name a pursuer with one memorable visual tag (a colour or object).
   - The narrator answers with a boastful line that breaks the overconfidence rule.
4. **Uncanny official signal, 17–31%:**
   - A neutral outside source (broadcast, notice, call) reports an old incident that matches both the pursuer's tag and the strangers' tag, including a death.
   - The narrator cuts the source off before it ends (loop).
   - Passengers react; someone says the coincidence out loud.
   - The narrator gives a reassuring false explanation that closes the loop on paper and deepens it in reality.
5. **Trust lull, 31–45% (keep it under 20 s; this is the weak spot in the reference):**
   - A small kindness, names exchanged, thanks.
   - One supernatural foreshadow line (prayer, charm, ritual).
   - Plant a callback device here (a mirror, a camera, a window).
6. **Clue ladder, 45–62%:** three escalating inconsistencies about the strangers, each one shown as a question the narrator asks.
   - Clue 1: something about age, appearance or timing is wrong.
   - Clue 2: their story has a gap in logic.
   - Clue 3: a physical symptom, which a family member suppresses unnaturally.
   - Each answer gets interrupted, dodged or met with an awkward smile. Close none of them.
7. **External escalation, 62–93%:**
   - The pursuer returns, cued by a sound first and then the visual tag.
   - The callback device reveals one grotesque, physically wrong detail about the pursuer. Make it ambiguous: attack or warning?
   - The narrator rationalises it.
   - Add setting constraints (narrow, no exits, a drop).
   - Escalate the pursuer's signalling (horn, lights, shouting) and use one explicit death metaphor at the peak.
   - Cut back once to a passenger behaving strangely.
8. **Fake win and hard cut, 93–100%:**
   - The narrator repeats the overconfidence line to the passengers and makes a decisive move that shakes off the pursuer.
   - End on a lingering image of the aftermath. No resolution, no CTA.
   - The viewer should feel that the pursuer was the warning and the passengers are the danger. The price of the broken rules stays unpaid until Part 2.

**Devices to reuse:** a rule list as Chekhov's guns; a confession cliffhanger in the hook; colour-tag callbacks; an official signal that is cut off; a false rational explanation; an unanswered question to a suspicious character; a symptom that a guardian suppresses; a sound cue before a visual reveal; a mirror reveal; a single grotesque image; a death metaphor at the peak; a fake win; an ending based on dramatic irony.

**Pacing targets:**
- Re-hook at least every 6–8 s; never let a gap run past 15 s.
- Cut the picture every 2 s.
- Put a 3–5 word punch line at each spike.
- Alternate the inside threat and the outside threat whenever one of them stalls.
