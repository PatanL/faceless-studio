**Key mechanic:** this is a **rules-list PSA** with no characters and no plot. The first 41 s build a creepy entity through a list of symptoms, narrowing from "school districts" to "your own mind". Then one line, "in your class" (with a full-screen yellow flash), turns the viewer into the subject, and the remaining 46% of the runtime is a numbered survival list. Each rule hides a small story ("if you find yourself seated beside them without remembering how you got there"). The list has 3 numbered rules, and then the numbering silently stops while rules keep coming. Nothing is explained, and the broadcast just signs off. Retention comes from **escalating proximity** (district, then classroom, then your house, then your seat, then your memory), not from a twist.

### Escalation ladder (the threat gets closer)

1. Abstract and institutional: "multiple school zones" (0:03)
2. Every classroom: one extra student (0:06)
3. Social memory fails: teachers and classmates (0:13–0:15)

8. **Your class**: the turn, with the yellow flash (0:41–0:45)

Distance shrinks at every step, from district to room to house to seat to memory. Harm peaks at step 11, and the last two steps move the threat into the viewer's own past.

### Ending

There is no reveal and no twist. Two final "do not" lines, then the brand sign-off, "SIGNAL LOST", and a soft CTA disguised as broadcast copy ("Stay tuned. Stay safe."). The SUBSCRIBE desk is on screen in every anchor shot, so the CTA is built into the scenery rather than spoken. Ending without an answer leaves every loop open, which suits a series channel: the next "broadcast" is the follow-up.

## 3. Transferable skeleton (no original wording)

Format: a deadpan "official transmission" about one impossible entity, followed by a survival list addressed to the viewer. 85–100 s, about 280–300 words, about 180 wpm (slower for the first 12 s and the last 5 s).

1. **Ident + attention command (0–3%).** A fixed brand ident in a calm institutional voice and a one-line order to pay attention. Visual: a recurring "presenter" pictogram with the subscribe cue built into the set.
2. **Vague official event, then the specific uncanny fact (3–12%).** One bureaucratic, unexplained event at a large scale, then one concrete, countable anomaly that pays off the title before 0:12. The entity appears visually here, in its signature colour, for the first time.
3. **Symptom list in third person (12–30%).** 4–6 short lines on how ordinary witnesses fail to notice or remember it. Use contradiction pairs ("absent from X, yet always Y") and two parallel fragments for rhythm. Insert the **first "you" around 20%**, with a glitch frame.
4. **Personal-space shock (about 24%).** One conditional line where the entity shows knowledge of the viewer's private space, with a short trailing clause as the sting. Hold an iconic image of that space.
5. **Into the viewer's mind (30–40%).** The entity defeats perception, then a present-tense second-person line says it is happening to the viewer now.
6. **Dry official bridge (40–43%).** One understated line implying institutional concern or a cover-up. This lowers tension before the turn.
7. **The turn (43–48%).** "If you suspect it in *your* [place], follow these rules." This is the video's single full-frame colour flash or pattern interrupt. Show a rules-document icon.
8. **Rule 1, the longest (48–64%).** A numbered prohibition, then a conditional that assumes the entity has already got to the viewer, then an **exact ritual phrase** to say, then "no matter what happens" stakes that are never explained. Cut back to the presenter at the end of this rule as a chapter break.
9. **Rules 2–3, fast (64–76%).** Each is a numbered prohibition plus a one-line impossible consequence. Rule 3 carries the **first real harm** (people missing, kept vague) and echoes the private-space motif from step 4.
10. **Unnumbered rules (76–90%).** Quietly drop the numbering. Add a participation rule the viewer can try right now (count or check something), then "you are not imagining it". Then a rule implying the entity has already interacted with the viewer in the past, solved with a mundane polite exit.
11. **Two short absolute prohibitions (90–94%)** that close off fighting back or drawing attention.
12. **Ritual sign-off (94–100%).** The brand name, a "signal lost" card, and a soft CTA written as broadcast copy. No explanation, and every loop after the first stays open.

**Pacing rules:** one new uncanny fact or rule about every 5–6 s, one big jolt about every 12–20 s, one visual (pictogram) change about every 3 s, and at most one hold of about 10 s (on the ritual). Use "you" for about 6% of words, almost none before 20% of the runtime and most of it after the turn. Keep the visual grammar strict: the entity is the only element in colour, everything else is monochrome, and the presenter shot works as a chapter marker.
