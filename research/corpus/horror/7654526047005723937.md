### How it ends

No resolution. CTA "tap the story below for the full ending" lands straight on that threat, at about the highest danger of the video.

## 3. Pacing numbers to copy (for PLAYBOOK)

- 232 wpm, flat; 5-9 word sentences; past tense first person.
- Hook 14 s / ~57 words, threat at the door by 5 s, first twist by 9 s.
- A micro turn every ~11 s; an audience-chorus beat every ~45 s; a channel switch every 30-40 s.

## 4. Transferable skeleton

Abstract form, no original wording. Durations scale to a 3-6 min target; % is of runtime.

1. **Cold threat (0-2%).** One sentence: a danger is loose inside the hero's shared building/space.
2. **False safety (2-3%).** The official channel (group chat or announcer) reassures, then reports the danger is over far too quickly, with a specific number.
3. **Knock (3%).** A trusted-looking authority arrives at the hero's door.
4. **Omniscient warning (3-4%).** A source the hero cannot explain (overlay text, stranger's message, a rule list) says the authority is the danger and gives a victim count. The hero freezes.
5. **Deduction (4-7%).** The hero links two facts (count of reassurers = count of victims) and realises the safe channel is compromised. Two inner questions.
6. **Pressure stack (7-16%).** The door threat escalates in three steps (command, threat to break in, call for reinforcements with the hero's address). The chat piles on with fake reassurance, legal threat, a key, peer shaming. Hero says "something is off" and refuses.
7. **Frame reveal + isolation (16-31%).** The omniscient source reveals a larger game (the hero is being watched or tested) and states one rule: there is always a way out. Each outside channel fails in turn (calls, messages), a lone honest neighbor warns by room number, the fake authority mutes everyone. End on "do I just wait to die?"
8. **Planted object, midpoint (31-43%).** The hero remembers an object with a mysterious backstory. It reaches the one real authority, with a hard countdown. In rapid questions the real authority confirms the impostors, scales the threat (one -> many) and identifies the impostor manager by a physical description. The call dies on the countdown. A cryptic rule about an unnamed "it".
9. **Lull and a new face (43-51%).** The threat leaves; an off-screen crash; a pleading visitor whose description matches the impostor. The visitor offers a story with checkable details (a floor, a room).
10. **Dilemma with chorus (51-58%).** The omniscient chorus argues both sides and makes a joke bet. The hero checks one surface clue and opens the door.
11. **Hidden hand + clock (58-72%).** The hero quietly withholds his best cards from the newcomer (and from the viewer's full view), measures how long the barrier will last, the threat returns with a heavier tool, and the hero lays out a 4-step plan with positions.
12. **Double twist (72-78%).** The plan triggers, the newcomer betrays, and the hero is revealed to have been somewhere else all along; explain it with one piece of clean spatial or time logic from the newcomer's own story.
13. **Crowd reward (78-82%).** The chorus celebrates, pays off the joke bet, and ends with "it isn't over".
14. **Cost reveal (82-88%).** The hero checks the newcomer's story and finds proof of a real victim. The chorus re-asks the big series question (who is behind the game).
15. **Eavesdrop cliffhanger (88-99%).** A captured device lets the hero (and viewer) hear the enemy: confusion, a smarter voice spots the trick, a leader issues a precise sweep plan that converges on the hero's position, and the last enemy line is a threat.
16. **CTA (last ~2 s).** Send to the next part at the peak of danger.

Production notes for our horror kit (code-only): four overlay types cover almost every shot (door/peephole two-shot, group-chat half-screen, glowing "omniscient text" box, phone-to-ear); puppets need only 4-5 eye/brow swaps plus a hand-to-chin pose; the last 10% can be one static prop shot with a flashing "sound" icon while voices carry the cliffhanger; add a periodic light sweep to keep still frames alive. Use our own story, characters, setting, numbers and the omniscient-source device in a different form (it is the reference series' signature; vary it, e.g. a rule sheet, a stranger's texts, or an app notification feed).
