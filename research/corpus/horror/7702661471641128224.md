## 3. Transferable skeleton (no original wording)

For a **Part 2 (or later) episode** of a serialized 2D horror story, 150–200 s, at about 330–370 wpm with a cut every 2–4 s.

1. **0–6% · Mid-scene cold open.** Resume with a mundane action that hands control from the narrator to the companion or the situation (they sleep, someone else drives, the narrator looks away). No recap.
2. **6–10% · Jolt.** A time skip reveals that a known element from the last episode has disappeared or changed. Withhold the next observation for 2–3 s ("when I looked over...").
3. **10–25% · Concrete danger + fast physical ladder.** Open with one hard number or gauge shown as a macro insert. Then add 6–9 rungs, roughly every 3 s: a command is ignored → wrong body texture → a deadline appears ahead → the narrator's intervention fails → wrong emotion (calm or a smile) → maximum effort fails → the death thought.
4. **25–33% · Last-second save.** A small object, seen earlier or justified in a one-line flashback, is used in a specific ritual action on a specific body spot. The effect is instant, followed by a near-miss stop at the edge.
5. **33–36% · False calm + doubt.** The companion gives a mundane excuse; the narrator says it isn't that simple.
6. **36–42% · Act break + evidence.** Time-of-day change (night → dawn), then new physical evidence on the companion's body that can grow later.
7. **42–50% · Mirror reveal.** Just ahead, someone else suffered the fate they barely escaped, by the same stated cause. A **specific identifier planted in the previous part** (plate, room number, name tag) proves it was the person who broke the rule. Turn it back on the companion: they broke it too.
8. **50–62% · Outside check → twist.** The narrator verifies a small detail of the companion's earlier backstory with a family member. A pause, then a short question that falsifies it, plus an airtight reason. The narrator hides it and stands in safe daylight feeling cold.
9. **62–65% · Reframe.** A blunt statement, then a self-correction: it's not the person, it's what's in them.
10. **65–73% · Mentor call.** Callback to lore mentioned before. The mentor's concern escalates by category ("this isn't the usual kind"). Add a time limit (before dark), a specific place, and a password or referral.
11. **73–88% · Threshold + test.** Mundane-wrapped occult location with a dense sensory list of ritual objects. An old, indifferent expert; the password makes them stop mid-task.
12. **88–98% · Examination ladder.** Each rung compares against an earlier state (worse than this morning, heavier than one person, the mark has spread). The expert's face darkens.
13. **98–100% · Diagnosis cliffhanger.** The expert names the entity in one line. Cut immediately: no remedy, no CTA voice line.

**Rules of thumb pulled from this one:**
- Close one loop and open a larger one inside the same 5 s.
- Give every act exactly one macro "evidence" insert per major turn.
- Reuse one body detail at two points (breathing, mark length) to show change without exposition.
- Let a 2-word line of dialogue carry each twist.
- Keep a sensory-list scene under ~25 s, because that is this video's longest gap between re-hooks.

**For our kit:**
- Macro inserts: a gauge/number card, a road/edge establishing card, an object-on-mirror close-up, a skin-mark close-up that can grow between shots, an ID-plate close-up, and a phone "in call" screen.
- Stick-figure characters need: a blank stare, a creepy grin, red-faced strain, pale-apologetic, eyes-closed-shadowed and old-man-frown expressions.
- Backgrounds: cab interior, roadside/cliff edge, sunrise, rest stop, rural phone cutaway, and a funeral-goods shop.
