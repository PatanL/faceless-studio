### Escalation ladder
1. Rule spoken (words only)
2. Companion breaks a minor rule casually and reacts strangely
3. A stranger breaks the major rule
4. The stranger's body is wrong (stiff gait, impossibly loud thuds)
5. The hearse responds (the door opens, a token is given)
6. False relief: a rational, named custom
7. The companion claims a personal link to the dead
8. The companion's body acts on its own (seatbelt, door, jump, no pain)
9. The companion repeats the ritual exactly while the procession waits for her
10. The dead itself reaches out (the bony hand; the template deviates)
11. The companion returns *wrong* (too neat, too calm, drained face)
12. Her explanation contradicts known facts
13. She asks to take control of the vehicle

The ladder also moves **closer**: road ahead → stranger outside → companion inside the cab → companion in the driver's seat. Each rung brings the threat nearer to the narrator, and so to the viewer.

### How it ends
No resolution. A time jump ("10 minutes later") leads to the possessed character asking for control. The narrator agrees with kind, reasonable logic (dramatic irony), and the video cuts mid-thought. The last shot copies the first (POV, charm, hearse in fog), so the loop feels complete visually while the story is open. "Part 1" in the title sends viewers looking for Part 2.

## 3. Transferable skeleton

The template uses no original wording. Percentages are of runtime. Targets: 60–90 s pilot or 3–4 min long form, about one cut every 2–3 s, a re-hook at most every ~10 s (median 8 s), and only one breather up to ~25 s, placed just before the midpoint.

1. **Hook (0–3%, ≤6 s, ~30–35 words).** State an insider rule of a specific job or place as if everyone knows it ("people in [trade] never [act]"). Give the polite public reason, then the real, fearful reason. The threat is already on screen in frame 1.
2. **Personal entry (3–8%).** "Tonight" or "last week" in the first person. The rule's object arrives uninvited. List 2–3 vivid omen props and one detail that can only be half seen.
3. **Rule #1 as conflict (8–11%).** A companion who voices the viewer's impatience proposes breaking the rule. The narrator answers with a short maxim, a flat imperative, and an appeal to the authority of the trade.
4. **Trap and irony (11–15%).** A number that removes escape (distance or time until help), a grim statistic about the place, and a "this is actually the safe option" line that the ending will turn around.
5. **Minor rule broken first, stated second (15–21%).** The companion casually does a small forbidden act. The narrator snaps, then states the rule ("that's another rule"). The companion reacts strangely and nobody explains why (loop A).
6. **A stranger breaks the major rule (21–26%).** Name one hyper-specific detail (an ID, number or label) as a plant. Subvert the expected beat: they don't flee, they stop and confront.
7. **Uncanny ritual #1 (26–37%).** A slow, wrong-bodied approach with a distinctive movement detail you will reuse. A counted ritual action with impossible sensory intensity, and a gesture that asks for something (loop B). The supernatural side *responds*, and a token changes hands.
8. **Explanation and false relief (37–45%).** The viewer-proxy asks what happened. The narrator names a custom or rule and lists who does it, why, and the exact steps and count. This list becomes the checklist the mirror will hit. Add one hedge ("first time I've seen it").
9. **Lull (45–50%).** Resume, silence, the companion's face hidden from the narrator.
10. **Personal link (50–54%).** The companion quietly claims a connection to the dead or the entity (loop C). The narrator dismisses it with a logic check showing it is impossible for them to know.
11. **Possession signs to action spike (54–67%).** A small sound with a hidden cause, revealed seconds later (a safety device undone). Then a non-response, a sudden grab, and quick mechanical or sensory inserts (warning light, brake, tyres, cold air). The body ignores pain. Use "she acted like she didn't hear" twice.
12. **Mirror ritual (67–78%).** The companion repeats the stranger's movement and counted ritual beat for beat, and the world pauses as if waiting for them. Change one detail so it is worse than the first time (the entity itself hands over the token). This is the first direct glimpse of the entity (loop D).
13. **Wrong return (78–88%).** Short sentences, one action each. A posture that is too neat, total silence, an underlit face, a slow turn toward the narrator.
14. **False close and contradiction (88–97%).** The narrator asks directly and gets a plausible, sympathetic answer that hides a small inconsistency. Then the narrator recalls a private fact that contradicts it (loop E), and talks himself out of worrying (dramatic irony).
15. **Cliffhanger (97–100%).** A time jump, then the changed companion asks to take over the one thing keeping the narrator safe. The narrator agrees for kind reasons. Cut mid-thought on a callback to the earlier "safe option" line. The last shot repeats the opening shot. Label the video "Part 1".

**Rules for reuse:**
- Plant at least two unpaid specifics (a number or label) for Part 2.
- Close the early loops before 50%, and leave every loop opened after 50% open.
- The scariest image appears once, late (~78%), and is held still.
- The mirror's power comes from repeating the *movement* description almost exactly. Reuse your own wording within the episode, never the source's.
