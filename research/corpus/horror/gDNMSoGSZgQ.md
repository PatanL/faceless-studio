**Key mechanic:** a **folk rule stated in the first 3 s and broken by 0:10**, so the whole short is the punishment. A dying animal speaks a **cryptic prophecy aimed at the narrator** ("you'll be the last"), the elder explains the lore in three short lines, then gives a **second rule** (hide, stay silent) and **leaves**. The second half is pure waiting in a small dark space: protector gone, figure at the window, thuds on the door, then the **one voice the child trusts** (the father, who did the killing) calls him to open up. The kid breaks the silence rule and the video hard-cuts. The viewer is left with "is that really Dad?" and "what was the prophecy?" unanswered.

### How it ends
A **fake-authority lure at the door, then a hard cut on the rule-break**. The expected rescuer (Dad, set up at 0:41) is also the person the curse belongs to, so his voice is both relief and threat. The visual answers what the narration won't: the face at the door is drawn with a wide red grin, the same red used for the kill. The narrator obeys and the clip ends mid-sentence. Caveat for our use: the PLAYBOOK flags "cuts mid-sentence with no part promise" as a weakness on the control video; here it works because the cut lands exactly on the lure, but our version should end on a **complete** last line that leaves the same question open.

### Pacing and device notes for the PLAYBOOK
- **324 wpm**, in the translated-TikTok band (208–389). Hook faster (~390 wpm), body flat (75–85 words per 15 s).
- **Rule in 3 s, broken by 10 s.** The rest of the runtime is consequence.
- **Victim quotes the crime back:** the dying animal repeats the broken rule as a curse. A callback 12 s after the rule, in a new voice.
- **"You'll be the last"**: the curse is redirected from the guilty adult to the innocent narrator, which puts the viewer in danger.
- **Elder as lore engine:** three stair-step lore lines (5–7 words each) ending on the biggest noun, then a strange, specific instruction plus a protector spirit, then the elder **leaves**.
- **Hiding rule with two terms (stay hidden, no sound)**, each tested once: the scream is stifled (kept), the hiding is broken by the trusted voice (broken).
- **Protector named as the next arrival** ("I'll get your dad") so the impostor beat needs no setup at the end.
- **Waiting run of five short lines** (time, nothing, no return, numb, peek). It is the only slow stretch and it comes right before the threat appears.
- **Sound over sight at the climax**: the threat is a silhouette, then thuds, then a voice. Nothing is shown clearly.
- **Visual grammar (from the sheets):** greyscale everywhere; red used only for the kill silhouette and open mouths (fear, the grin); extreme close-ups on faces during waiting and fear beats; the threat appears through a window and a door, never in full.
