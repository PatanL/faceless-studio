- **Hook length:** the first bubble is 5 words in 1 s, and it carries the whole hook. The full cold-open block is 27 words over 13 s (22% of runtime). The first 3 s give command + refusal + "where are you", which is enough to set conflict, fear and a split family.
- **Re-hook interval:** a new piece of information or a turn arrives about every **3.5 s** on average (0:00, 0:05 things, 0:10 farewell, 0:13 time cut, 0:16 "something huge", 0:20 metal, 0:25 truck, 0:27 smoke, 0:29 "Jesus", 0:31 heading back, 0:35 30 min, 0:36 tractor, 0:39 Mom goes, 0:44 people hurt, 0:47 "what's out there", 0:50 slow ambulance, 0:56 tower, 0:57 end card). The longest gap is 0:39–0:44 (5 s), filled by Mom's long bubbles. The chat panel itself resets (scrolls clear) about every 8–9 s (0:09, 0:13, 0:23, 0:32, 0:39, 0:48), which works as a visual re-hook.
- **Open loops:**
  - L1 *What are "those things"?* Opens 0:05, re-touched at 0:16 ("something huge") and 0:47 ("what's out there"). **Not closed.**
  - L2 *Why does the kid refuse, and does he shoot?* Opens 0:01. Not closed.
  - L3 *How is Dad upstairs when he is 30+ min away with a broken tractor?* Opens at 0:04 vs 0:22/0:33. Not closed.
  - L4 *What crashed?* Opens 0:14, **closes** 0:25 (an overturned truck). This is the only loop Part 1 closes, and it leaves the sub-loop "hit something huge / what was it carrying" open.
  - L5 *Will Mom be okay?* Opens 0:39. Mom is missing from the flash-forward chat, which is a quiet dread signal. Not closed.
  - L6 *Will the cell tower work?* Opens 0:56 as a plant. Not closed.
  - Net result: 1 loop closed and 5 open at the end card.
- **Escalation ladder (present timeline):** a distant sound → a second witness → visual confirmation → smoke/fire risk → an emotional reaction that hints at more than the text says → help is far away (a number) → help is delayed (a broken machine) → a family member walks toward the danger → a warning is overruled → the outside rescue (ambulance) is also slow → a fragile lifeline is named. Each rung either takes away a safety net or moves a loved one closer to the threat. Above all of it sits the flash-forward ceiling (things breaking in, shoot them all), which the ladder never reaches in Part 1.
- **Pacing:** 245 wpm overall. The cold open is short, shouted all-caps bubbles of 1–5 words. The present timeline speeds up to about 290 wpm with longer bubbles (Mom's are 6–11 words). The final bubble is the longest in the video (17 words).
- **How it ends:** the video stops mid-conversation on a plant, not on a scare. It blurs the screen with a "Full Story" title and a big green arrow pointing down at the related-video/Part 2 link. There is no voice CTA. The cliffhanger works because of dramatic irony: the viewer knows the calm will end in "kill them all" and wants to see the bridge.

## 3. Transferable skeleton (no original wording)

For a **Part 1** chat-story Short of 55–65 s at about 240–290 wpm, one bubble every ~1 s, with the chat panel resetting every ~8 s.

1. **0–3% · Flash-forward first bubble.** The worst moment of the story: an all-caps imperative about an unnamed threat ("them"), sent by a trusted figure. 3–6 words.
2. **3–8% · Refusal + location question.** The phone owner refuses and asks where the other person is. The family is split before anything is explained.
3. **8–15% · Calm instructions in lowercase.** The trusted figure drops the volume: one-word address → "listen" → their position (plant a position that contradicts the present timeline) → a vague noun for the threat with an ellipsis → breach threat → restate the order with one word emphasized.
4. **15–22% · Plea + farewell-coded lines.** Fear statement, then 2–3 "last words" lines, ending on the order made bigger in scale. Hard cut.
5. **22–25% · Time-jump reset.** Switch to a different, mundane thread (a group chat with a cozy name). Roll-call the cast in 1–2 bubbles.
6. **25–36% · Sound anomaly, two witnesses.** One character asks if others heard something big; a second confirms from far away (distance = scale) and adds one sensory detail. Leave one vague size word that hints the cause is bigger than it seems.
7. **36–52% · Eyewitness report.** The character with a vantage point describes it: what it is → a danger sign → a hesitation fragment → a one-word exclamation suggesting unspoken horror → a flat assessment.
8. **52–66% · Isolation clock.** The rescuer sets off: far away → a hard number of minutes → "maybe more" → a mundane, setting-specific reason the vehicle or tool is unreliable.
9. **66–91% · Doomed good deed.** A third character, already close, decides to walk toward the danger for a sympathetic moral reason. The protagonist voices the viewer's worry ("is that safe / we don't know what's out there") and gets overruled kindly. Add one more slow outside rescue.
10. **91–97% · Resigned plant.** The protagonist gives in, repeats a warning, and names a fragile lifeline with a hyper-specific backstory detail (built by a family member, last year). Make it the longest bubble.
11. **97–100% · Blur + end card.** Freeze/blur the chat, show a big "full story" title with an arrow toward Part 2. No voice line.

**Rules of thumb pulled from this one:**
- Let the flash-forward set the ceiling, and keep the present-timeline ladder well below it for all of Part 1. The dread comes from the gap.
- Plant at least one contradiction between the flash-forward and the present (who is where, who is missing) so attentive viewers stay to solve it.
- Leave one cast member out of the flash-forward, then make that person the one who walks into danger.
- Never name the threat. Use 3 vague references that grow in ominousness (a thing, something huge, what's out there).
- Use one-word bubbles ("Okay", "Son", "Alright") as rhythm beats before each turn.
- Close at most one loop in Part 1 and leave 4–5 open.
- Switch case for tone: all-caps for panic, lowercase one-word lines for dread.

**For our kit (horror phone component):**
- Two thread modes with distinct headers: a 1:1 chat (photo avatar) and a group chat (group icon and name), with a hard switch between them as the time-jump transition.
- A per-sender mini avatar on grey bubbles in the group chat. Blue right bubbles for the owner. A clean panel reset (clear and restart from the top) every ~8 s instead of scrolling.
- One TTS voice per character (parent A, parent B, kid), with all-caps bubbles delivered louder and faster.
- A moving background layer behind the phone panel (Textify uses game footage; we need an original code-made equivalent: a slow parallax/procedural loop such as a dim farmhouse, a flickering field at night, or an abstract motion texture) so the frame never sits still.
- End card: blur + big title + animated down arrow, held for about 2 s.
