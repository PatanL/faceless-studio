### How it ends
A **voice-swap twist plus a threat to the viewer**. The victim's own thread speaks in the third person ("Ava can't...") in a calm voicemail-style register right after a run of screaming caps. The last line moves the target from the victim to the phone owner with "you" and an ellipsis. No resolution, no CTA; the open "will the police come / is Sarah next" questions drive comments and rewatches.

### Pacing and device notes for the PLAYBOOK
- **274 wpm** in very short bubbles (mean 4.9 words). This is the same tempo as the narrated TikTok reposts (~262 wpm) but delivered as dialogue, which is cheaper to produce (no characters to animate).
- **Two-voice ping-pong**: the victim reports, the stand-in asks or denies. The stand-in's lines are almost all questions ("What do you mean?"), reactions ("you're freaking me out") or denials ("you're imagining things"). These are the viewer's own thoughts, so the viewer never has to think them.
- **Warning → broken rule → punishment**: a common-sense warning (don't watch at night) is given twice and ignored, so the horror feels deserved.
- **Plant → callback**: a throwaway detail (a figure in the video, 0:13) returns 29 s later as the threat in the room (0:42).
- **Distance countdown** instead of a clock: corner, closer, next to the bed. Each rung is one short bubble.
- **The single image** is held back to 90% and used as proof. Everything before it is text only.
- **Silence beat**: two unanswered name checks ("Ava?", "AVA??") just before the reveal. In chat format, the gap *is* the scare.
- **Visual grammar (from the sheet):** 100% of tiles are the same chat panel over gameplay. The panel keeps 3–6 bubbles and resets every 6–8 s. The gameplay runs through biomes; the climax (0:37–0:54) happens to fall on the brightest warm desert/terracotta stretch, and the twist on green leaves. The gameplay is retention filler and does not track the story beats (likely not intentional). For our kit: chat panel in the upper half, the lower half a moving, non-narrative loop made in code.
