**Key mechanic.** The whole story is a **"forbidden ordinary phrase"**. A completely normal, polite question makes every listener react with terror or rage, and nobody explains why. In the original Chinese there is almost certainly a homophone or pun (the chat UI reads 我问别人想上几楼 / 可他却像是见了鬼一样), but in English the explanation is lost. That loss turns the story into pure surreal dread. The master loop (*what does the phrase mean?*) is **never closed**. Each new listener adds one fragmentary clue and raises the cost: a death wish, laws and regulations, location tracking, "loser", and finally the mother. The video ends on the mother disowning the narrator, with no answer.

### Hook
- **Length:** 1 narration line plus 1 dialogue line. That is 27 words in 4.1 s, or 21 words in 3.3 s for the first sentence alone.
- **Shape:** "I was [doing a mundane thing] when [a stranger with an odd object] looked at *me* like [I was the monster]." Then the innocent trigger line is spoken right away.
- **Why it works:** The usual horror roles are flipped in the first sentence: the narrator is what the other person fears. The phrase that drives the whole story is planted before the viewer knows it matters.

### Escalation ladder (who reacts, and how much harm)
1. Stranger with a tank: terror, flight, insult, avoidance (social)
2. Second stranger: slow stare, shriek, runs (confirms the rule)
3. Machine: censorship, then location tracking (institutional or state)
4. Friendly delivery rider: inhuman stare, then a beating (physical)
5. Own mother: silence, alarm, then "good for him", disowning (family, total exile)

The reactors grow *closer* to the narrator: stranger, stranger, machine, a "safe" stranger, family. The harm grows *deeper*: avoidance, threat, physical violence, emotional abandonment. Every rung uses the same trigger. Only the reactor and the cost change.

### How it ends
A hard cut on the trusted figure's betrayal line ("I don't have a kid like you"). The narrator is injured, alone, and still downstairs, and is cut off from the elevator, the police and family. The mystery gets no explanation. The video ends at its emotional low and its highest curiosity at the same moment. This is a classic Part-1 cliffhanger, and the unexplained pun works as comment bait.

| 0-3% | **Hook** | Open in the middle of a mundane routine. A stranger reacts to *the narrator* with fear, and the trigger act happens inside this window as dialogue. | Role inversion ("they were scared of me"); one odd prop or detail; trigger planted in under 5 s |
| 3-10% | Incident 1 | Reaction far out of proportion. The stranger flees and drops one cryptic, lethal-sounding warning. | Pattern interrupt (sudden flight); cryptic warning as clue 1 |
| 10-14% | False lead | The narrator guesses a rational cause, gets scared anyway, and makes a small escape. | Wrong hypothesis; bodily-fear cue |
| 14-22% | Aftermath 1 | Show the stranger's mundane cost and their costly avoidance, then a rejection line. | Mundane stakes; avoidance that costs effort (hints at a world-rule) |
| 22-25% | Investigation | One small "the world is off" detail, then narrow the mystery to the trigger itself. | Rhetorical questions; withheld conclusion split over 2 lines |
| 25-30% | **Test** | A new stranger arrives and the narrator deliberately repeats the trigger. A bigger reaction confirms the rule. | Callback; controlled experiment; slow-then-sudden reaction |
| 30-37% | **Oracle 1 (non-human authority)** | Ask a machine or official source. It refuses, then makes a worse, quieter demand (tracking, reporting, registration). | Format switch to a phone or UI screen; fake authority; surveillance threat at about the 60 s mark |
| 37-44% | Dread + self-ban | The narrator shuts the device off, restates the question, and avoids the location. | Body cue; restated loop; insisting how normal the act is |
| 44-46% | Forcing function | A small bodily or practical need forces a return to the dangerous place. | Number detail; ticking need |
| 46-55% | False safety | The kindest-seeming stranger yet. The narrator does them a favor and wraps the trigger in politeness. | Over-sold warmth; dramatic irony; rule-of-three third use |
| 55-61% | Turn | Warmth drains away at the exact trigger word, then an uncanny stare, then a pause. | Pinpoint timing; uncanny description; long pause before action |
| 61-74% | **Peak** | Physical consequence. The reactor's angry lines leak 1-2 partial clues about how the trigger is *heard*. The reactor also avoids the place. | Action chain; clue in dialogue; mirrored exit (callback) |
| 74-81% | Aftermath 2 | The narrator is hurt and states the obvious next step (police or help). The earlier surveillance clue blocks it, so they turn to family instead. | Plant-payoff callback; motif object (black screen) |
| 81-90% | **Oracle 2 (trusted human)** | The narrator retells the event, saying the trigger to the trusted person. Dead silence follows. | Dead-air beat; heartbeat cue; unanswered "hello" |
| 90-96% | Recognition | The trusted person clearly *knows* and is alarmed, and asks where the narrator is (echoing the tracking threat). The narrator begs for the answer. | Confirmation without explanation; the demand voices the viewer |
| 96-100% | **Kicker** | The trusted person sides with the attackers and cuts the narrator off completely. Hard cut. No explanation. | Betrayal reversal; final exile; master loop left open (Part-2 / comment bait) |

**Rules of this skeleton**
- One trigger, used **4-6 times**. Every use gets a *different reactor* and a *larger cost*. Order reactors from far to near: stranger, stranger, system, a "safe" stranger, family.
- Restate the master question in new words about every 20-30 s. A rhetorical question every 4-6 s keeps the inner monologue going.
- Hand out **one partial clue per reactor**. Each clue says how the trigger is *perceived* (deadly, illegal, insulting, a marker of a type of person) and never what it *means*.
- Keep one visual world-rule clue in the background (everyone else avoids the place) and pay it off twice with mirrored exits.
- Plant the institutional threat (tracking or reporting) once, then use it later to **block the obvious solution**.
- Alternate spikes with short releases (escape, black screen, sitting against the wall). The longest beat (about 13%) is the physical peak at about 61-74%.
- End on the lowest emotional point and the highest curiosity at once. The narrator's goal is unmet, every helper is gone, and the reason stays unrevealed.
- Pacing target: very dense narration (about 380-400 wpm TTS in this reference; slow it toward about 200-250 wpm for our own voice if it reads as rushed) with almost no silence, except one deliberate dead-air beat just before the final reversal.
- Our version needs its own original trigger and its own clue chain. Do not use an elevator-floor question, a propane tank, an AI censorship notice, a delivery rider or a mother disowning the narrator.
