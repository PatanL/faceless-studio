**Key mechanic:** the title promises a tiny time unit ("a nanosecond"), but the script never shows a nanosecond. ## 3. Transferable skeleton

Template for a VO-led "what if you spent [tiny time unit] in/at [extreme place or state]" explainer Short, about 120–155 s, ~160–170 wpm, second person. Structure only, no wording.

1. **Premise line (0–3%).** One sentence, 10–14 words, no lead-in: a promise frame plus "you", the tiny time unit and the extreme place. Write it so it can grammatically follow the final word of the script (for the loop).
2. **Define the place + promise broken rules (3–11%).** One pointing line naming the place and its single defining property (plant the property the climax will use). One line naming the key boundary/term and promising that ordinary intuition stops working there.
3. **Consequence 1: kill the escape hatch (11–17%).** Open with an ordinal. Call back to the title's tiny time unit and say right away that it doesn't save you. Follow with a short double-negation rhythm line.
4. **Contrast pair, round 1 (17–25%).** A "but" line from an outside observer's view (paradoxical), then a "but for you" line from the inside view (ordinary), ending on continued motion toward the threat.
5. **Explicit tease → body escalation (25–42%).** A short "here's where it gets [emotion]" line with no information. Understate the body threat, explain the mechanism through a comparison between two parts of your own body, then reveal the official term with an authority attribution. Raise to fatal, then one short deadpan joke to release tension.
6. **Loophole (42–55%).** Direct-address turn offering a way to survive the last beat, with one scale number. Explain the counter-intuitive rule behind it and hedge it ("might").
7. **Take the loophole back + no-exit analogies (55–72%).** A "but" line saying the loophole only delays things. Then two analogies in a row: one physical (an everyday force that pulls every way at once) and one mind-bending (the threat is like something everyone experiences and can't stop). Land the second in a parallel "just like you can't… you can't…" form.
8. **Contrast pair, round 2 (72–86%).** Name the second phenomenon. Repeat the outside-view line (callback to step 4), then flip it from the inside view into a spectacle line with a rule-of-three widening in scale and a compressed-time phrase that echoes the title unit.
9. **Endpoint + honest unknown (86–95%).** An inevitability connector, arrival at the final state, which closes the master loop and calls back to the step-2 "rules" promise. Then one line admitting nobody knows what's there, and why.
10. **Callback outro, cut mid-sentence (95–100%).** Repeat the title's time unit inside a comic piece of advice, and stop on a conjunction so the replay's first line finishes the sentence. No spoken CTA; put the comment question and links in the description.

Pacing targets: hook ≤ 4 s; first real payoff by ~11%; a turn ("but", "hey", tease, new term) every ≤ 15 s on average and never more than ~28 s without one (fill any long gap with analogies, not facts); ≥ 2 outside/inside contrast pairs; 3–4 named terms, each explained in plain words in the same breath; humour right after the darkest beat; master loop open ≥ 70% of runtime; title time unit called back at ~10% and ~95%.
