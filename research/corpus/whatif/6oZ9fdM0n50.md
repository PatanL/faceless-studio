## Transferable skeleton (no original wording)

For a "Can [tiny number] of [thing] really [huge outcome]?" what-if, ~6–7 min, ~140 wpm narrated, ~930 words.

1. **Cold open, 0–7 %** (~55–60 words, ~30 s, slowest pace). Second-person ordinary moment → 2–3 sensory details showing something is off → premise at maximum scale → one-line self-correction that leaves exactly the scarce resource → optional second character voice → stakes line contrasting the tiny resource with the enormous goal, with a light joke.
2. **Title card, ~2 %** (5–8 s, music only) restating the question.
3. **Optimistic setup, ~12 %**. Define the target number. Give the best-case real record, then an absurd extreme record with a sceptical aside. Make a "generous" assumption and run a simple multiplier ladder (A becomes B becomes C) to an apparent success with a time estimate. End on a one-word false resolution.
4. **Twist, ~7 %**. "Except" problem at a specific step. Short time-skip montage (3 rungs, one comic) up to the problem moment; slow reveal; a character reaction line.
5. **Mechanism, ~12 %** (fastest pace). Promise "here's why". Explain with one everyday analogy, one "almost everyone" fact, one cited statistic, normal-vs-our-case contrast, then a scary ratio.
6. **Historical proof, ~6 %**. "This has already happened" → one real case told in 4–5 short sentences ending in a dramatic finality line.
7. **Escalation in your world, ~15 %**. Return to "you". Add 3 stacking threats, each with its own number, signposted ("then there's…"), one ominous understatement, ending on a worst-case line and an emotional low point shown as a quiet time-skip scene with an either/or question (~60 % mark).
8. **Reversal, ~20 %**. "But" turn. 3–4 survival cases of rising scale and closeness to the viewer (animal with named individuals → second animal, faster → humans → the viewer's own ancestry), each with a before/after number and one honest caveat. Tease the last one with "you're proof".
9. **Verdict, ~8 %**. Restate the title question, give a short honest answer, call back to an earlier case, add one counter-intuitive insight, then the real-world number with a ratio vs "you". Close with a character joke.
10. **CTA, ~2 %**. A personal choice question to the viewer that calls back to the opening image.
11. **End screen, ~6 %**.

Pacing rules: a re-hook (question, "but/except" turn, ominous one-liner, or character interjection) every ~30 s; one long loop from ~5 % to ~85 % with 4–6 short loops (2–25 s) nested inside; every hard number on screen as a counter/date chip plus a sourced caption; one recurring home set used for open, verdict and CTA.
