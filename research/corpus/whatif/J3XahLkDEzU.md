**Key mechanic:** the video is a **time-skip survival diary**. Every chapter opens with a day or year stamp (spoken and shown on a flip sign), so the viewer always knows where they are and that the next chapter will be later and stranger. Inside each chapter the pattern repeats: a concrete second-person action, then a real-science or real-history aside that explains it (often with a dated anecdote), then a one-line deadpan joke to end the chapter. A dog found in chapter 3 gives the viewer an emotional anchor across the whole video. The ending flips from "scientists agree" to openly labelled speculation for the payoff.

## 3. Transferable skeleton

A template for a 9–12 min long-form second-person "what if you were the only/last X" survival-diary explainer. Structure only, no original wording.

1. **Cold open, mundane to impossible (0–4%).** Open in an ordinary queue or routine. Make the impossible event happen inside the first sentence through a counting device (position, number of people). Add 2–3 sensory traces that show the event *just* happened, plant one emotional object (an animal, a personal item), then a "hope" device (phone, radio) that fails. State the title premise as fact around 20–25 s, with a hedge that leaves room for doubt.
2. **Wish-fulfilment phase (4–9%).** 5–6 quick "you can now…" perks (free food, dream vehicle, luxury home with a specific number). Use a repeated tag phrase ("…for nobody") 2–3 times and save its third use for a twist of meaning.
3. **First collapse with a mechanism (9–19%).** Foreshadow line → plain-language metaphor of the system → a dated real precedent with numbers → an expert time estimate → a pre-empted obvious fear → the failure shown as a cascade. Turn the darkness into a wonder reveal using a short dated anecdote with a misdirect and a punchline.
4. **Second collapse, chapter-stamped (19–31%).** Spoken day stamp plus on-screen flip sign. Show 2–3 decline mechanisms (utility, smell, physical cost that calls back the perk's number). Reframe: "you have inherited everything, with expiry dates", then a rapid list of expiry rungs as a timeline graphic. Plant a body risk (a nutrient) and a tender delayed reward (an item saved for later).
5. **Rebuild 1, chapter-stamped (31–44%).** Choose a new home by an ancestral rule of three. Give one counter-intuitive "best tool" answer. The planted body risk arrives as a named condition, with a callback to the fun phase as its cause, fixed by a simple action. State that the real enemy is psychological ("not X, Y"). Pay off the cold-open object (the animal returns, with offspring), add a real "deadline" fact, and gain a companion with a withheld name revealed visually.
6. **Rebuild 2, chapter-stamped (44–56%).** A hedge in the chapter stamp ("probably") opens a time/sanity problem → dated expert anecdote with a date reveal → an ancient-method fix → exact correction. Then a two-word goal ("Next, X.") → a counter-intuitive fact about why the resource is unusable → the rule behind it → ironic one-liner → DIY fix with a comic specific cost → emotional payoff that calls back the first collapse (the first light/water/etc. you made yourself). **Midpoint = first self-made win.**
7. **Trial season, chapter-stamped (56–68%).** Environmental threat → decay mechanism (plant it for later) → myth-bust about what survives → turn from joke to real danger for "you" → reprise the psychological-enemy line → historical castaway precedent with an absurd true detail → a sad-funny parallel with the companion → a spectacle in the sky that falls because nobody maintains it → anniversary milestone (close the saved-reward loop) → "you're still here".
8. **Time-lapse montage (68–74%).** "Then the years stack up." One line per year, formula "Year N, [inventive build]". Include one gag, one pain beat, one withheld "we don't talk about it", and plant the device that will carry the finale (a transmitter or signal).
9. **Long-horizon chapter (74–86%).** Pay off the decay plant (nature retakes the city) → animals return with a real precedent number → the companion ages → the big project finished → cosmic-scale science that makes the project meaningful, ending on a planted gap or silence → small human ritual ("every evening, one small voice").
10. **Old-age chapter (86–94%).** Ageing gag → the companion's gentle death and heir (callback joke on the name) → bookend the opening location as transformed → misdirect ("the stadium is full") → nature punchline with a precedent → a fading-light metaphor with a half-life number → mortality stated plainly → persistence line echoing an earlier phrase.
11. **Speculation flag, then twist (94–99%).** Draw the line between consensus science and speculation out loud, then a precise day count, then the main loop is answered by the outside world. The explanation uses the planted science, and the irony is the scale mismatch (they came for billions and found one).
12. **Bathos button (last ~5 s).** End on the smallest domestic detail colliding with the cosmic event, then music out. No CTA; the last line is the share hook.

**Pacing targets:** ~140–150 wpm (start ~165, end ~100). Hook ≤ 30 s / ~75 words with the event inside the first sentence. A chapter stamp (spoken + flip sign) every 60–90 s. A real dated fact/anecdote with a reveal every 25–40 s. A deadpan one-liner every 40–60 s. 1–3-word punch lines at the end of most sub-beats. At least 3 long plants (> 3 min) paid off, plus a bookend callback in the last 90 s. Each beat type keeps its own visual mode (story set / cream explain board / chapter sign).
