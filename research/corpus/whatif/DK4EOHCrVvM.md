## 3. Transferable skeleton

A template for a long-form (~10–11 min, ~1,750 words at ~165 wpm) second-person "What if you could see [hidden quantity] above everyone's head?" story. Structure only, no wording.

1. **Cold open, mid-scene (0–7%).** Open on "you" doing a tiny everyday favor for a low-status stranger. Withhold the reveal one beat, then show the stranger's number at its most impossible: very large and exact to the last digit, read in full, then rounded and repeated. Contrast it with two or three concrete details of the stranger's situation. Turn the device on "you": show your modest number with a deadpan one-word reaction. State the central problem ("you don't know what it means") and add a flash-forward warning tied to a time frame. This opens the master loop (the stranger) and the personal loop (your number).
2. **Rewind and learn the rules (~12%).** Jump back to the morning the power appeared. Show it first on family (two numbers, one comic edge case such as a pet), then a relatable phone-search joke, then the street: a rapid list of strangers and numbers ending on a comic low one. Plant two contradictions (rich-looking person = zero, poor-looking person = huge) and let the viewer solve them a beat early. State rule #1 precisely, hedged with "if that's true".
3. **Selfish goal, Plan A (~10%).** "You" wants to profit. Two or three short interview scenes with high-number, unlikely people, each ending in a rule-of-three Q/A and a deadpan button. Plan A fails comically.
4. **Plan B and a false win (~12%).** A "what if instead…" pivot. A time-compressed search finds the biggest number of the whole video on an underdog. You join their project, a montage plays, and you check the number every morning. End on "still [number]. Perfect."
5. **The choice (~5%).** An outside offer arrives. Compare the numbers side by side so the viewer computes the choice too. "You" acts confidently on rule #1, then gives a sarcastic self-congratulation.
6. **Twist 1 and rule #2 (~6%).** "Or so you think." The number crashes on screen (before → after). Staccato disbelief lines. Rule #2 on a diagram: the number is a changeable prediction. Close with a guilt antithesis and an open question about what else you can change.
7. **Fall (~5%).** The relationship breaks, echoing the false-win line. Your own number drops at the recurring reflection set.
8. **Midpoint re-hook (~1–2%, at about 55–60%).** "Something doesn't make sense": return explicitly to the cold-open stranger.
9. **Humanize (~7%).** Name the stranger, give a fast-fall backstory, and introduce a lost relationship with a physical prop (a photo or similar). "You" stops caring about the quantity: the arc flip, at about 65%.
10. **Quest and pattern reveal (~6%).** A short montage of helping. Find the linked person, who carries the *identical* number. Reunite them using the prop, and hold a silence beat.
11. **Twist 2, rule #3, and the master loop closed (~9%).** "Something extraordinary happens": the stranger's number drops even though you did good. Rule #3: the number is the peak along the *current path*. Explain with a "not because X but because Y" chain, ending in a dark implication (the original number came from a tragic future that is now averted). Close on a bittersweet paradox line.
12. **Breather (~2%).** Check your own number at the reflection set, with a last self-deprecating joke.
13. **Climax and rule #4 (~8%).** "Something catches your eye": a confident, successful-looking person shows zero (a callback to the early zero contradiction). Cross-cut a converging physical danger. Rule #4 adds time/death to the meaning of the number. Shout, near-miss, silence, then the number jumps from zero, so the save is shown as a number.
14. **Theme (~3%).** "For the first time you realize…", then three parallel lines recapping the story's cases.
15. **Personal payoff (~4%).** "And you?" Your number starts ticking upward in the smallest unit, live on screen. A theme button that flips the quantity's own vocabulary into a values statement, ending on a one-word answer.
16. **CTA (~2%).** Echo the hook's "here's the problem" phrasing as "here's the question", then pose a binary moral dilemma that restates the title premise.

**Pacing targets:**
- Sentences: ~165 wpm, average under 7 words per sentence, frequent 1–3 word beats.
- Verbal re-hooks: a pivot phrase every ~30 s, never more than ~70 s without one, and fill long stretches with comic dialogue.
- Numbers on screen: a new or changed number about every 8 s, and the peak number by about 33% of runtime.
- Rules: four revisions spaced 2–3.5 min apart, each reinterpreting an earlier planted image.
- Loops: the master loop spans 75–80% of runtime; the personal-number thread is checked at every act break.
- Tone: deadpan comedy buttons in the first half only, sincerity in the second.
- Visuals: a diagram card on every rule change and a reflection set used three times.
