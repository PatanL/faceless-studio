**Key mechanic:** the what-if sets up a **world-sized empty stage**, and the story is about the few people who refuse to leave it. The video spends ~25% building the rule (everyone sleeps for 6 months; there are shelters, protests and a conspiracy streamer; the government counts down). Then a small crew who **dodged the rule with a secret workaround** (a serum that keeps them awake) wakes up in an empty world and robs it. The workaround is the hidden cost: the serum the conspiracy streamer feared ("they're going to put stuff in our bodies") is the thing that kills them, one by one, while the money piles up. The video closes by **repeating the boss's boast word for word** over a shot of one man alone in a room of useless cash as the world wakes up. Story beats are marked by **on-screen time cards** ("2 weeks before the awakening…", "The day before…") that count down to a deadline set in the first 12 seconds.

## 3. Transferable skeleton

A template for a 4–5 min long-form, story-led what-if sketch: "What if everyone had to [do the same thing at once for a set time]?" told as a short story about the few people who dodge it. Structure only, no original wording. For a narrated build (our explainer kit with TTS), keep the picture-led structure but add a narrator line at each act turn and cut the brand open to ≤3 s.

1. **Premise card (0–3%).** One title card that states the premise in a word or two. No narration, or one line at most. Get to the world within 3 s if the audience is new.
2. **World rules through comedy (3–15%).** 3–4 quick vignettes, ~5 s each, that make the strange rule look like a normal civic event: festive decoration, one small-talk line that states the duration (this sets the master clock), a sign that answers an obvious logistics question, and a protest that shows not everyone agrees.
3. **Dissent made personal + plant (15–21%).** A familiar character type comments on the rule (a streamer, a radio caller, a neighbour). Hide the story's real danger inside a joke line that sounds paranoid. Cut, with no explanation, to a small secret group gathered around a mysterious object and a plan board (opens the "what are they doing?" loop).
4. **Official start (21–28%).** An in-world official voice (broadcast, alert, siren) with a branded programme name and a polite slogan, plus one detail showing nobody is left to watch. Then one quiet shot of ordinary people under the rule.
5. **Misdirect and reveal (28–40%).** Show the secret group looking like they gave in too, hold ~10 s, then a 2-word reveal that they beat the rule with a workaround. Give a precise elapsed time to start the story clock, then have the leader state the goal as a grand, quotable boast. **Write the boast so it can be repeated word for word at the end with the opposite meaning.**
6. **Wish-fulfilment montage (40–65%).** Wordless or near-wordless: the empty or changed world (frozen city, nature taking over, an absurd animal ladder) cut together with the group's progress. Keep a visible pile that grows (loot, territory, whatever they're after). One planning line in genre talk to give the montage a shape. A new shot every 5–10 s; one gag at most.
7. **The cost turn (65–78%).** A single small symptom (a sound) ends the honeymoon. One member confesses the workaround has a cost and names a way out. Someone collapses on screen. The leader puts the goal above the group in a short, sarcastic greed speech. The group chooses to carry on (point of no return).
8. **Countdown fall (78–89%).** Two or three time cards counting down to the master deadline, each gap shorter than the one before. One loss per card, shown through aftermath (a burial, a body found), not the event. One accusing question exposes that the way out was a lie, and one line names the twist plainly. Then the survivor's 2–3-word hollow win.
9. **Deadline + bookend (89–99%).** The world goes back to normal in a location from act 1. The final picture puts the biggest pile next to the fewest people. Play the act-2 boast again word for word with no comment. Leave the survivor's fate open.
10. **End card (last ~1%).** A brand pun as the only CTA.

**Pacing targets:** ~60–80 wpm for a picture-led sketch (speech in 2 bursts, act 1 and act 3), or ~140–160 wpm if narrated. A hard visual change every ~10 s, never more than ~25 s on one shot type. A story turn every ~15–20 s (never more than ~35 s without one, and only inside a montage). Comedy buttons every ~15 s in act 1, none after the cost turn. Goal stated by ~35%, cost turn at ~65–70%, deadline reached at ~90%. Plant the danger in the first 15% as a joke, and pay it off in act 3 as the twist. Set the deadline in the first 5% and count it down on screen in the last 20%. For a cold audience, compress beats 1–4 into ~25–30 s (the original takes 80 s).
