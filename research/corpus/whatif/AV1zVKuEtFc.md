**Key mechanic:** the what-if is not narrated, it is **dramatised as a social conflict**. Earth undersells the Sun ("it would just get dark"), the Sun overhears and takes offence, and the science arrives as the Sun's threat (light delay: "you're seeing my past, I'm already gone"). The other planets then explain the real consequence while blaming Earth. The video never shows the Sun vanishing: it ends on a fake-out ("bluffing") followed by a sting that brings back a real long-term threat (red giant). Only ~3 facts are delivered, and only ~1/3 of the runtime is science; the conflict carries retention.

**Science check:** the ~8-minute light delay is right (~8.3 min). Gravity changes also travel at light speed, so the planets would keep orbiting for those ~8 minutes as well (consistent with "already gone"). Jupiter could not hold the inner planets together (they would leave on straight tangent lines); the line admits this ("don't know if it'll be enough"). Red giant is ~5 billion years away and is expected to swallow Mercury and Venus, Earth is uncertain, and Mars and Jupiter survive, so "consume you all" is comic exaggeration.

## Transferable skeleton (no original wording)

For a "What if [essential object/body] disappeared?" short as a **character skit** (personified objects, no narrator), ~75–85 s, ~160 wpm across the dialogue, ~200–220 words, 4–5 speaking characters.

1. **Idle question, 0–6 %** (~15 words, ≤5 s). Two side characters doing something ordinary together; one asks the title question in casual, filler-heavy speech.
2. **Underselling answer, 6–13 %** (~10 words). The other gives the obvious, too-small answer that reduces the subject to its most visible job.
3. **Subject overhears, 13–21 %** (~25 words). The personified subject slides in, offended and sarcastic about being reduced to that one job. A bystander signals "it heard you".
4. **Real fact as a threat, 21–36 %** (~35 words). The subject delivers one true counter-intuitive fact as menace (a hidden delay, a hidden dependency), adds a dismissive shrug word so it stays light, then a quiet "it's already happened" and a loud "you'll find out" on a full-screen colour shift.
5. **Split reaction, 36–43 %** (~18 words). One character apologises on the offender's behalf; the offender flatly refuses and dares the subject to go through with it (restating the title question as a dare).
6. **Explainer by a third character, 43–63 %** (~50 words). A new voice cuts in with an insult and a pun on the subject's name, then gives 2 consequences (one vivid comparison, one cause→effect rule), each over a 2–3 s minimal line-art diagram. A fourth, bigger character offers to fix it and admits it won't be enough.
7. **Blame + capitulation, 63–73 %** (~28 words). The scold blames the offender with an ironic contrast (we expected a different doom); the offender apologises directly to the subject.
8. **Silent menace hold, 73–76 %** (2–3 s, no words). Full-screen threat colour on the subject.
9. **Bluff reveal + warmth, 76–85 %** (~10 words). Short reversal, then a reassurance and an over-sweet promise.
10. **"Until" sting, 85–95 %** (~20 words). The subject calmly adds a real, far-future version of the same threat; the offenders ask a nervous question that nobody answers.
11. **End card, ~5 %**, silent.

Pacing rules: a new speaker or shot every ~4–5 s, no line over ~20 words; one bigger verbal re-hook (threat, insult, new character, reversal) every ≤7 s; at most 2 full-screen colour shifts, both on threat beats; science ≈ 1/3 of runtime and every fact is spoken by a character with a motive; the master loop ("will it really do it?") opens by ~15 % and closes at ~75 %, and the final loop stays open.
