**Key mechanic:** a **matched-frame day/night swap**. The premise ("humans sleep by day, live by night") is never said. It is shown by cutting between the same place, from the same camera angle, under two lights: at night it is busy and lit up; by day it is empty, shuttered or creepy. Each gag is one inversion of an everyday rule (rush hour, shopping hours, who is out at odd hours, nightclubs, the night shift). After ~45 s of wordless montage, the sketch ends on one short dialogue scene that inverts *language* instead of pictures (time words in everyday sayings flip), and calls back to the sunset from the opening time-lapse as something exotic.

## 3. Transferable skeleton

A template for a ~90 s 16:9 (or 9:16 crop) **wordless "If humans [one trait flipped]…" sketch**, built from code: each location is one background drawn once and rendered under two palettes (state A / state B). Structure only, no original wording, venue names or jokes.

1. **Series sting (0–9%).** Logo card plus a short sung or spoken series tag. The title/thumbnail carries the premise. Skip or cut the sting to ≤ 3 s for a new channel with no recognition.
2. **Premise by transformation, no words (9–16%).** A 4–6 s time-lapse or morph that shows the flipped trait switching on (light changes, scale changes, gravity changes). Include one throwaway image that will come back at the end as the emotional callback.
3. **World wakes up (16–25%).** A quiet establishing shot with one sight gag for attentive viewers (an animal or object that already lives the flipped way), then the first visible inversion of a routine (the household "starts its day" at the wrong moment).
4. **Inversion montage, 4–5 gags (25–76%).** Each gag is one everyday rule flipped, shown as **A-B-A**: the place in its new busy state → the same frame in the old state, empty or odd → back to A. Order: big public routine (commute) → commerce (shops) → a 4 s dark-ish micro-gag (who is "suspicious" now) → leisure with an **inverted sign or name** the viewer reads for the joke → work (the shift that is now "the odd shift"). Gags 6–14 s each, a cut every ~3–4 s, a same-frame swap every ~9 s. Allow at most one single spoken reaction mid-montage to break the music.
5. **Character payoff scene (76–98%).** Slow down. Enter one location from the montage (the last one), give one character a physical tell of the flipped trait's cost (tiredness, soreness, sunburn). Three short spoken lines that flip **everyday sayings** to fit the new world: the first is a coworker's observation, the second the character's proud-tired confession, the third, after a flashback that reuses the A/B swap, a sincere line that calls back to the throwaway image from step 2 (the thing that is ordinary to us is rare and beautiful to them). Button with a fourth line from someone else who brushes off the sincerity and casually says another flipped routine as if it were normal. Hold the awkward reaction for ~3 s; add nothing on top.
6. **End card (98–100%).** 2 s, a subscribe ask that puns on the series name. No recap, no question.

**Pacing targets:** ≤ 50 spoken words in total, ≥ 85% of them in the last quarter. A cut every ~3.5 s; a new gag every ~6–7 s; a same-frame A/B swap every ~9 s; never more than ~14 s on one gag. First visible inversion by ~20% (by ~10% if there is no brand sting). The callback image is planted in the first 15% and paid off in the last 20%. Dialogue at normal conversational speed (~130–140 wpm) with a held close-up between lines; three flipped sayings (rule of three), the middle one sincere.

**Build notes for our studio:** fits the code-only pipeline well (two palettes per SVG background, simple stick figures, no TTS except 4 short lines, music bed). It needs licensed or original music, because the music does all the pacing. Each location needs a night palette (navy, warm window glow, lamp cones) and a day palette (flat noon white or golden low sun) that is clearly the same place.
