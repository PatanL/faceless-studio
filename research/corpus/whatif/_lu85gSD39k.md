**Key mechanic:** this is a **stop-by-stop ladder on a log time scale**. There are 10 stops (10³ → 10⁹ years), and each one is a self-contained ~60 s mini-episode built from the same internal template. Two **rules set in the hook** ("it can never go back", a mute robot companion) and one **planted number** (day length, "keep that number in mind") run as threads through every stop. They all pay off in the stranded ending. The title number (1 billion) works as a countdown in reverse: the viewer knows exactly how many rungs remain, because the "stop anywhere" timeline shows all the stops at 0:10.

- **Hook length:** the premise question alone is 20 words in 7 s. The full cold open, through "Let's go", is **87 words in 34 s** (~153 wpm). Within that time it gives the premise (0:00), a constraint (0:07), a promise of repeated reveals (0:14), a destination tease (0:19), a companion and gag (0:24–0:29), and a launch prompt (0:32). The first stop card lands at 0:54.
- **Re-hook interval:** there are three layers.
  - A **stop card every ~66 s** (range 46–80 s). Each card is a big glowing number title, so the main rhythm is "new chapter every minute".
  - **In-stop pivots every ~15–25 s**: "And here's the strange part", "And there's another change", "But something is wrong", "In fact", "Down on the ground…", "Up in space…", "Here's a fact that's a little scary", "This stop is special", "Two more things", "And the sun has changed, too". Each stop carries 2–3 facts joined by these pivots.
  - A **robot gag roughly once per stop** (2:33, 5:15, 6:15, 8:07, 10:30, 11:43) for comic relief right after the heaviest fact.

## 3. Transferable skeleton

Template for a long-form (10–14 min, ~150 wpm, ~1,900–2,100 words) "what if you went N [units] into/through X" journey explainer. Structure only, no wording.

1. **Cold open, ≤35 s (~4%).**
   - Restate the title as a second-person action with one physical control and one huge number.
   - One irreversible rule (a one-way trip or no undo), stated within 10 s.
   - A promise of repeated reveals ("each stop shows something no one has seen").
   - A destination tease ("by the end, X won't be recognizable").
   - A mute or limited sidekick with a one-line gag rule.
   - A launch prompt.
   - Show the full route (all stops on a timeline or dial) in the first 15 s so progress is visible.
2. **Launch ritual, ~20 s (~2.5%).** Sound, then a visual time/space-lapse with an accelerating spoken rhythm, then a hard stop. Reuse a compressed version before every later stop.
3. **Ladder of 8–10 stops on a log scale (~70–75%), grouped into 3 acts of ~3 stops (~3–3.5 min each).**
   - Act 1: the familiar world, slightly off. Include one counter-intuitive "almost nothing changed" beat that teaches the core scale idea.
   - Act 2 opens with an explicit "this is where it gets weird" line at ~30% of runtime.
   - Act 3 reaches planetary or cosmic scale.
   - Stop lengths are 45–80 s, averaging ~65 s.
   - Each stop follows the per-stop template:
     1. A big number card.
     2. An arrival with one sensory impression.
     3. "You expect a familiar thing."
     4. "But it's gone or wrong."
     5. The mechanism, with one number and one everyday analogy.
     6. A pivot ("another change": sky ↔ ground ↔ space).
     7. A sidekick reaction.
     8. A motivated exit.
4. **Plant a tracker number in Act 1** with an explicit "remember this, it will grow" line. Advance it once per act (×3–4), then give its final value in the climax. A second tracker (a percentage) is optional in Act 3 to set up the climax.
5. **Open the master mystery early (~10% in)** with a question the journey can't answer (where are the people / who else is here). Touch it once in Act 3, then answer it at the end as unknowable plus hopeful speculation.
6. **Climax stop, ~70 s (~9%).**
   - Pays off the title number and the destination tease.
   - Slows the pace ("one last time", "slowly").
   - Mirrors the first stop's list of absences.
   - Ends on a perspective flip: the familiar present was a brief moment.
7. **Stranding twist, ~10 s.** The hook's irreversible rule closes and the sidekick gag turns tender.
8. **Recap montage, ~25–30 s.** List every stop by its 2–3-word nickname over a growing thumbnail grid.
9. **Moral, ~20–35 s.** Turn the bleak destination into gratitude for the present, using one quotable thesis line plus a callback list of 3–4 items from the stops.
10. **CTA, ~10–15 s.** Ask "which stop would you choose?", where the options are the stops already shown, then a series sign-off. Return to the opening location visually.

Pacing targets:
- ~150–155 wpm, flat from start to finish.
- A stop card every ~60–70 s.
- An in-stop pivot phrase every 15–25 s.
- A sidekick beat once per stop.
- At least one open loop spanning 90%+ of runtime (the irreversible rule).
- A tracker number visible across 3+ stops.
- Micro-loops (expectation → "but") closed within 30 s.
- Hedged authority ("some scientists think / studies suggest") on speculative claims.
- One everyday analogy per stop.
- A recap plus a moral plus a choice-CTA as the ending stack.
