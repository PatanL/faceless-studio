## Transferable skeleton (no original wording)

For a "What if you had [one superpower]?" story-explainer, ~10 min, ~180 wpm, ~1,800 words, second person throughout, ending as episode 1 of a series.

1. **Cold open, 0–6 %** (~90–100 words, ~35 s). Ordinary "you" moment → a tiny accident triggers the power involuntarily → effect widens in 3 rungs (object → person → whole place) → you are the sole exception → a tactile test with a simile → an innocent small fix → release → a bystander resumes mid-sentence and half-notices → you deflect in two words → title question.
2. **Grounding science, ~8 %** (fast). "Here's how it works" → 3 real-world cases climbing in scale (your body → technology/physics with one precise number and an "it would break your phone" consequence → an animal), each closed with an everyday payoff.
3. **Problems, ~5 %**. "But you don't just do X, you do Y" → "you'd have a problem — actually N" → numbered physical problems, worst last, ending on a claustrophobic image.
4. **Rules, ~12 %**. "So it must have rules" → 3 numbered on-screen rules: rule 1 fixes the problems, rule 2 defines interaction and gets a visual-gag test plus a funny misfire with an outsider punchline, rule 3 is a hidden personal cost that "you" dismiss with a one-word false resolution, then a test that ends on a mild physical cost.
5. **Share the secret, ~3 %**. Show one friend; friend names a pop-culture equivalent; short comic exchange.
6. **Wish-fulfilment montage, ~12 %**. "At first…" → 8–10 rapid uses climbing from harmless to mildly unethical; include one gross-out with a direct "don't" to the viewer, one "nobody said you were a hero" aside, one unseen observer line, one habit stressed as "every day" (feeds rule 3), one physical sports gag, one pure kindness tagged as "the best thing you ever did", one running prank with a family member.
7. **Wonder and loneliness, ~5 %**. "But" tone shift → sensory absences → 3–4 surreal places → superlative loneliness line; slow the pace.
8. **Cost comes due, ~6 %**. Call back rule 3 → small daily amount → yearly total → a family member notices → a nested "what if it went wrong forever?" left unanswered.
9. **Turning point, ~5 %**. "Then one day" humiliation → "for the first time" you use it for revenge → it works → narrator admits it feels good.
10. **Slippery slope, ~10 %**. Triad of "nobody can see / stop / know" → 3-rung theft ladder of rising value → identity correction (from wanting to taking) → one mechanism that defeats evidence → rationalisation lines → costume motif → friend asks directly, you lie, friend's look lingers.
11. **Climax temptation, ~8 %**. Big target with a schedule → you freeze it → you discover someone in danger in the same frame → explicit callback to the earlier kindness → you choose wrong → consequence heard off-screen.
12. **Fallout, ~6 %**. News report as outside authority → you are on camera but faceless → "should feel bad / feel untouchable" → friend asks outright → one-word lie → silence.
13. **Cliffhanger, ~13 %**. Cut to someone else watching → they arrive as a group of powered characters from other episodes (each identified by one prop; one named via a sibling video) → banter → you flex the power and walk among them, calling back your invincibility line → the hook's core rule is broken by a new figure → one-line threat.
14. **CTA, ~1–2 %**. Title question + binary "join or fight" question about the series' organisation + "only the beginning".
15. **End card, ~2 %**. Logo + "to be continued".

Pacing rules: re-hook every ~15–20 s (numbered list, "but" pivot, one-word false resolution, "then one day", tease line, or character cut-in); a character voice every 30–60 s; plant 2–3 items in the first third and pay them off in the last third (a hidden cost rule, a kindness, an unseen observer); keep one loop open for the sequel; every rule and key number on screen as a chip or icon panel; switch from day to night palette at the moral turn.
