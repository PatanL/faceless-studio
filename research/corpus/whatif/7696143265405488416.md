## Transferable skeleton

A template for an ~80 s, 9:16, caption-led "constant changes for N seconds" short. No original wording. Timings are % of runtime.

1. **0–10%: Premise card plus pre-event countdown.** A title asks a what-if that changes one well-known quantity for a short, exact time. A calm, ordinary scene is shown and seeded with 4 to 6 props that will each fail later. The HUD counts down to the event. Nothing breaks yet.
2. **10%: Trigger.** A flash or hard cut. The HUD flips from countdown to an elapsed counter counting up, with a chapter label and a slow-motion note that explains the runtime. Show something breaking in the viewer's own hands first.
3. **11–25%: The one rule, stated as a number.** The first caption gives the change as a single percentage. The next two captions turn it into (a) a shape change you can picture and (b) the mirrored percentage. Everything after this follows from the rule.
4. **25–42%: Chapter 2, then street-scale consequences.** A new chapter label re-hooks. Three one-line consequences, each on a familiar object or structure that only works because of the quantity that changed. The last line is an absolute statement ("nothing that does X can…").
5. **42–65%: Chapter 3, jump to the sky.** Hard cut to the biggest visible object. One line on how it deforms. Then **one second-person line about the viewer's own body** (around 50%). Then a line that sums up the whole planet's motion.
6. **65–78%: Chapter 4, the planet.** The HUD shows the exact planetary figure and the caption rounds it. Convert it right away to the place on screen (an exact, odd local number). Climax line: an understated "it has to go somewhere", followed by the visual of exactly that.
7. **78–82%: Timer ends.** The counter reaches its target and the quantity shows its normal value again.
8. **82–90%: Twist.** One line saying that the reset does not undo the damage. Show it from the viewer's own first-person position, with damage spreading across the view.
9. **90–100%: Black, then end card.** Fade to black (about 2 s). Repeat the title. Add one small-caps line with a real, checkable historical fact connected to the premise. Hold it until the loop point. The post description asks a "which would happen first near you?" question.

**Pacing targets:** about 13 captions of 5 to 8 words. A new caption every 4 to 5 s. A chapter label every 10 to 18 s (4 chapters). About 60 to 80 wpm of caption text. Every number derives from one ratio, so show the working in a fact file. Exactly one second-person line, at the midpoint.
