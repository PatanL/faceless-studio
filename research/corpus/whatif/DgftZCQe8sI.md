**Key mechanic:** a **countdown timeline from hour 1 to after your death**, told as a survival guide in the future tense. Each chapter is a time stamp (spoken *and* a lower-third card) plus one named system that fails (power, water, fresh food, animals, sanity, nuclear, internet, orbit, cars, vitamins, medicine, buildings). Inside a chapter the pattern is: stamp → a real mechanism in plain words (with a number) → what it means for *you* → a practical "rule"/fix. Unlike the same-premise J3XahLkDEzU it has almost no jokes and no recurring plants beyond the dog; it runs on **information density + steady dread**, and it goes one step further than J3Xah: **the viewer dies on screen** at ~89%, and the video ends on an emotional "message carved in stone" and a comment prompt instead of a twist.

## 3. Transferable skeleton

A template for a 11–13 min long-form second-person "what if you were the last/only X" timeline explainer that ends with the viewer's own death and a comment prompt. Structure only, no original wording.

1. **Wake-up cold open (0–4%).** Start with an ordinary first action of the day. Show the absence through 2–3 missing everyday signals (device, street sounds, a "no X, no Y, no Z" list), then one warm trace that proves the event just happened. Escalate "here" → "everywhere", then state the event with one big number and the title premise by ~30 s.
2. **Question stack + format promise (4–6%).** Three questions (what happens to you, how long you last, what happens to the world), then a one-line promise of the structure ("unit by unit, from the first hour to the end"). These become the master loops for the climax and the deep-time ending.
3. **Hour 1: disbelief (6–10%).** Stamp card. The failed contact attempts (list of 3 escalating to emergency services), the frozen digital world described with one metaphor. Plant the digital world as a thread to close later. End on a tease that the first real problem is somewhere unseen.
4. **First system collapse (10–21%).** Name the system with a superlative, explain how people keep it balanced, state the time frame for failure, the safety cut-outs, the fuel supply problem, then the failure as a set piece the viewer watches. Turn the dark into a wonder reveal. Add a nuance beat ("not everything stops at once") with 2 exceptions and their time ranges, then a numbered survival rule as a quotable imperative.
5. **Domino chapter (21–26%).** Stamp card. "Once A goes, B goes too": mechanism, a 3-step decline in one sentence, a survival-time number shown as bars, a short-term fix, a permanent fix (list of 3), and a "small thing can now kill you" plant.
6. **Delayed fantasy, then rot (26–33%).** Stamp card with a superlative subtitle. A 3-line "every X is yours" anaphora that plants one luxury object, cut off within ~20 s by "but" and a sensory decay list with expiry times. Close with a rule about what lasts and one surprising long-lasting item.
7. **Living things and a companion (33–42%).** A sound tease opens the chapter. Pets, then farm animals with escalating numbers, then the threat they become. Turn the threat into the decision to take in one animal; a 3-line "it will…" benefit list whose last item is emotional; end on "the real enemy hasn't arrived yet".
8. **The mind (42–49%).** Stamp card. Social-brain explanation, real isolation research with an escalating symptom list, a "cell the size of X with no release date" type metaphor, three "you'll…" coping behaviours (plant one that the ending pays off), a parallel thesis line and a routine as the fix.
9. **The slow hidden threat (49–57%).** "While you're busy with X, Y is growing." Big count of the facility type, the reassurance (they shut down), the short "that part works" turn, the less-known real problem, its mechanism and time range, a calibrated "it won't X, but Y" and one practical rule.
10. **Lost technology chapter (57–64%).** Stamp card. The network goes dark (an "every…" list of what is now locked away, closing the hour-1 plant), then a sky spectacle that falls because nobody maintains it, framed as a lucky sighting and a poignant "coming home" line.
11. **Transport dies (64–68%).** Stamp card. Pay off the luxury object: mechanism, "even X goes bad", symbol → object reframe, then a 3-step regression ladder to older ways of moving.
12. **Year 1, the body and the first win (68–77%).** Stamp card plus a visible ageing cue on the hero. Pay off the rot plant as a named deficiency with a 3-symptom ladder (strangest last) and a historical precedent. Q/A "the solution?", an identity shift, resources "waiting" in shops and libraries, fail-and-retry, then one small home-grown triumph as the midpoint win. Cut with "meanwhile" to a 3-image nature-returning list ending on a twist clause.
13. **Year 5, the body betrays you (77–82%).** "The biggest threat isn't A, B or C" (a recap of earlier threats) → "it's you". Three ordinary injuries, a doctor-could-fix-it contrast, a fading-supply bar, "no X, no Y, no Z", "your own A, B, C", and a quiet line about doing everything more carefully as you age.
14. **Years 10–30, the world decays (82–90%).** Stamp cards. The decay mechanism as a 3-step process board, an infrastructure list that calls back an earlier system (pumps → flooded tunnels), then the reveal that the city is now a forest with a sound swap that echoes the silent opening. Fold the companion's death into one clause and name the hero "the last witness".
15. **Your death and deep time (90–95%).** The viewer dies in the second person, gently, closing "how long could you survive". Three "no one will…" lines (plant the last one), the planet keeps going, the time scale jumps to centuries, and a list of what remains that ends on the most poetic off-world item.
16. **The final act + epitaph (95–97%).** "Maybe, just maybe, one last thing": the hero leaves a message for whoever comes next; 4–5 two-word declaratives on screen, ending with a callback to the title.
17. **Comment CTA (97–100%).** Narrator switches to "I" for the first time and asks the viewer to answer the epitaph question in the comments, then subscribe with a "next time, an even bigger question" tease on the end card.

**Pacing targets:** ~150 wpm, steady. Hook ≤ 35 s / ~90 words to the stated premise (≤ 50 s with the question stack and format promise). A time stamp (spoken + lower-third card with a subtitle) every ~50 s, with time steps that roughly double (hour, day, week, month, year, 5, 10, 30…). A tease line at most chapter seams (~every 60 s) that the next stamp answers. A real fact with a number every 20–30 s, each with its own explain board. 2–4-word punch lines closing most sub-beats. At least 4 plants of 4–7 min (luxury object, food decay, small infection, companion, digital world) closed before the death beat. The viewer's death at ~88–90%, then deep time, an epitaph and a comment prompt in the last ~6%.
