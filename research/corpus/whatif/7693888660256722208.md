Key mechanic: **selective deletion**. Only one element vanishes, so every shot is a "what is made of it, and what isn't" puzzle: the tower goes but its paint and the people stay, the bus goes but its passengers stay, the plane loses its bolts, your blood loses its oxygen carrier, the planet loses its core. The clocks are the series template (pre-event countdown, then a slowed "returns in" clock: 3 s of story x15 = ~46 s of runtime, 0:08 to 0:54). The new twist relative to the neutrons/π entries: **the worst damage comes after the element returns**. A third clock (shockwave) starts only once everything looks normal again.

Every number is checkable (tower ~7,300 t, paint ~60 t, core ~85% iron and ~6,970 km wide vs Mars ~6,780 km, iron ~32% of Earth's mass, ~4 g in a human body), which is what makes the absurd premise read as science.

## Transferable skeleton

No original wording. Times assume a 60-90 s short; scale proportionally.

1. **0-10% Premise + pre-event clock.** Title card: "What if [one ingredient of the world] vanished for [a few] seconds?" with the keyword in its own colour. HUD countdown to the event from frame 1, plus one quiet fact line about how much of the planet/body is that ingredient. Open on a calm, famous real place dominated by a large object made of the ingredient; first caption puts "you" at a named spot facing it.
2. **10-25% Event + landmark consequence.** Clock hits zero and resets as a "comes back in" clock with a slow-motion multiplier (story seconds x multiplier ≈ 55-60% of runtime). Show the effect before explaining it. Caption the landmark's mass in a number; HUD subline names the odd leftover (the part not made of the ingredient). Then the human stakes: who was on or in it, with a height or distance figure.
3. **25-40% Selective-deletion gags, moving closer.** Three quick rungs, each one line: an everyday object goes but the people in it stay (state the contrast as a two-clause punchline), a threat appears overhead, then it reaches your own body (POV shot of your hands). Give each a two-to-four-word HUD "why" label and start a personal countdown for the body rung.
4. **40-55% Scale jump to the planet.** One line: the planet's interior is mostly the ingredient, so it's gone too. HUD shows a personal stat that follows from it (your weight, your air, your day length). Pay off a small object planted in the opening shot to show the change. Pull back to space and cut to a cut-away diagram; caption the size of the gap compared with a famous body, tied to "under you".
5. **55-68% Wordless planetary stretch.** 10-12 s with no captions. HUD subline swaps every ~5-6 s with a speed number and a force/pressure number.
6. **68-70% Return.** Clock hits zero with a flash and an "it's back" HUD state. One caption says the return itself is a collision.
7. **70-76% False calm + delayed clock.** Hard cut back to the opening shot; everything looks normal. One HUD line notes a small permanent change (the leftover from step 2 did not return). Then a new countdown for the delayed consequence, shown as jumping time-skip values so minutes pass in a few seconds.
8. **76-90% Delayed payoff.** Countdown at zero with a superlative comparison ("bigger than any [record]"); caption with a "[N] minutes later, [effect] reaches [opening place]" stamp; 7-10 s of wordless destruction of the landmark that survived step 2.
9. **90-92% Black**, 1-2 s.
10. **92-100% Bookend card.** Repeat the title with the coloured keyword, plus a two-line fact: one planet-scale share, one "you carry N of it" personal-scale figure. Hold ~6 s for the loop. Put the "which one next?" ask in the post caption.
