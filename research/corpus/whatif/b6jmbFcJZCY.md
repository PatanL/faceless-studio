### Hook
- **Length:** 49 s, 119 words (6.7% of runtime). The premise itself lands at 0:13 ("everyone is gone") after 29 words; the rules line at 0:16 brings it to ~43 words / 20 s. The remaining 29 s are a **trailer**: three dated consequences (hours / 2 days / a week), each a specific promise, then one undated mystery.
- Layers: (1) a true "right now" fact with a big number, (2) the narrator performs the trigger ("watch what happens when I snap"), (3) rules that remove objections (no war, no virus, no bodies), (4) two mundane sensory details (coffee, cars), (5) a callback to the opening number with the new threat, (6) a trailer of dated payoffs, (7) a master mystery, (8) "Let's start the clock."
- Visual hook: a bright cartoon sky with planes, then a data-map with the big number as a title, then an orange countdown timer on screen at 0:37 before the narration explains it.

### Escalation ladder
- **Time ladder (the spine):** 10 s → 3 h → Day 1 → Day 2 → Day 3 → Day 7 → weeks → Year 1 → 10 → 50 → 100 → 300 → 1,000 → 1,000,000 → (records last) 1,000,000,000 years.
- **What-fails ladder (acts 1–2):** moving things (cars, planes) → systems (grid) → cities (flooding) → living things (pets, livestock) → a worldwide nuclear disaster → whole neighborhoods (fire) → roads (ice) → your house → space hardware → famous bridges → skyscrapers/whole cities → dams/rivers.
- **What-lasts ladder (act 3), shrinking in size and growing in time:** stone monuments → carved faces (7 M yrs) → a rock layer as thick as your hand (1 M yrs) → footprints on the Moon → a record carrying a child's laugh (1 B yrs). The object gets smaller and more human as the time gets bigger; the last item is the most emotional.
- **Tone ladder:** awe → dread (nuclear) → surprise relief (Chernobyl) → comedy (rats, lice, cats) → spectacle (landmarks fall) → wonder (pyramids, Moon, Voyager) → meaning (thesis).

### How it ends
Master-loop payoff (Voyager record, 10:34) → explicit bookend "back to the snap" → 5-item recap of the time ladder with an on-screen icon timeline → thesis twist that reframes the whole video (permanence is maintenance; "it's a job"), using three callbacks (bridges painted, tunnels pumped, lights kept on) → uplifting turn ("lasts as long as we keep showing up") → personal comment question tied to the theme (pick one thing to survive) → subscribe ask framed as an identity ("you're clearly curious") + "it's free". No sponsor, no mid-roll ask, no end-screen narration.

## Transferable skeleton

A long-form (10–12 min, ~145–150 wpm, ~1,700–1,800 words) "what if [everything X] stopped right now" timeline. No wording from the source.

1. **0–7% Hook (≈45–50 s, ≈110–120 words).**
   - a. A true present-tense fact with a big number about this exact moment ("right now, N [things] are doing Y").
   - b. Narrator performs the trigger as an action the viewer watches (one gesture = time-zero).
   - c. One-line premise, then a 3-part "rules" line that removes the obvious objections.
   - d. Two tiny mundane sensory details still in motion, then a callback to the opening number with the new danger.
   - e. **Trailer:** three dated consequences with rising stakes (hours / days / a week), the last one with an on-screen countdown.
   - f. **Master mystery:** an undated, surprising final answer withheld ("the last thing that... isn't where you think").
   - g. "Start the clock" line; the HUD clock appears.
2. **7–40% Act 1, the first week (5–6 chapters, 30–90 s each).** One chapter per time stamp. Pay trailer item 1 within ~30 s of the hook. Each chapter: time stamp → mechanism the viewer didn't know (cross-section diagram) → one big number → global widening (list of named cities) → one-line ironic button. Insert one emotional chapter (animals/people-adjacent). Before the countdown chapter, tease it with an icon only. Pay the countdown with an explicit "this is the countdown from the start" callback.
3. **~40% Twist chapter (≈30 s).** "Here's the strange part": a counter-intuitive outcome backed by one real-world precedent; end on an aphorism that reframes humans.
4. **40–68% Act 2, speed up (years → centuries).** Say "let's speed up". Make one beat about *your* house ("the house you're in right now"). Insert a "look up" pattern interrupt. Add a two-list comic segment (losers / winners, Q→A, ending with a repeated punchline). Plant the thesis keyword in a mechanism (something that only survives because someone maintains it). Knock down famous landmarks in split screens. Bust one "permanent" myth with a two-word negation.
5. **68–80% Act 3, what lasts (millennia → millions).** "So here's the question: what's still standing?" Answer counter-intuitively (oldest, not newest). Ladder of survivors from big monuments to one rate-based number (X lasts N million years). Tease "something lasts longer, and it's less impressive", reveal a human trace measured against your body.
6. **80–88% Master reveal.** Explicit callback ("I told you..."). Two-stage reveal: first answer, then "even that isn't the last thing", ending on the most human, emotional detail and the biggest number in the video.
7. **88–97% Bookend + thesis.** "Back to time-zero." Rapid recap of 4–5 milestones as an icon timeline. Reframe the video's meaning using 3 callbacks to earlier mechanisms (anaphora). Two-word thesis punch. Uplifting turn to the viewer's agency.
8. **97–100% CTA.** A personal, on-theme pick-one question for comments; subscribe ask framed as an identity compliment + "it's free".

**Pacing targets from this reference:** 147 wpm; hook ≤ 50 s with the premise stated by ~15 s; time stamp or tease at least every 25–35 s; chapters 20–90 s; ~4 hook loops paid in order of length with the first paid ~25 s after the hook ends (1:14 here, 10% of runtime) and the master loop held to ~85–90%; 33% of runtime on the first week, year 1 by ~43%, logarithmic time jumps after that; thesis + CTA ≈ 12% of runtime.

**Visual system to build once and reuse:** top-left "time since [event]" HUD (clock → DAY n → YEAR n), bottom milestone scrubber, dark data-globe with glowing dots, cross-section diagram kit, spaced small-caps keyword titles, split screen, stick-figure narrator reserved for question/thesis/CTA beats, end recap icon timeline.
