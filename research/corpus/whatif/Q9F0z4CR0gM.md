**Transcript caveats (auto-sub errors):** "Behringer/Beher" = **Barringer** (Meteor Crater); "Chickixaloo/Chickixaloop" = **Chicxulub**; "Omuam Mua / AmuA Mua / Muam Mua / Oh, Mua Mua" = **ʻOumuamua**; "Y4" = **YR4** (2024 YR4); "Tungusa" = Tunguska; "Demorphos" = Dimorphos; "K218b" = K2-18b; "Paleoarchian" = Paleoarchean; "aid expanse" = arid expanse; "ground.news/wiff" = ground.news/wif (per description). Unit slips: "9 m a second" = 9 mi/s; "6 m in diameter" = 6 mi; "2 and 1/2 m across" = 2.5 mi; "6 milesi" = 6 miles. Fragments split across caption lines ("time.", "could see.", "of a desert.", "stage.") are merged into the line before them below.

**Fact notes:** most figures are in the published range but the VO consistently picks the **top of the range** for drama: YR4 at ~60 m and "15 Mt" (published energy estimates are roughly 8 Mt); S2 at "200 times" Chicxulub's mass (published 50–200×); the YR4 3.1% peak and the later drop to ~0.001–0.004% are correct, as is the lunar-impact possibility. ʻOumuamua's "1 gigaton" and 4 km crater are the video's own assumptions (no size is given for the rock itself, only its 10:1 shape). The Hiroshima comparison (15 Mt ≈ 1,000 × 15 kt) checks out.

**Key mechanic:** the title promises five impacts **at once**, but the script is really **five self-contained "rock cards" told in ascending order of deadliness**, glued together by "while X is still burning, Y is about to hit [new region]" bridges that keep up the simultaneity fiction. Each card runs the same loop: identity (name, when/how found) → size with an imperial conversion and a familiar object → historical event or impact odds → "what if it hit today" relocation → impact cascade told partly in second person (flash → blast → shock wave → you on the ground → wider region → aftermath) → a **verdict line on a running extinction scale** ("not world-ending, but…" → "not dinosaur level yet, but getting there" → "humanity wouldn't either" → "you are not surviving this one"). The "all at once" combination itself is paid off in only **19 s (22:37–22:56)**: the title is a container; the ladder is the show. A dinosaur cold open (0:06) is the planted loop that the 4th card pays off, and the promise of "a rock much bigger than the dinosaur killer" (1:04) is held for ~18 min until the 5th card.

## 3. Transferable skeleton

A template for a long-form (~20–24 min, ~2,800 content words at ~135–140 wpm, plus an optional ~2.5 min sponsor) "What if every [dangerous thing of one kind] hit/happened at once?" countdown-simulation. Structure only, no wording.

1. **Cold open on the famous benchmark (0–3%, ~40 s, ~80 words).** Open on the single best-known real instance of the threat with its date, withholding the victim's name for one beat and revealing it in one or two words. Transpose it to today in one line ("if this happened now, [we] would be at risk"). Then state the premise: a countable list (N worst instances, past and future) all combined at once. End with a joking fake spoiler that gives away the outcome, so the question becomes *how bad*.
2. **Title sting (~1%).**
3. **Roadmap + primer (~4%).** Promise ascending order: start with the smallest, end with one far bigger than the famous benchmark (open the long size loop). Delay one beat to teach a baseline fact (this thing happens all the time at tiny scale; here's what normally protects us), with one reassurance line addressed to the viewer and then a turn back to the danger.
4. **Unit card ×N, in ascending deadliness (each ~12–21%; N = 5 fills ~20 min).** Use the same sub-beat loop in every card:
   - a. **Bridge** from the last card that keeps the simultaneity fiction: [last unit] still producing [visible aftermath] in [region] while [next unit] approaches [new region].
   - b. **Identity:** name card plus how and when it was found or happened (a real date and a real instrument or mission).
   - c. **Size:** metric number + imperial conversion + one familiar object or landmark.
   - d. **History or odds:** either a narrator-guided time jump into the past with a short historic cascade, or the real probability sequence (alarming → revised down → tiny but nonzero).
   - e. **"What if it happened today?"** State the simulation rule (where it lands and why), and openly choose the more spectacular target when realism allows either.
   - f. **Impact cascade** in fixed stages: flash/first sign → main event + energy in a familiar unit (bomb multiples) → shock or wave → **a second-person slot** ([viewer placed outside / in the nearest real town]) → widening radius with distance stamps and named places → aftermath with time stamps (seconds → days → weeks → months → years).
   - g. **Verdict on a running scorecard** ([not world-level] + [but this-level]), stepping up one level per card.
   - h. **One unique twist per card** so no two are alike: a different mechanism (no crater vs crater), a real mitigation aside (a planetary-defense type fact, myth-busted), an unsolved mystery with three hypotheses from mundane to wild left open, or a counterintuitive "this was actually good for [early life]" reversal.
   - i. **One quip per card minimum** (a personification, a comic epithet for the unit, a sarcastic button).
5. **Sponsor slot (optional, ~10%, starting ~20%).** Place it right after card 1's verdict and the cliffhanger bridge to card 2. Segue through an in-world device (e.g. two contrasting fictional headlines about the event just shown), and exit with a one-line pun back into the next card.
6. **Ladder check (~at 65%).** Before the benchmark card, say explicitly that we haven't reached the famous level yet but we're close.
7. **Benchmark card (~16%, at ~65–80%).** The famous instance from the cold open: give it a rhyming or celebrity epithet, call back the cold-open date, run the history cascade, then flip the original victims to the viewer in a 3-word second-person sting. Teach the real cause with a "not because of X, but because of Y" line (the long aftermath, not the flash). End the aftermath on a pop-culture survivor image.
8. **Breather + false peak (~2%, at ~82%).** A narrator reaction, a one-line recap of the scorecard so far, a joke, then a claim that the benchmark was not the top, a short escalation line and a rhetorical question voicing the viewer, to re-open the long size loop.
9. **Top card (~13%).** Go farthest back in time with an alien-Earth scene. State its size as a multiple of the benchmark (closing the long loop). Add the counterintuitive twist, then turn it back to the present-day threat. Give the verdict *first*, then a fake stop (the narrator offers to end early, then continues), the biggest numbers on every ladder, and a one-sentence list of all earlier hazards framed as a sequence the viewer has already learned.
10. **Combination payoff (~1–2%).** Finally compute the title: the small ones together are survivable, the top two are not; one absolute line.
11. **Outro (~2–3%).** A comfort reframe (the viewer is safe in the present) that echoes the primer's reassurance line, then a cut-off tease of a related episode and the channel's brand phrase as the last words. No subscribe ask needed.

**Pacing targets:**
- Words: ~135–140 wpm for this documentary register; mean sentence ~13 words, with a 2–4 word punch line in every cascade (~6% of sentences).
- Verbal re-hooks: a pivot line every ~40–45 s of content; gaps up to ~90 s are fine only inside a cascade where every line is a new consequence (~one per 5 s).
- Units: a new named unit every ~3–5 min; the longest card goes to the most newsworthy unit, not the biggest.
- Ladders: run 4–5 numeric ladders at once (size, energy in one consistent unit, quake/intensity, crater/radius, reach), and step each up on every card; give every number an imperial conversion and a familiar comparison.
- Scorecard: one verdict line per card, one level higher each time, plus one explicit "not there yet" check before the benchmark card.
- Loops: the master "all at once" loop spans >90% of the runtime but needs a bridge line per card to stay alive; plant the benchmark in the cold open and close it at ~65–70%; hold a "bigger than the benchmark" loop from the roadmap to the top card (~80% span).
- "You": keep it rare (~15% of sentences) but in the same slot of every cascade, and save the bluntest second-person line for the benchmark card.
- Tone: quips throughout, including the last act; real missions, odds and dates in every card to license the worst-case exaggeration.
