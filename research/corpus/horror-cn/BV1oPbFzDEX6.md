## 2. Story outline (my summary)

1. **The warning.** Late at night, in a 500-member school group chat, someone asks "is anyone still alive?" The narrator (a student in a dorm) jokes that he's fine and about to sleep. The sender's reply is the title rule: don't sleep, sleeping kills you. He tells the narrator to check his roommates.
2. **The rule is confirmed.** Two roommates are dead in their bunks. They aren't breathing, and they're smiling. The usual snorer is silent. A dense, unnatural fog presses against the window. The third roommate can't be woken at first, then suddenly wakes. There is no cell signal, but Wi-Fi works. The room next door is all dead too.
3. **The world is cut off.** Only two other students answer in the chat:
   - a loner who stayed up watching a movie;
   - a sharp, analytical student who points out that nothing has reached them from outside for an hour. Trending topics and comments are frozen at the moment the fog appeared.
   
   Conclusion: the school has been "peeled out" of the world. Waiting for rescue just means waiting until you fall asleep.
4. **The escape fails.** Five survivors meet at the dorm door and walk toward the nearest gate in formation. The fog is dry, not damp, and it feels like it is watching them. Everyone gets drowsy. The loner collapses mid-route, and the rest run back.
5. **The fog is alive.** The analyst reframes the "watcher" as the fog itself. The fog exists in both waking and dream space. It can enter rooms in dreams but not in reality. So something in the dorm must repel it, and they start searching.
6. **Suspicion rotates.**
   - The roommate whispers that the first warner's message was *too precise*. A normal person would guess food poisoning or a heart problem, not "death by sleep."
   - The dead loner then comes back flying, with a scythe, through a second-floor window. The analyst says "split up," and everyone scatters.
   - Hiding with the analyst, the narrator is told the warner was bullied by his roommates and probably watched them die without helping. That makes him guilty of inaction, not the culprit.
   - The real suspect, the analyst says, is the roommate who woke from a sleep nobody could wake him from. How do you know who woke up?
7. **The death at the door.** The narrator voice-calls the warner, and the ringtone sounds right outside their door. The warner is standing there dead, smiling, phone in hand. His Notes app has one entry: "4 minutes 20 seconds."
8. **The gamble.** The analyst theorises that the smiling dead are in a pleasant dream space. There is a safe window between falling asleep and dying, and the note is that window. They must fall asleep *together* so they can meet inside the dream.
9. **The dream space.** A black sky merges with the fog. Rows of classmates lie on the ground. The roommate appears and gloats that he is the vessel for the "Lord of the Mist." The dreamers' joy is fuel for the Lord's descent. The two kill him. The analyst then offers himself: kill me, so at least you live.
10. **The detective reveal.** The narrator names the analyst as the Lord of the Mist. The analyst was "too smart, as if he'd read the script." The fog revived only one attacker. The analyst gave the dumb order to split up, and the others obeyed it. The roommate's confession made no tactical sense. So the narrator has been the only living person in the school all along. The whole night was staged to make him kill his friend in grief, which is the ritual condition.
11. **The generational coda.** The entity says the narrator is as clever as his father. Thirty years ago the father saw through the same trick and signed a contract: everyone gets revived, the father must have a son, the ritual resumes when the son is grown, and the father must never tell. The narrator realises that is why his father kept trying for a son after seven daughters, a dark-comic payoff. He asks "why my family?" and is told the family has "eerie blood." He signs the same contract, hoping *his* son will see through it too. THE END.

Short lines worth noting for analysis (no more than these): the title rule 千万别睡，睡着了就会死 ("whatever you do, don't sleep; fall asleep and you die"), and the reveal line 聪明的就像提前看过剧本一样 ("smart like he'd read the script in advance").

## 6. GENERIC vs SPECIFIC

**GENERIC: genre conventions anyone can use**

- A one-rule title premise in the form "Don't do [ordinary bodily act] or you die" (sleep, speak, blink, look back). The rule is stated in the first 10 s.
- A night-time group-chat ping as the cold open; the first message is "is anyone still alive / awake?"; the hero's casual reply sets up irony.
- Verifying the rule on the nearest person (roommate, partner, family), with a homely behavioural tell that is missing (snoring, a habit).
- Isolation that keeps the internet up: no calls, but the chat works. Social feeds frozen at a timestamp as proof of being cut off.
- An environmental agent (fog, darkness, sound) that turns out to be alive or intelligent.
- An escape attempt that fails, with the first on-screen collapse partway there.
- The dead returning as hostile puppets; the "how do you know the one who woke is still them?" impostor fear.
- Rotating suspicion among a small group of survivors; re-reading the chat history as evidence ("too precise to be innocent").
- A phone ringing just outside the door after you call someone.
- A dead person's phone holding a cryptic number note.
- Deliberately breaking the rule as the final gambit.
- **Helper-is-the-monster** with a detective summation: "you were always right, and that was your tell."
- **"You were the only one alive the whole time."**
- A villain whose victory needs a ritual condition (the hero must choose to do X).
- A loop or generational ending (it happened to my parent; now me).
- First-person past-tense narration ("我…"), a hero who reasons out loud, and short spoken lines.

**SPECIFIC: this author's expression; a transposed story must NOT reuse any of these**

- Names and roles: Chen Jie / Liu Meng / Meng Jun / Gao Xiang / Wang Qiang; room numbers 202 / 103 / 105; "school group chat (500)"; a university dorm with four-person rooms.
- The **exact rule** (sleep = death) combined with a **fog** that lives in both the waking world and the dream world.
- The "Lord of the Mist" (迷雾之主), its descent ritual fuelled by dreamers' joy, the smiling corpses as harvested happiness, and the rows of sleepers in a black dream field.
- The "4 minutes 20 seconds" Notes-app clue and the safe-window logic.
- The snore-silence tell used both by the hero and in the warner's message.
- The scythe-wielding flying revived classmate crashing a second-floor window.
- The bullied-warner backstory (guilty of not helping) as the misdirect.
- The specific reveal reasoning: "smart as if he'd read the script" + "why only one revival" + "the split-up order was dumb" + "the others obeyed."
- The sacrifice bait "kill me so you can live" as the ritual trigger (the hero must kill his friend in grief).
