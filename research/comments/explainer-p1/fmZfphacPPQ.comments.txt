[874] Your body would not 'implode' at 3000 meters just because of water pressure, this is a misconception. Our bodies are about 60% water, and water itself is nearly incompressible—so it wouldn’t get crushed outright. The real issue is the air we breathe. Air is highly compressible, which is why divers have to carefully manage and pressurize it. If, hypothetically, humans could breathe a non-compressible liquid (like fish use water across gills), there wouldn’t be a crushing effect. So, it’s not your soft squishy body that’s the problem; it’s the compressible air spaces in your lungs and sinuses that become dangerous under extreme pressure.

[525] Hello, scuba diver here, the painful sensation in your ears is the pressure in your ears becoming intense because of the pressure of the water and pushing on your eardrums. You can fix this pain by doing something called the valsalva technique or the valsalva maneuver and it’s where you pinch your nose and gently try to push air out of your nose as if you were exhaling normally but you don’t want to actually blow air out of your nose. If done proper it will balance the difference in pressure and relieve the pain temporarily (this technique will need to be repeated if continuing you dive deeper). If the technique is not done properly or at all then the pressure difference will increase and could cause your ear drums to rupture.
