[0] ive just had a look at the paper this is based on and personally wouldnt be that confident about the chemical mimicry claim.  /  / in the study, they looked at the surface waxes (cuticular hydrocarbons, or CHCs for short) of larvae and the termites they were housed with. CHCs are complex mixtures of waxes that many social insects use as pheromones to communicate colony identity (so, whether they belong to the same colony or not) which works by colony members interacting (grooming each other, feeding one another or just bumping into each other in close quarters) and the slightly different scent profiles of every individual mixing together to form a colony scent. workers then check others for colony identity by comparing their own scent to that of the other individual (the scientific term for this is the 'armpit effect'). /  / all that to say, the researchers looked at the CHC profiles of THREE (3) blow fly larvae that were kept with termite workers. they claim that the larvae had profiles that 'matched those of their colonies, even to a sub-colony level'. small sample size aside, with the identity and quantity of 44 compounds in the profile theres gotta be something there, right?  / yes and no! the profile the larvae have Does match, cant dispute that, but we dont know if thats because the larvae produce that mixture to match the colony (a very complex mechanism to evolve!), or if they have a CHC profile that is unintrusive to the termites as they 'infiltrate' the colony by just kinda hanging out in there long enough to pick up the odor of the colony from the workers themselves (the easier and more flexible thing to do). theres a bunch of species that do the latter with ants, and i think its kinda jumping the gun to imply chemical mimikry when you tested for it with larvae kept with a bunch of workers. i understand its a difficult experimental setup to test for it properly, but like... then dont claim chemical mimicry.... theres more than enough other really really cool stuff they found and described in the paper without overstating the chemical ecology! as a chemical ecologist whos very into social insects its a big bugaboo of mine 😅 /  / if you wanna read the paper yourself, its "Blow fly larvae socially integrate termite nests through morphological and chemical mimicry" by Schär et al. (2025) in Current Biology. its unfortunately paywalled but if youre at a university theyre probably subscribed to it!
