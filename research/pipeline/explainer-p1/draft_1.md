# explainer-p1, draft 1: Why does the deep sea make monsters?

## Topic note (read first)

The handed-over research disagrees on the topic. The reference brief (`refs/BREAKDOWN.md`) proposes
"how long you'd last in every prehistoric ocean"; the audience brief and the fact sheet both pick
**mzeN0BXR4e0, the deep-sea "why do they get creepier" premise**, and only the deep-sea facts were checked
this session. This draft takes the deep-sea topic (so every number below has a checked source) and keeps the
reference brief's **structure**: cover-frame hook with "you", three teaser hard cuts, a progress device by
~9 s, fixed-slot rungs that each change setting, a turn where the favourite falls, an answer plus a twist,
and a scrubber rewind that loops into frame 0. If the orchestrator picks the prehistoric premise instead,
this draft should not be used for it.

Angle (from the fact sheet): the "why" is answered plainly (no light, so no plants, so almost no food), and
the twist corrects the source title: the deepest fish is not a monster. Ending: the audience brief's
most-liked device, the point of view flipped so our floodlight is the alien thing.

Overlap avoided: no anglerfish at all (explainer-anglerfish), no pressure/body ladder (explainer-pressure-ladder);
the snailfish is used for a different point (it is not creepy), and no pressure numbers are spoken.

## Narration

```narration
Your sub sinks past two hundred metres, and the fish turn into monsters.

A secret searchlight. Teeth too big for its face. A mouth that fits fish bigger than itself.

Why? Blame what's missing.

First, light. Past two hundred metres it's too dim for plants, but hunters below can still see your silhouette. So the hatchetfish lights its belly to erase its own.

Past a thousand metres the sunlight's gone, and most animals can't see red. So the stoplight loosejaw hunts with a red beam its prey can't see.

Second, food. No plants means mostly dead scraps drifting down from above. So when a meal swims by, you can't let it go.

The hand-sized fangtooth has some of the biggest teeth for its size of any fish. The black swallower gulps fish longer than itself.

So the bottom must be worst?

Over eight kilometres down, the deepest fish ever filmed is a pale little snailfish. Basically a tadpole.

The monster faces live in the dark middle: no light, no plants, no meal to waste.

From down here, the weirdest thing around is your floodlight. Some fish are so black they bounce back under half a percent of it. To them, the monster is whoever brought the lights. You.
```
