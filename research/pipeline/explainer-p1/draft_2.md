# explainer-p1, draft 2: "Pick the shark"

**Blender: no.** Flat 2D house style A, built in code (Remotion/SVG) like every kurz-lane episode. Both
Kurzgesagt references are flat vector in every frame; FEEDBACK_LOG 2026-11-01 parks 3D/Blender; BRIEF rule 4
says everything visual is code. The realistic look belongs to the Zack D. lane only.

**Premise.** The reference brief's ladder ("how long you'd last in every prehistoric ocean"), told as a
paradox. The hook states the absurd true result first: you'd last longer next to Megalodon than in an ocean
with no animals in it. Then the scrubber **rewinds** from today, one era per rung. Each rung fills the same
slots (date tag, name tag, you-vs-it scale, one mechanism, verdict chip). The predators get stranger and then
smaller, the viewer relaxes at the turn ("Finally, safe"), and the last rung, the empty Archean ocean, is the
fastest death of all, because the air has no oxygen. The stinger closes line 1's frame ("pick the shark") and
the scrubber snaps forward to the Megalodon cover, so the last frame is frame 0.

**Topic note for the orchestrator.** The two research briefs disagree. The reference brief (refs/BREAKDOWN.md)
picks "every prehistoric ocean" (nAWpLtG-kNs, with qrfhhZ-Wvmk as proof it transfers to the ocean); the fact
sheet picks the deep-sea "creepier the deeper" topic (mzeN0BXR4e0). This draft follows the reference brief,
as the task asked, so the fact sheet's deep-sea facts are not used. The era facts below were checked
separately and need the same spot-check before voicing.

Why this order (and not oldest-first like ERAS/OCEANS): running the clock backwards keeps the master question
("how can an empty ocean be the deadliest?") open until ~75 %, as PLAYBOOK §9.9 requires, and the scrubber
moving left is the visible progress device (PLAYBOOK §1b, BREAKDOWN §6).

## Narration

```narration
You'd survive longer beside a Megalodon than in an empty ocean.

Let's test every prehistoric ocean. No boat, no gear. Just you.

Today: hours. No wild orca has ever been recorded killing a person.

So, rewind.

Ten million years ago: Megalodon, a shark longer than a bus. But it hunted whales, and to it you're a crumb. Hours, probably.

Seventy million: Mosasaurus, a sea lizard about as long as a bus. It breathes air, so it surfaces right beside you. Minutes.

Three hundred seventy million: Dunkleosteus, an armored fish nearly as long as a car, with jaws of bone that wear themselves sharp. Minutes again.

Nobody can test this. Best guesses.

Five hundred million: the top predator, Anomalocaris, is shorter than your arm, and probably couldn't even crack a shell. Finally, safe.

So keep going.

Three billion years back, there are no animals at all. Nothing to bite you. Just air with almost no oxygen. One breath, and you black out in under a minute.

The deadliest ocean ever wasn't full of monsters. It was empty.

So if you ever have to choose, pick the shark.
```

## Facts used (every number; check again before voicing)

| Claim in script | Basis | Confidence / note |
|---|---|---|
| No wild orca has ever been recorded killing a person | No documented fatal attack by a wild orca (only captive incidents); OCEANS fact note confirms | High |
| Today you'd float for hours | Survival afloat without gear is limited by exhaustion and water temperature (hours, longer in warm water) | Playful estimate; "hours" is conservative |
| Megalodon ~10 million years ago | *Otodus megalodon* ranged ~20-3.6 Ma (Miocene-Pliocene) | High |
| Megalodon longer than a bus | Size estimates ~15-20 m (e.g. Cooper et al. 2022 ~16 m); city bus ~12 m | High (all estimates exceed a bus) |
| It hunted whales | Megalodon bite marks on fossil whale bones (e.g. Collareta et al. 2017; Godfrey et al.) | High; "you're a crumb" is a joke, hedged "probably" |
| Mosasaurus ~70 million years ago | *Mosasaurus hoffmannii* ~72-66 Ma (Maastrichtian) | High; say "seventy million" |
| Sea lizard about as long as a bus | Mosasaurs are squamates (related to monitor lizards/snakes); *M. hoffmannii* estimates ~11-13 m in recent work (older estimates up to ~17 m) | Medium-high; "about as long as a bus" stays inside the range |
| It breathes air | Mosasaurs were air-breathing reptiles and had to surface | High |
| Dunkleosteus ~370 million years ago | *Dunkleosteus terrelli*, Late Devonian (Famennian, ~372-359 Ma) | High |
| Armored fish nearly as long as a car | Engelman 2023 (*Diversity*): ~3.4 m typical, up to ~4.1 m; average car ~4.5 m | High; do not say "bus" |
| No teeth, jaws of bone that wear themselves sharp | Placoderm gnathal plates, self-sharpened by wear against each other (Anderson & Westneat 2007, *Biol. Lett.*) | High |
| Anomalocaris ~500 million years ago, top predator | *Anomalocaris canadensis*, Burgess Shale ~508 Ma; widely called the Cambrian's apex predator | High |
| Shorter than your arm | Body length estimates ~30-50 cm (older 1 m claims are not supported; Daley & Edgecombe 2014) | Medium-high; check before voicing |
| Probably couldn't even crack a shell | Bicknell et al. 2023 (*Proc. R. Soc. B*): frontal appendages modelled as too weak for hard-shelled trilobites; likely took soft prey | Medium; hedged "probably" |
| Three billion years back, no animals at all | Earliest animals ~0.6-0.8 Ga; at ~3 Ga life was microbial only | High |
| Air with almost no oxygen | Before the Great Oxidation Event (~2.4 Ga), atmospheric O₂ < 0.001 % of present level (Pavlov & Kasting 2002, mass-independent sulfur isotopes) | High |
| One breath, and you black out in under a minute | Oxygen-free gas: loss of consciousness within seconds to under a minute (US Chemical Safety Board, nitrogen asphyxiation bulletin, 2003) | Medium-high; the exact time varies |
| The deadliest ocean was the empty one | Follows from the verdicts above: < 1 min vs minutes/hours | Framing claim; all verdicts are labelled best guesses on screen |

Avoided on purpose: Cambrian oxygen numbers (estimates vary widely, OCEANS's "Everest" line overstates them,
so the Cambrian chip reads "HOURS?"); Dunkleosteus as bus-sized and bite-force psi figures; Cameroceras
lengths; Jaekelopterus (Devonian, not Silurian); a Megalodon maximum length; Archean sky colour and Moon
distance (set dressing stays neutral: pale water, bare rock, stromatolites, dim sun).

## World consistency notes for the build (FEEDBACK P2)

- Today: modern open ocean; one orca only, no reef.
- Miocene: open ocean, baleen whale as prey; no ammonites (extinct by then).
- Late Cretaceous: ammonites allowed; no whales, no Megalodon.
- Late Devonian: armored fish, ammonoids allowed; no reptiles, no sharks bigger than the placoderm.
- Cambrian: shallow seafloor, trilobites, sponges; no fish predators, nothing on land.
- Archean: no animals, no plants on land, no green seaweed; only microbial mounds in the shallows.
- "You" keeps the same size in every rung so the comparators read honestly; the bus silhouette is the same
  length in rungs 6 and 7.
