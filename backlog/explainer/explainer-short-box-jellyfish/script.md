# You Have 2 Minutes After A Box Jellyfish Sting (Short)

**Premise.** The sting has already happened in the first line, and a red 2:00 countdown is on screen from frame 0.
We meet the near-invisible animal, learn what its stinging cells do, and the clock gets spoken out loud as the
body ladder climbs (1:30, 1:20). At the turnstile it becomes a game: your options, each one knocked out by a fact
you just learned. Vinegar works, and then the catch: the pain from rung 3 has already put you down in the shallows,
so you can't pour it yourself. Someone else stops the clock. Twist: it wasn't looking at you. Stinger: it comes back
every season, still invisible.

Master loop: "two minutes until what, and can I stop it?" Opens 0:00, closes on "someone else" at 1:01.3 (78%).
Micro loops: what touched you (0:00 -> 0:11), how fast (0:22 -> 0:23), how long you have left (spoken stamps at
0:28, 0:37, 0:58), which option works (0:37 -> 0:54), what the catch is (0:59 -> 1:01).

## Facts to verify (sources to check before publishing)

- Chironex fleckeri bell typically ~16-20 cm, up to ~30-35 cm across ("up to dinner-plate size"; a dinner plate is ~27 cm). Up to 15 tentacles per corner (up to 60), the longest up to ~3 m, so the longest outreaches a 2.4 m ceiling. Australian Museum; Queensland Health stinger pages.
- Nearly transparent and hard to see in the water. Australian Museum.
- Often called the most venomous marine animal (NOAA National Ocean Service; Guinness "most venomous jellyfish"). Hence the spoken "called", not a flat claim.
- Recorded deaths in Australia: sources give 64 to 70+ since records began in the 1880s (Fenner, Medicine Today 2002: 67 since 1884; Wikipedia summaries 64-69). On screen: "60+ DEATHS ON RECORD IN AUSTRALIA".
- Worst-case deaths within minutes (commonly quoted 2-5 min; NT Health "within minutes"). Most stings are not fatal (MJA 2005 Top End study: most cases mild). Spoken "Worst case" in the hook; on screen "MOST STINGS AREN'T FATAL" at 1:06.
- Nematocysts: capsules with a coiled thread, triggered by contact (cnidocil / chemical cues). Standard cnidarian biology.
- Discharge acceleration up to ~5.4 million g, measured in Hydra (Nüchter et al., 2006, Current Biology), not in Chironex. Spoken "in a cousin"; on screen "MEASURED IN HYDRA, A RELATIVE".
- Rifle bullet in the barrel: 5.56 mm at ~990 m/s over a 0.51 m barrel averages ~98,000 g (v^2 / 2L); a 9 mm pistol averages ~65,000 g. 5.4 M g is ~55x the rifle figure, so "fifty times a rifle bullet" is conservative. On screen the bar reads "~100,000 G".
- The thread fires in microseconds and pain signals take tens to hundreds of milliseconds, so "the venom's in before you feel it" is literally true even though the pain is described as immediate. Shown as a plain 1-2 order (VENOM IN, YOU FEEL IT), no invented number.
- Severe stings cause immediate severe pain and can cause collapse and drowning. ARC Guideline 9.4.5; Queensland Health.
- Venom porins make pores in cell membranes, releasing potassium (hyperkalaemia) that can cause cardiovascular collapse (Yanagihara and Shohet, 2012, PLoS ONE).
- First aid for tropical Australian box jellyfish (ARC 9.4.5, SLSA, St John): get the person out of the water, call 000, start CPR if they are not breathing, then pour vinegar for at least 30 s. If no vinegar: pick the tentacles off and rinse with seawater. Do not rub; do not use fresh water. Urine is not recommended. Vinegar stops undischarged nematocysts from firing; it does nothing for venom already injected (Welfare et al. 2014 question whether it is enough). Scope tag on screen: "TROPICAL AUSTRALIAN BOX JELLYFISH" (US guidance differs; vinegar can worsen bluebottle stings).
- Rubbing and fresh water trigger undischarged nematocysts. ARC 9.4.5; Queensland Health. Urine is ~95% water, so it is a hypotonic rinse with variable effects (basis of the "fresh water with extra steps" joke).
- Vinegar stations on posts at many north Queensland beaches. Queensland Government / SLSQ.
- 24 eyes in four rhopalia of six (Chironex; Nilsson et al., 2005 described the same arrangement in Tripedalia). Check a Chironex-specific source before publishing.
- Diet: prawns/shrimp and small fish; tentacles work like a fishing net. Australian Museum.
- Season: Queensland stinger season roughly November-May (NT from October). Spoken "every wet season"; on screen "STINGER SEASON ~NOV-MAY".
