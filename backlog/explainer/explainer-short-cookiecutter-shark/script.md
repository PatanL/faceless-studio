# The Shark That Bites Perfect Circles Out Of You (Short)

**Premise.** A shark shorter than your arm cuts round plugs out of whales. We name it, count its three tools, run
them once on a whale (the lure, the seal, the hooks, the blade, the twist, the plug), then run them again on YOU with
one new and worse fact (the depth of the cut, and what it does with the plug). Then we reveal it really happened to a
night swimmer off Hawaii in 2009. It ends on the circle it started on, back in the night water, under someone swimming.

**"You" plan.** The whale is the demo and the scale, never the victim the viewer identifies with. The viewer is at risk
from the first line. Every whale-half number is tied to the viewer's body: shorter than your **arm**, a kilometre below
your **feet**, the same three tools **you** would get, a plug bigger than a **golf ball**, as deep as your **thumb** is long.

**Spine loop (L1):** "how does something that small bite a perfect circle?" Opens 0:00, closes 0:36.3 (the plug).
**Second loop (L2):** "would it do that to me?" Opens 0:08.4 ("you included"), turned at 0:39.2 ("You don't."), played on you 0:39.8-0:49.5, made real 0:49.7-0:55.8.
**Micro loops:** "three tools" (counted at 0:12.1, re-counted at 0:24.0, paid 0:25.2 / 0:27.4 / 0:29.4); "bait" (0:20.8, paid when "anything it reaches" arrives at 0:21.3); "you just forgot" (planted 0:02.6 "Forget great whites", paid 1:05.0).
**Planted word, paid bigger each time: "circle".** Perfect circles (0:01.6, hook) → three tools for cutting circles (0:13.4) → healed white circles on the whale (0:37, visual) → "a circle of you" (0:48.9) → "you don't grow that circle back" (0:57.7) → circles on submarine domes (1:07, visual) → "one more circle" (1:14.2, the last word).

## Facts to verify (sources to check before publishing)

- Cookiecutter shark (*Isistius brasiliensis*): adults ~42 cm (males) to ~56 cm (females). "Shorter than your arm": an adult arm, shoulder to fingertip, is ~60-65 cm. (FishBase; Florida Museum of Natural History, Ichthyology, "Cookiecutter shark")
- Blue whale ~25-30 m. 0.5 m on 28 m is ~1.8%, and a 19 cm pencil on a 12 m bus is ~1.6%, so the visual-only "pencil on a bus" gag is honest. (NOAA Fisheries)
- Spends the day below ~1,000 m (records to ~3,700 m) and rises toward the surface at night. (Florida Museum; Compagno 1984, FAO Sharks of the World). 1 km ≈ 3,300 ft. The Burj Khalifa is 828 m, so the meter at 1,000 m sits just past its tip. This is visual only.
- Belly photophores glow green. A dark collar band under the throat has none, and seen from below it may look like a small fish that lures predators. **This is a hypothesis** (Widder 1998, *Marine Biology* 132: 669-675). It is hedged aloud ("Scientists think it's bait") and on screen (BAIT?, BEST GUESS).
- Fleshy lips and a strong pharynx make a suction seal. The small, narrow upper teeth anchor it. The large triangular lower teeth interlock into a single saw-like blade. The shark twists or rotates its body to cut out a round plug. (Florida Museum; Jones 1971, *Fishery Bulletin* 69). The rotation is inferred from the wound shape, not filmed. The VO says "twists", and the top-down shot turns the body about 300°, not a full spin.
- Wounds are roughly round, ~5 cm across and up to ~7 cm deep (Florida Museum). A golf ball is 4.27 cm, so "a plug bigger than a golf ball" is true across. An adult thumb is ~6-7 cm long, so "can cut as deep as your thumb is long" holds for the deep end of the range, and "can" carries the "up to".
- It eats the plug, which is its main food from large prey (Florida Museum: it "feeds on" the flesh it removes). It sheds the whole lower row of teeth at once and swallows it. Recycling calcium is the leading guess. (Strasburg 1963, *Copeia*; Florida Museum)
- Bite marks are recorded on whales, dolphins, seals, tuna, great white and megamouth sharks (Florida Museum 2013 great-white study). In the 1970s it bit the neoprene (rubber) sonar domes of US Navy submarines (Johnson 1978, *Naval Research Reviews*).
- 2009 (16 March): long-distance swimmer Mike Spalding was bitten at night while crossing the Alenuihaha Channel, Hawaii, and survived. Honebrink, Buch, Galpin and Burgess 2011 (*Pacific Science* 65(3): 365-374) call it the first documented attack on a live human. The Florida Museum puts the first bite on the chest and a second on the left calf. Our art places no wound on the body; a motif ring floats beside the figure. We do not name him. "The first person known to survive its bite" follows, because no earlier bite on a living person is documented.
- "You don't grow that circle back": the Florida Museum says such wounds need "plastic surgery to close the wound" and cause "permanent tissue loss". https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/science/first-cookiecutter-shark-attack-on-a-live-human/
- Not used (no source found tonight): how long this swimmer's wound took to heal, and any later bites on live swimmers. Don't add "and he wasn't the last" without a source.

## Shot list

Kit: `studio/src/kits/explainer`. Times are global seconds from the measured captions. **S** marks a scene boundary: one scene per paragraph, 13 scenes. A scene starts just before its first word (pipeline/episodes/auto.py), and only S rows may carry a `transitionIn` (cut, fade, dipBlack, whip, glitch). Every other shot change is a cut **inside** a scene: the outgoing layers get `end`, the incoming layers get `start`, and a `step`-eased camera keyframe jumps the framing. Camera presets are only none, pushIn, pullOut, panLeft/Right/Up/Down and drift. Anything else is written as keyframes.

**Assets** (inline `svg` layers; the kit has no cookiecutter, and the template rule is never a new component name):
- `CC_SIDE`: the hero, side view facing right, viewBox 0 0 400 110, mouth at (392, 58). Each overlay is yellow (#ffe14d) and normally at opacity 0, and its opacity is keyed to its spoken word.

- Honest-scale rule: whenever the shark shares a frame with a kit figure that has `pxPerM`, the `CC_SIDE` layer width is set by hand to 0.5 × pxPerM px (17 px at pxPerM 34).
- Rings: `RING` (yellow stroke circle). It never "draws on". It enters with scale 0.6→1 and opacity 0→1 over 0.35 s. `HOLE` (dark disc + soft edge).
- Planes: `SKIN_WALL` (pale warm grey with pores). `SKIN_WALL_HUMAN` and `XSEC_HUMAN` are colour string-replaces of `SKIN_WALL` and `XSEC_WHALE` (cream blubber / pink muscle → skin / fat / red muscle).
