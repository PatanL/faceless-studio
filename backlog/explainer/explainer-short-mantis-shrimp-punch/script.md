# How A Mantis Shrimp Would Hit You Twice

**Premise.** A peacock mantis shrimp (a "smasher") is about as long as your finger, and its club is one of
the fastest strikes in nature. Frame 1 makes it your size and puts it in front of you. The hook promises it
would hit you twice and holds back how. The ladder then runs on you: the real speed (felt as a pro boxer),
the naive scale-up with an open hedge, bullet speed at your face, too fast to blink at, the spring-and-latch
"how", and then the second hit from the collapsing bubble, which pays the hook. At the turnstile ("that's
usually the weaker hit") the axis switches to force: a big blue whale's weight, told through two boxing
jokes. A tone break flips it to reality. Small ones hide in live rock and turn up in home tanks, and one
finger-sized shrimp reportedly cracked its own aquarium glass. The stinger names it: Tyson, which pays off
the "pro boxer" plant from 0:07.

**Master question (spine loop):** what would this shrimp do to you at your size? Teaser in the hook: "hit you
twice" (paid at 0:31.5-0:41.7, 39-51%). The spine closes at the force peak, 0:53.9-1:01 (66-75%: "remember
neither", "no referee would bother counting you out"). The twist then gives the back fifth a real-world reason
to exist.

**Ladder axes (PLAYBOOK §4):**
1. *Speed, one quantity:* pro boxer (9 m/s) -> mantis club (23 m/s) -> scaled club ~400 m/s = handgun bullet.
2. *Kind of threat:* the club -> the water itself (bubble) -> force (weight of a blue whale) -> the real
   animal in a real tank.
The frame stays the same, with YOU as the target. Only the twist leaves the hypothetical, and it moves into
the viewer's own home ("you might only find yours").

**Science honesty notes (fixes from review):**
- *No "supersonic".* Sound travels ~1,500 m/s in seawater, so a 400 m/s club underwater is not faster than
  sound. Every "supersonic" and "faster than sound" claim, the SOUND 343 m/s bar and the whip are cut. The
  speed ladder now ends on "handgun bullet speed", which holds at any medium as a speed comparison.
- *One hedge, on the scaling.* "keep the timing, and ignore the screaming physicists" is said at the moment we
  scale (0:13-0:15) and is called back on the force line ("the same lazy math"). Measured values are never
  hedged. Scaled values always are.
- *Peaks said as peaks.* "can hit at double", "up to two and a half thousand times", on-screen "UP TO 80 KM/H",
  "UP TO 10,000 G".
- *"Usually the weaker hit"* is true on average. Cavitation peaks average ~50% of the impact peak, but they
  can exceed it (Patek & Caldwell 2005), hence "usually".
