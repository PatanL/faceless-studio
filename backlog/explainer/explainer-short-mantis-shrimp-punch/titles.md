Ranked best first. Patterns come from the title formulas in `research/corpus/explainer/PLAYBOOK.md` §2 and §7, and from the title notes in `research/library/index_notes/explainer.md`: every Shorts outlier puts "you" in the title, and most frame a personal danger or a negation. Views and outlier scores come from `research/corpus/explainer/INDEX.json`, apart from PsEZl1iV1h8, which is from `research/library/videos/`.

Guardrails:
- Keep "mantis shrimp" (the search term) in the first half when the formula allows it.
- Put the viewer in the title as the target.
- Never promise "supersonic" or "faster than sound". The video is set underwater, and sound in seawater travels at ~1,500 m/s.
- Avoid the saturated angles "punches like a bullet" and "if a mantis shrimp were human-sized" (Jon Bondy's clam video has 36M views, with many repeats; see the evidence jsonl). The two angles nobody leads with are "it hits you twice" and Tyson, so they lead options 1 and 6.

The on-screen hook ("At your size, this shrimp would hit you twice.") acts out option 1, so a viewer who clicks on the title gets it confirmed in 3 s (PUFFER's title-as-promise device).
