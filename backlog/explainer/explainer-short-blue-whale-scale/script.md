# 5 Rounds Against A Blue Whale (Short, 9:16)

**Premise.** A scale Short told as a five-round contest with one stated rule: bigger wins. YOU take on a blue whale's flipper, one breath, its newborn calf, one day of its food, and its biggest blood vessel. Each round turns one on-screen number into a spoken "N of you" and ends on a spoken 2-word verdict. The hook promises that you win exactly one round. That opens the spine loop ("which one, and how?"), and the four straight losses keep it open. The only win comes last (round 5, 91%). It is a myth-bust: the famous "you could swim through its arteries" claim is false, because you are too big for its aorta, and by the stated rule that makes you the winner. The stinger turns this into the irony that closes the video. The plant "Too small" (round 1) is paid off by "too big" in the last word.

**Progress device.** A scoreboard `Tag` stays top-centre (y 270 px, inside the 230 px top safe line) for the whole video: `WHALE 0 : 0 YOU`. It changes on each spoken verdict ("Point, whale." at 15.28 / 23.66 / 34.07 / 44.13 s; "Point, you." at 55.82 s). Five states, generated by the storyboard builder so the score can never drift from the VO.

**Two-axis ladder (PLAYBOOK §4).** Axis 1, the whale's margin grows: just longer than you (flipper) -> 5 of you stacked (spout) -> 35 of you (a newborn) -> 230 of you (one day of food). Axis 2, after the peak the kind of comparison flips: from "how much bigger is it" to "where are you bigger", and the scoreboard flips with it. The back third does not sag, because round 5 is the reversal the hook promised, held until 91%.

**Stakes (PLAYBOOK §9.1).** Every round puts you in the scene in the present tense: lie down next to the flipper, stand under the spout, be piled on a scale, try to swim through the aorta. The myth-bust is told as your own failed attempt, head in and shoulders stuck.
