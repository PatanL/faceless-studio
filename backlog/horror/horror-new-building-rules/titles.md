# Title options: horror-new-building-rules (2 parts)

Ranked best first. Each option names the Part 1 title and the Part 2 title it pairs with (both parts must work when found on their own, PLAYBOOK §5). Title patterns come from the horror corpus and library (`research/corpus/horror/INDEX.json`, `research/library/library.json`, `research/library/index_notes/horror.md` "Titles" row) and the topic evidence in `backlog/horror/evidence/`.

What the evidence says:
- On TikTok the corpus hits use hashtag-only captions, so the title does little there; the **cover frame** does the work (thumbnail.md). Titles matter on YouTube Shorts.
- The YouTube outliers have short first-person or imperative titles of 3-8 words ("I need your help." tpS0hPca-fk 653K, 3.5x; "SOMEONE IS WATCHING ME" Xv32FAAUI-0, 7.5x; "Someone Broke Into Our Hotel Room" Qz-TrB6W2SM 1.52M, 9.0x). Controls are long and stacked with hashtags.
- Rule-as-title and "If X... Y" titles carry this niche: "Don't Answer Your Mom's Voice" (Textify, 2k3w_XHRd1s, 2.74M), "If Your Dog Starts Barking at Night... LOCK The Door" (1yhOSytDEf4, 707K), "New Household Rules (Pt3?)" (BciSW_2VMZg, 104K, 11.0x), "Mom's Note Day 1" (cVUJmsVkUW4, 291K, 31.0x).
- A vague clock time alone fails ("2 : 48 AM.." epzTfKxp16E, 1.16x).

Constraints: no "pinned rules", "new rules in our building chat" or "Rule 4 / not home" wording (originality lens: the pilot owns those); the title must agree with the story's mechanic (the unit number); one part label per title.
