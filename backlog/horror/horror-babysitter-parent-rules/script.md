# The Parents Called. They Didn't Know Their Own Password.

**Premise.** Ivy is babysitting six-year-old Theo on the 34th floor. The fridge note has five rules; rule five, in red, says nobody comes in without the password, and if the parents call she must ask for it. They call. Mrs. Doyle doesn't know the password she whispered outside Theo's door at 7:40. As Ivy goes to hang up, Theo's extension picks up and a sleepy voice says it. The caller is delighted, so Ivy buzzes them up. Then the baby monitor shows Theo hasn't moved since 7:12. The rules read differently now: they have keys and never knock, and if Theo talks in his sleep, he isn't talking to you. The elevator she called stops on 34. Three knocks, and Mr. Doyle's voice says the password. Rule five says let him in, rule three says it isn't them; the chain slides an inch before she lets go. The door counts to five while she texts the real Mrs. Doyle, and finds a text she missed at 9:40: still at dinner. The door goes quiet. On the monitor, Theo whispers into a receiver that something under the bed holds to his ear, and then the monitor says her name. She doesn't answer. Mrs. Doyle replies: "What rule four? I wrote three rules." Rules one and four, and every number, are in pencil. "Get Theo. Get out." The house phone rings from Extension 2. Ivy follows the only real rule and asks for the password. It answers in her voice.

**Mechanic (one thing the viewer can track).** Whoever says the password gets trusted, and the password moves one step closer each time: Mrs. Doyle's mouth, the child's mouth, the door, Ivy's own mouth. Ivy acts on it once (she buzzes them up), nearly acts on it twice (the chain), and the last time it is her own voice asking.

**Plants and payoffs.**

- **Frame 0** shows three things the story pays: rule five in red; the lock-screen preview "Mrs. Doyle · 9:40 PM: Still at dinner!" beside the handset ringing "INCOMING · DOYLE" (two channels claim the parents at once: paid at 1:02.9, when Ivy finds the text she missed); and the note in two hands, with rules 1 and 4 and every number in grey pencil (paid at 1:28.2).
- **Rule five** is read in the hook (0:00), used at the door (0:49.9, it now admits the visitor) and kept at the end (1:34.0, "Her rule five"). Its full text is the same on the cover, the cards and the end card: "Nobody comes in without the password. If we call, ask for it. Wrong answer? Hang up." The VO at 0:12.2 quotes its last clause ("Wrong answer meant hang up").
- **The whisper "outside Theo's door at 7:40"** (0:08.5) plus "No movement since 7:12" (0:20.6): Theo couldn't have heard it, but whatever was under his bed could.
- **"Buzz us up"** (0:17.1) against rule three "We have keys" (0:32.8): the first logical tell ("So why buzz?"), and the reason the elevator climbs (Ivy called it).
- **Rule three vs rule five** is the note's planted contradiction (PLAYBOOK §1D rules note: at least two). The second is rule four ("He isn't talking to you") against a mother who doesn't know it, paid by the twist.
- **Rule four** binds her twice: it makes the whisper sinister (0:35.5) and it is the thing she obeys when the monitor says "Ivy?" (1:22.7).
- **Extension 2** is planted on the handset (0:12.2) and rings at 1:31.6.
- **The four "marmalade"s** are four voices: warm whisper, child through a phone line, a man through wood, Ivy's own voice through a phone line.

**Loops open at the cut (2 + 1 re-opened):** what is under Theo's bed; what now has Ivy's voice; and (end card, no VO) the handset on the floor dialling "DOYLE MOBILE". Closed before the cut: who called (not the parents), how the "child" knew the password (he didn't), will Ivy open the door (no), are the parents safe (yes, at dinner), can the note be trusted (no: two rules are forged).

### Narration (single-voice fallback for `pipeline/audio`)

The real build is multi-voice: each line in `lines.py` gets its own Kokoro voice and filter (`VOICES`, `FX`), and the SFX come from the storyboard's `_sfx_cues` (word-anchored, global seconds). The block below parses cleanly with `pipeline/audio/script.py` (checked: one `[music: dread]`, no `[cut]`, the two music dropouts as `hush=` on cues, SFX names that exist in `studio/public/sfx`). Use it only if the multi-voice mix is unavailable; in that case the four "marmalade"s lose their voices, which is the episode's main device.

```narration
[music: dread] I was babysitting on the thirty-fourth floor. Rule five, in red: if we call, ask for the password. [sfx: phone_buzz_long vol=-10] They called. I asked. What password, Ivy? We're downstairs. [sfx: glitch vol=-12]

She'd whispered it at seven forty, outside Theo's door. Marmalade.

Wrong answer meant hang up. Then the line clicked. [sfx: click] Theo's extension had picked up. Marmalade. [sfx: stinger @0.1 vol=-10] There's my boy! Buzz us up, honey. Theo knew it. So I pressed nine. [sfx: click]

The baby monitor showed Theo asleep, facing the wall. No movement since seven twelve. He hadn't moved to pick up. And I'd let them in.

Rule one. Asleep by eight. Don't wake him, whatever you hear. Rule two. Chain on till we text from the lobby. No text. Rule three. We have keys. We never knock. So why buzz? Rule four. If Theo talks in his sleep, don't answer him. He isn't talking to you. [sfx: glitch]

In the peephole, the elevator was climbing. Twelve. Twenty. Thirty-one. It stopped on thirty-four. Nobody got out. [sfx: ding] [pause: 0.7]

Then three knocks. [sfx: knock3 hush=1.6 wait] Ivy? It's Mr. Doyle. Marmalade. Rule five. Nobody comes in without the password. He had it. The chain slid an inch. But they never knock. I let go. [sfx: click]

I'll count to five, Ivy. I texted Mrs. Doyle. Are you outside? What does rule four mean? [sfx: message_send] One. Two. Three. Above it, a text I'd missed. Nine forty. Still at dinner. Delivered. Not read. Four. Five. The door went quiet. Then the monitor crackled. [sfx: static vol=-14]

Theo's lips were moving. He whispered, She won't open it. The phone was at his ear. Something under the bed was holding it. [sfx: heartbeat_slow hush=3.0] [pause: 3.0]

The monitor's talk light came on. Ivy? Rule four. Don't answer him.

My phone buzzed. [sfx: phone_buzz] What rule four? I wrote three rules. Chain, keys, password. The other two were in pencil. Both about Theo. Get Theo. Get out. [sfx: message_pop]

[sfx: phone_buzz_long] The house phone rang in my hand. Extension two. Her rule five. I asked for the password. [sfx: breath vol=-16 hush=1.2] [pause: 1.2] It answered in my voice. Marmalade.
```

## Pacing check (measured)

- **Runtime 100.5 s**: 317 words, 230 wpm while speaking (Kokoro on the Spark CPU), 189 wpm overall with 6.5 s of timed silence. At the edge of the 50-100 s Shorts band, down from 117 s planned in the previous draft (which was really ~123 s at the measured rate).
- **Hook**: line 1 is 7 words (babysitting + 34th floor). Rule five by 2.4 s, the call at 5.3 s, "What password" at **6.4 s**. 34 words spoken in the first 10 s (PLAYBOOK median 43.5).
- **Jolts**: 44 scenes in 100.5 s (median ~2.3 s per shot). Every scene changes something the viewer can name (a readout, a rule, a count, a bubble). The longest still stretch is the peak: the arm settles at 76.7 s and the frame holds to 80.0 s, 3.0 s of it silent (`check_storyboards.py` max hold 4.3 s).
- **Shape**: hook 0-6%; plant 8%; compliance 12-20%; proof 20-27%; rules lull 27-39% (cards every ~3 s, MOM-NOTE style); proximity 39-45%; **turn 45-52%** (the password at the door, rule five now admits the visitor); near-miss 53%; lifeline under the count 55-69%; **peak image 71-80%**; temptation 80-84%; **twist 84-90%**; voice swap 98%; end card 98.9-100.5.
- **Rules**: 5 stated; rule 3 broken by the knock; rule 5 inverted at the door; rule 4 obeyed as an action; two contradictions planted (rule 5 vs rule 3; rule 4 vs the mother); the twist forges rules 1 and 4.
- **Ladders**: proximity (lobby, elevator 12/20/31/34, door, Theo's bed, the handset in Ivy's hand, Ivy's own voice) and channel removal (house phone → monitor → texts unread → the door goes quiet → the house phone belongs to it). Volume at the door: polite password, count, silence.
- **Numbers**: 7:40, 7:12, 9:40, 34th, 12, 20, 31, 34, rules 1-5, the count 1-5, Extension 2. 30 spoken number words (17.9 per minute; the rule ordinals and the count inflate it; clock times and floors alone are ~5 per minute).
- **Viewer proxy**: "So why buzz?" (the keys tell), "What does rule four mean?" (Ivy asks the viewer's question to the real authority, and it gets answered).
- **Silences**: 0.7 s on the empty hall, 1.25 s of clear air for the knocks, 3.0 s on the peak image, 1.2 s breath on the line before the last word.
- **Sentences**: median 3 words, 71% punch lines; this script is built from fragments read as cards and readouts. Lines that carry the story (n1, n7, n12, n16, n23, n27) are 8-14 words.
- **Non-graphic**: no injury, no gore words; the arm holds the receiver and never touches the child's skin on screen. The cover shows a note, a ringing phone and a text, never the child (mark the upload "not made for kids"; keep Theo off every thumbnail).
- **No spoken subscribe**, no part card. Standalone.
