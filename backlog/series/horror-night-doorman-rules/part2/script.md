# Part 2/5: "Rule 4"

**Part summary.** 2:41 a.m. on Lena's first night shift at the Calloway. Line 1903 rings from an apartment rule 3 says is empty. She obeys Gus ("let it ring") and counts: it stops at 19 rings, the same as the floor. At 3:00 rule 4 makes her count the keys, and a 96th key, tagged 1903, hangs on the hook that was empty all night (96 hooks, one per apartment; 95 keys because 1903's went up with Gus). Gus says don't touch it. The visitor log answers Part 1's open question about how Gus knew: last night at 2:41, the same minute tonight's call came, he wrote "going up myself, back in five" and never signed out. He claims to be home in bed. Three slow taps on the board behind her; her right hand goes cold; the key is in her fist. Under Gus's log line there is a new one in her handwriting, "going up myself", with the time box still empty. Then the owner texts for the first time: bring the key to 1903. Rule 5 says do what he says; rule 3 says send nobody up.

**What this part pays and plants** (ledger in BIBLE §4):
- Pays (Part 1): "How did Gus know the door was open?" at 35.9 s (44%), through the log.
- Plants: 19 rings (pays P5); owner's real number 555-0178 on the staff sheet, on screen only (pays P3); "I'm home in bed" (pays P4); Lena's log line with an empty time box (fills itself with 4:12 in P4); Pell's 555-0143 = the disconnected 1903 line on the staff sheet (pays P3); the call came at 2:41, Gus's minute (desk clock, on screen; pays P5); the key in her right hand, palm soaked (pays P5).

[00.0] Rule three: nineteen-oh-three is empty. It rang my desk anyway. Nineteen times.

[03.3] My first night on the desk. The old night man, Gus, had left me six rules and one order: let it ring.

[09.0] So I counted. Seven rings. Twelve. Nineteen.

[11.1] It stopped on nineteen. Same as the floor.

[13.4] Eighteen minutes later, the desk clock hit three, and rule four was waiting.

[16.2] At three, count the keys. Ninety-five. If there's a ninety-sixth, don't touch it.

[19.6] Ninety-three. Ninety-four. Ninety-five.

[21.2] And on the last hook, empty since I sat down, hung a ninety-sixth key.

[24.6] The brass tag said nineteen-oh-three. And it was still swinging.

[27.7] I sent Gus a photo. He answered in one second.

[30.1] Don't touch it. Don't look at it. Put your back to the board.

[33.1] So I stared down at the visitor log under my elbow instead.

[35.9] The last line was in Gus's handwriting, from last night. Two forty-one. Nineteen-oh-three called. Going up myself. Back in five.

[41.1] No sign-out under it. Not in twenty-four hours.

[43.7] I asked him, did you ever come back down? Typing. Stopped. Typing.

[46.5] I'm home in bed. Leave the key.

[48.6] Behind me, the swinging stopped. Then the key tapped the board. Three times, slow, like knuckles on a door.

[52.9] I didn't turn around. But my right hand had gone cold.

[55.5] I looked down.

[56.1-60.1] *(HOLD 4.0 s: silence, heartbeat only)*

[60.3] The ninety-sixth key was in my fist. I never reached for it.

[69.6] No time yet. Nineteen-oh-three called. Going up myself.

[72.4] Then my phone lit up. A contact I'd never used. Mr. Pell, the owner.
