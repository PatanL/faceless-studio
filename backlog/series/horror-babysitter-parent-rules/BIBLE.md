# Rule Five: series bible

## 1. Logline

A babysitter on the 34th floor follows the parents' fridge note to the letter, until the note itself turns out to be half-forged by whatever lives under the six-year-old's bed. Over one night she has to work out which rules are real, get the boy out of his room, out of the apartment and down 34 floors, while every voice she trusts (the parents, the boy, the neighbour, her own) is being copied.

**Ownable mechanic (PLAYBOOK §8 "one ownable rule"):** rule five, in red: *Nobody comes in without the password. If we call, ask for it. Wrong answer? Hang up.* The password is **marmalade**. The series is a slow inversion of that rule: in Part 1 the password proves you're family, by Part 4 knowing the password proves you're **not** (only the thing under the bed heard it at 7:40), and in Part 5 the only proof left is the real rule three: *we have keys, we never knock.*

**Why a series and not only the standalone:** demand for rules-note and babysitter serials is proven (MOM-NOTE "Day 1" 291K, 31x, with sequels at 202K and 187K; "More creepy texts from the babysitter" 2.22M per backlog/horror/TOPICS.md), and the premise has five distinct barriers (phone, front door, child's room, hall, stairwell/lobby) that each carry one part. Each barrier is one rung of the proximity ladder (PLAYBOOK §4A).

## 4. The rule system and how it escalates

**The note (frame 0 of Part 1, the cover):** steel fridge, five numbered rules in two hands. Navy marker = Mrs. Doyle. Grey pencil = Theo, tracing his mother's letters as Mister Under dictated. Every number is in pencil (the forger renumbered the list). Bottom line in navy marker: *Emergencies: Mr. Pell, 34C (house phone ext. 3412).*

| # | Text | Hand | Truth | Broken / inverted in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Asleep by 8. Don't wake him, whatever you hear. | pencil (forged) | Keeps Ivy out of Theo's room | Obeyed P2 (temptation); broken on purpose P3 (she wakes him) |
| 2 | Chain on till we text from the lobby. | marker (real) | Real and protective | Strained P1 (the chain slides an inch); broken on purpose P4 (she leaves with no lobby text) |
| 3 | We have keys. We never knock. | marker (real) | The only rule that never fails | Broken by the Caller P1 (three knocks); the proof that saves them P5 (keys vs knocks) |
| 4 | If Theo talks in his sleep, don't answer him. He isn't talking to you. | pencil (forged) | The forger's one honest line: he is talking to someone else | Obeyed P2; revealed as forged P2 twist; re-read P3 when Theo talks awake |
| 5 | Nobody comes in without the password. If we call, ask for it. Wrong answer? Hang up. | P5 | The building itself (the elevator, the lobby) | none | 3 is the last proof: the real parents use keys; the copies knock on the glass | 1 (keys) saves Theo, then 0: the sting turns rule 5, the first rule, against Ivy's own voice |

**Mister Under's crayon rules (P3, under the bed frame, a child's capitals):** 1. DONT LOOK UNDER. 2. IF SHE COMES IN, BE ASLEEP. 3. WRITE MY RULES ON MOMS NOTE IN PENCIL. 4. SAY THE WORD WHEN I ASK. 5. DONT GO PAST THE FRONT DOOR. The fifth is why Theo says "Not the front door" (P3 cut), and it's the one the viewer argues about: a warning or the thing's own rule? (comment bait, §10).

**How the thing works (fair-play rules the viewer can track, never stated in full):**

1. **One ear, many mouths.** Mister Under (under the bed) is the ear; the Caller (outside the door, in the lift, the lobby pair) is the mouth. Anything said in Theo's room or just outside his door (through Extension 2, the monitor, or a whisper at the door) the Caller can repeat, in that speaker's voice. It heard the password at 7:40.
2. **It wears only voices that reached Theo's room.** Mr. Doyle, Mrs. Doyle, Theo, Ivy: yes. Mr. Pell: never. So in P4 "Pell" is Mr. Doyle's voice slowed and roughened (same am_michael family as the door, by design): the tell an attentive viewer can hear.
3. **It can shut any door; it can never open one.** Someone has to open it, or leave it open. P2: it shuts Theo's door (its move). P3: Ivy opens it, and her own pull leaves it held an inch ajar on the receiver cord. P4: Ivy unhooks the chain herself. P5: the lobby pair can't open the street doors, so they knock on the glass. A lift is not a door: it comes when called (P1 the lobby release Ivy buzzed brought it up).
4. **It learns the tests.** Because it hears everything said at Theo's door, it knows Ivy's checks before she uses them: Theo tells her "Mister Under says the word" in the hall outside his room (P3), so in P4 "Pell" plays dumb about the password.

Every impersonation in the series is checkable against these four facts. Review note (review.json): the earlier draft had Theo's door "swing open on its own" at the P3 cut and "Pell" in a voice that had never reached the room; both broke this list and are fixed in §5 and §8.
