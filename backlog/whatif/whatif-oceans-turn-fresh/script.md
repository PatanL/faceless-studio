Original script. **Premise.** At 1 a.m. every gram of salt leaves the sea at once. You are fishing off the quay in Bergen, Norway, where the Gulf Stream's warm water ends. The first effects are personal and almost nice: the spray tastes like rain, and the whole sea is drinkable. But fish blood is saltier than the new sea, so nearly every sea animal is dead before the boats go out, and for once you are saltier than the ocean. Then the map: the warm water off your harbour is pulled north by sinking salty water, and icy fresh water floats. At dawn the sea looks normal and your line comes up empty. In the first winter the twist lands on you: that current kept your winters mild. End card: the real precedent from 12,900 years ago.
