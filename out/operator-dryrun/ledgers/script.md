# The Ocean's Top Killer Has Never Hunted You. Here's Why. (Short)

**Premise.** An unnamed predator flips great whites, kills blue whales and wrecks yachts, yet in the wild it has never
hunted a person. We build its power, its mind and its heart in three fast rungs, fake a reassurance, show the closest it
comes to us (it sinks the boat and ignores the swimmers), give the likely answer (sonar reads us as "not food"), then
reverse: the only orcas that have killed people lived in tanks. The close upgrades the question and loops into the hook.

**Master loop (L1):** "why has it never hunted you?" Opens 0:04 (P1), re-armed at P7 ("So why spare you?"), closed at P10 ("Not a seal, not food.").
**Hook contract:** great whites (paid P4, ~18%), blue whales (paid P5, ~26%), yachts (paid P9, ~56%). The quiet qualifier "in the wild" (P1) is paid by the captivity reversal (P11).
**Planted and paid:** "sonar" is hinted by the dialect line (P6, sound), used as the answer (P10), and threatened in the stinger (P12, ship noise jams it). "Spare" (P7) returns as the last line's verb.

## Facts to verify (sources to check before publishing)

- Orcas have flipped / held great white sharks upside down (Farallon Islands, 1997): Pyle, Schramm, Keiper & Anderson 1999, *Marine Mammal Science* 15(2): 563-568.
- Orcas kill white sharks off South Africa and eat the liver; sharks left Gansbaai for weeks to months afterwards: Towner et al. 2022, *African Journal of Marine Science* 44(2) ("Fear at the top"); Engelbrecht et al. 2019 (same journal). "A pair of orcas" = the males nicknamed Port and Starboard.
- First documented orca predation on adult blue whales, March 2019 off Bremer Bay, Western Australia: Totterdell et al. 2022, *Marine Mammal Science* 38(3).
- Name: from Spanish whalers' *asesina-ballenas*, "whale killer"; the orca is the largest member of the dolphin family (Delphinidae): NOAA Fisheries species page "Killer Whale"; Encyclopaedia Britannica "killer whale".
- A big adult male weighs around 6 t (NOAA: males up to ~6 tons, max records ~10 t). A mid-size car is ~1.5 t, so "outweighs four cars" uses the conservative end.
- Teeth up to ~7.5-10 cm long including root (NOAA; SeaWorld/Busch Gardens Animal Info "Killer whale"). An adult finger is ~7-8 cm, so "as long as your finger" holds.
- Orca brain ~5.4-6.6 kg vs human ~1.3-1.4 kg, so "about four times heavier" (Ridgway & Hanson 2014, *Brain, Behavior and Evolution* 83). We say heavier, not smarter.
- Resident orca pods have distinct, learned call dialects: Ford 1991, *Canadian Journal of Zoology* 69: 1454-1483.
- J35 (Tahlequah) carried her dead calf for 17 days, July-August 2018: Center for Whale Research; NOAA Fisheries 2018 updates.
- Iberian orca interactions since 2020: hundreds of boats contacted (over 500 by 2023), several sunk (at least 6 by 2024), no people injured: GT Orca Atlántica; Esteban et al. 2022 *Marine Mammal Science*; IWC 2023 workshop. We say "hundreds" and "several".
- The sonar explanation is a hypothesis, not proven: said as "Likely" and tagged BEST GUESS on screen. Odontocete echolocation can sense internal structure (air spaces, bone, fat): Au 1993, *The Sonar of Dolphins*.
- 1972, Point Sur, California: surfer Hans Kretschmer was bitten by what is reported as an orca and released (Wikipedia "Killer whale attacks on humans", citing period reports). The only widely cited wild bite. We do not name him; no wound is shown.
- No documented human death from a wild orca; all recorded deaths involved captive orcas (Tilikum, and Keto at Loro Parque 2009): Wikipedia "Killer whale attacks on humans"; Kirby 2012 *Death at SeaWorld*.
- Tilikum was involved in three deaths: 1991 (Sealand of the Pacific), 1999 and 2010 (SeaWorld Orlando): OSHA v. SeaWorld 2012 decision; news archives. We name only the orca.
- Ship noise masks orca echolocation and calls; Chinook salmon decline limits southern residents: Holt et al. 2009 *JASA*; Veirs et al. 2016 *PeerJ*; NOAA Southern Resident Recovery Plan.
