# The Warship That Sank in Its Own Harbour (Short, story lane)

**Premise.** In summer 1628, before the new Swedish warship Vasa sails, Captain Söfring Hansson shows Vice Admiral
Klas Fleming a stability test: thirty men run across the upper deck. After three runs the ship rolls so far that
Fleming stops the test. The king, Gustavus Adolphus, is at war in Poland and writes to get the ship to sea. On
10 August 1628 she sails in front of a crowd, heels in a gust, rights, heels again until the open lower gun ports
go under, floods and sinks about 1,300 m out; about 30 people die. The inquiry punishes no one. The cause: too much
weight high up (two gun decks of heavy bronze guns), too little ballast low down, and no room to add more without
bringing the lower gun ports to the waterline. Raised in 1961, now in the Vasa Museum, Stockholm.

## Facts to verify (checked 2026-11-02)

- Stability test, summer 1628: 30 men ran across the upper deck; Vice Admiral Klas Fleming stopped it after three
  trips, fearing she would capsize ("until she nearly rolled over"); the navy still sent her out. The ship's master
  testified that Fleming said he wished the king were at home: the admiral's quoted line is a paraphrase of that
  testimony. (Wikipedia, Vasa (ship), citing the 1628 inquest; Vasa Museum)
- Gustavus Adolphus was leading the army in Poland and sent letters urging that she be put to sea as soon as
  possible: "Get her to sea" paraphrases the letters. On 5 August 1626 he ordered seventy-two 24-pound guns (too
  many for one gun deck) and he had approved her measurements and armament: "the man who ordered the cannons was the
  king" is accurate. She was built with two gun decks of bronze guns. (Wikipedia)
- 10 August 1628: sails set in front of a crowd of hundreds, if not thousands; a light gust heeled her, she righted,
  a second gust pushed the open lower gun ports under, the sea poured in and she sank about 1,300 m out, inside
  Stockholm harbour ("inside her own harbour" is accurate). "The crowd keeps waving" and "the whole city" are
  narrative colour for that crowd. (Wikipedia; Vasa Museum audioguide "The shipwreck")
- Inquiry, 5 September 1628: no one found guilty; Arendt de Groote, of the builders' side, answered "Only God
  knows". (Wikipedia)
- About 120 tonnes of stone ballast; experts estimate she needed about twice that, but more weight would have
  brought the lower gun ports to the waterline: "too little stone below", "more stone would only have pushed those
  gun ports down to the waterline". (Vasa Museum FAQ and exhibit text)
- The bottle with sand is our comparison. The script track's draft (research/library/drafts/story-vasa-proposed.md,
  judge 97.8 estimated) is adopted with these checks.
