## Facts to verify

- No sunlight below ~1,000 m; deep-sea anglerfish (Ceratioidei) live ~1,000-4,000 m — NOAA Ocean Exploration.
- The lure (esca) sits on the illicium, a modified first dorsal-fin spine — Pietsch 2009, *Oceanic Anglerfishes*.
- The glow comes from symbiotic bioluminescent bacteria — Hendry et al. 2018, mBio.
- Most species are hand-sized or smaller (~20 cm and under).
- Depressible, inward-pointing teeth; distensible jaws and stomach let them swallow prey up to about twice their length — Pietsch 2009; Monterey Bay Aquarium.
- In some families, dwarf males attach permanently and fuse tissue and blood supply with the female — Pietsch 2005, Ichthyological Research; Swann et al. 2020, Science.
- Up to eight males recorded on one female — Pietsch 2005.

## Narration

```narration
[music: deep_ocean] If you're ever a thousand metres underwater and you see a little light... don't swim toward it.

There's no sunlight down here. So when anything glows, everything in the dark comes over to look.

That light is bait. It hangs off an anglerfish's head, on a fishing rod grown from the first spine of its fin.

The glow isn't even the fish. It's bacteria living inside the tip. They get a home, and the fish gets a lamp.

Most anglerfish are smaller than your hand. So let's scale one up to your size. [sfx: whoosh]

Its teeth point backwards, like a one-way door. Anything that goes in can only go further in.

Its jaw and stomach stretch so far it can swallow prey twice as long as its own body.

At your size, that's something as long as a small car. In one bite. [sfx: impact]

And that's not even the strangest thing about it.

In some species, the males are tiny. When one finally finds a female in the dark, he bites onto her side, and never lets go.

His body fuses with hers. They share the same blood. His eyes and most of his organs fade away, until he's basically part of her.

Some females have been found carrying eight males at once.

So if you see a light in the deep, don't swim toward it. And if you're a male anglerfish... honestly, it's already too late. Which deep sea creature should I scale up next?
```
