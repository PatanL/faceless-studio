(the lure, three tools, the spin, the scoop), then run the same bite on YOU, then reveal it really happened to a
night swimmer in Hawaii in 2009. It ends on the circle it started on.

## Facts to verify (sources to check before publishing)

- Cookiecutter shark (*Isistius brasiliensis*): adults ~42 cm (males) to ~56 cm (females). "Half a metre" is fine. (FishBase; Florida Museum of Natural History, Ichthyology, "Cookiecutter shark")
- Blue whale ~25-30 m. 0.5 m on 28 m is ~1.8%. A 19 cm pencil on a 12 m bus is ~1.6%, so "pencil on a bus" is honest. (NOAA Fisheries, blue whale)
- It spends the day below ~1,000 m (records to ~3,700 m) and rises toward the surface at night. (Florida Museum; Compagno 1984, FAO Sharks of the World)
- Its belly photophores glow green, but a dark collar band under the throat has none. Seen from below, the collar may look like a small fish and lure predators. **This is a hypothesis.** Show it on screen as "SCIENTISTS' BEST GUESS". (Widder 1998, *Marine Biology* 132: 669-675)
- Fleshy lips and a strong pharynx make a suction seal. The small, narrow upper teeth anchor it. The large triangular lower teeth interlock into a single saw-like blade. The shark twists or rotates its body to cut out a round plug. (Florida Museum; Jones 1971, *Fishery Bulletin* 69)
- The wounds are roughly round, ~5 cm across and up to ~7 cm deep. (Florida Museum)
- It sheds the whole lower row of teeth at once and swallows it. Recycling calcium is the leading guess. (Strasburg 1963, *Copeia*; Florida Museum)
- Bite marks are recorded on whales, dolphins, seals, tuna, great white and megamouth sharks. In the 1970s it bit the rubber (neoprene) sonar domes of US Navy submarines, which were then given fibreglass covers. (Florida Museum; Johnson 1978, *Naval Research Reviews*)
- 2009: long-distance swimmer Mike Spalding was bitten at night in the chest while crossing the Alenuihaha Channel, Hawaii, and survived. He is the "first documented attack on a live human". The bite was identified from the wound's shape. (Honebrink, Buch, Galpin and Burgess 2011, *Pacific Science* 65(3): 365-374). We do not name him on screen.
- Eiffel Tower ~330 m, so three stacked is ~1 km. This is visual only.
