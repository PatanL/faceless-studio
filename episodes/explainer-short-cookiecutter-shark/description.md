## YouTube (expanded description for the same 9:16 upload, 87.2 s)

The cookiecutter shark is about half a metre long. Next to a blue whale, that's a pencil on a bus. By day it stays around a kilometre down;
at night it rises with a glowing green belly and a dark collar that may look like a small fish to a hunter below (scientists' best guess).
Anything that bites meets three tools: lips that seal like a suction cup, small upper teeth that hook in, and a lower row of triangle teeth locked into one saw.
Then it spins its whole body and pulls out a round plug about 5 cm wide. Whales, dolphins, tuna and great whites carry the scars,
and in the 1970s it even bit the rubber sonar domes of US Navy submarines. In 2009 a long-distance swimmer crossing a Hawaiian channel at night
became the first documented living person it bit. He survived, and the round wound alone identified the shark.

Facts and sources:
- Cookiecutter shark (Isistius brasiliensis): adults about 42 cm (males) to 56 cm (females). FishBase; Florida Museum of Natural History, Ichthyology, "Cookiecutter shark".
- Blue whale about 25-30 m; 0.5 m on 28 m is ~1.8%, a 19 cm pencil on a 12 m bus is ~1.6%. NOAA Fisheries, blue whale.
- Spends the day below about 1,000 m (records to ~3,700 m) and rises toward the surface at night. Florida Museum; Compagno 1984, FAO Sharks of the World.
- Green belly photophores with a dark, non-glowing collar that may act as a lure. This is a hypothesis. Widder 1998, Marine Biology 132: 669-675.
- Suction lips and pharynx, small anchoring upper teeth, large interlocking lower teeth that form one blade; the shark twists to cut a round plug. Florida Museum; Jones 1971, Fishery Bulletin 69.
- Wounds are roughly round, about 5 cm across and up to about 7 cm deep. Florida Museum.
- Sheds and swallows its whole lower tooth row at once (calcium recycling is the leading guess). Strasburg 1963, Copeia; Florida Museum.
- Bite marks recorded on whales, dolphins, seals, tuna, great white and megamouth sharks; in the 1970s it bit the neoprene sonar domes of US Navy submarines. Florida Museum; Johnson 1978, Naval Research Reviews.
- 2009: a long-distance swimmer was bitten at night crossing the Alenuihaha Channel, Hawaii, and survived; the first documented attack on a live human, identified from the wound's shape. Honebrink, Buch, Galpin and Burgess 2011, Pacific Science 65(3): 365-374.
