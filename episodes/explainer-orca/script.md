## Facts to verify (paraphrased; check before publishing)

- No confirmed case of a wild orca killing a human (captive incidents exist) — NOAA Fisheries / Whale and Dolphin Conservation.
- Adult males reach ~8 m (record ~9.8 m) and ~6 t — NOAA species profile.
- Burst speed ~56 km/h (~34 mph) — NOAA; recreational swimmers ~3 km/h, elite ~8 km/h.
- Pods have distinct vocal dialects and diets (resident fish-eaters vs mammal-eating Bigg's/transients) — Ford et al.; NOAA.
- Wave-washing seals off ice floes, Antarctic Peninsula — Pitman & Durban 2012, Marine Mammal Science.
- Great white carcasses with livers removed near Gansbaai, South Africa from 2017; sharks left the area — Towner et al. 2022, Ecology.
- First documented kill of an adult blue whale, March 2019, off Bremer Bay, Western Australia — Totterdell et al. 2022, Marine Mammal Science.
- Iberian orca–boat interactions since 2020 off Spain/Portugal, rudders damaged, several sailboats sank — Atlantic Orca Working Group / CA2 reports.
- Adult male dorsal fin up to ~1.8 m ("its fin alone is as tall as you") — NOAA / Ford.
- 56 km/h = 15.6 m/s, so 50 m takes ~3.2 s ("reaches you in three seconds").
- Harbour seal ~1.2-1.9 m, 55-170 kg ("you're about the size of the seals they eat").
- Adult blue whale ~100-150 t vs a ~75 kg person = 1,300-2,000x ("a thousand times your weight", conservative).
- Sinkings (checked 2026-11-01): most outlets count **seven** boats sunk since 2020, the latest two off Portugal on 13 Sep 2025 and
  10 Oct 2025 (Ti'fare, off Peniche); some tallies count 5 sailboats + 2 fishing vessels. Narration says "about seven", stat "~7".
  Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_orca_attacks ,
  https://news.mongabay.com/short-article/2025/09/iberian-orcas-sink-sailboat-in-latest-ramming-game-in-portugal/ ,
  https://www.yacht.de/en/special/distress-at-sea/in-distress-orca-attack-sailing-yacht-sunk-off-lisbon-update/ — re-check on publish day.

## Narration

```narration
[music: deep_ocean] Wild killer whales could kill you easily. They never have.

Not because they can't. A big male is eight metres long, as heavy as four cars, and its fin alone is as tall as you.

It hits fifty-six kilometres an hour. An Olympic swimmer manages eight. You, maybe three. From fifty metres out, it reaches you in three seconds.

And you'd never meet just one. A pod hunts as a team, and you'd be outnumbered.

In Antarctica, a pod charges an ice floe together, and the wave washes the seal straight off. You'd go too.

Off South Africa, the shark you're scared of started washing up missing one organ. [sfx: impact] The liver. Orcas flipped them and took it.

The surviving great whites fled for months. If they run, what chance do you have?

In twenty nineteen, off Western Australia, a pod brought down the biggest animal on Earth. An adult blue whale, a thousand times your weight.

So why has something this fast and this smart never come for you?

Maybe you taste bad? Nobody's ever proven that. Too small to bother with? You're about the size of the seals they eat.

The best answer is culture. Orcas eat what their mothers taught them. And no orca mother has ever taught her calf to eat you.

Comforting, right? Except since twenty twenty, orcas off Spain and Portugal have been ramming sailboats and [sfx: impact vol=-3] snapping off their rudders.

[sfx: stinger @-0.1] About seven boats have sunk. Nobody knows why.

Maybe they just haven't decided about you yet. And that matters, because...
```
