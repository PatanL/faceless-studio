# The Chess Robot That Fooled Napoleon (Short, story lane)

**Premise.** In 1770 Wolfgang von Kempelen shows Empress Maria Theresa a chess-playing automaton, "the Turk". He
opens every door to show only clockwork, and it beats everyone for decades, Benjamin Franklin and Napoleon included.
After it burns in 1854 the secret comes out: a human chess master hid inside on a sliding seat, read the moves through
magnets under the board and worked the arm with a lever. Told RicksClips-style: protagonist and strange act first,
the doubting court, three escalations, the reveal last, the last line feeding frame 0.

**Loop.** Frame 0 = the inventor holding a candle at the open cabinet; the last line ("playing by candlelight") ends
on the candle inside the same cabinet.

## Facts to verify

- Kempelen built the Turk in 1769-70 to impress Empress Maria Theresa at Schönbrunn, Vienna. (Standage, *The Turk*, 2002)
- A life-size figure in Ottoman robes and a turban, seated behind a wooden cabinet; before games the doors were opened in sequence, a candle held behind, to show clockwork. (Standage; Windisch 1784)
- Toured Europe and America from 1770 to 1854 (with idle years), "eighty years" is a round figure. Played Benjamin Franklin in Paris (1783) and Napoleon at Schönbrunn (1809, operated by Johann Allgaier); the illegal-moves story and the swept board are the usual account. Napoleon lost.
- It burned in the Chinese Museum fire, Philadelphia, 5 July 1854. Silas Weir Mitchell's son published the full secret in 1857 ("The Last of a Veritable Chess Player").
- The operator sat on a sliding seat and moved as doors were opened; pieces carried magnets that pulled small iron markers under each square; a pantograph lever drove the arm; a candle lit the inside, vented through the turban.
- Hedges on screen: none. "Count" = Count Ludwig von Cobenzl, the first opponent.
