## Facts to verify

- Largest living reptile; males 6 m+ and ~1,000 kg — Australian Museum; Queensland DES.
- Ranges from India and SE Asia to northern Australia and the Solomon Islands; rides ocean currents, one tagged animal 590 km in 25 days — Campbell et al. 2010, Journal of Animal Ecology.
- Resting dives can last over an hour — Queensland DES / Campbell et al.
- Strongest bite force measured in a living animal, ~16,400 N — Erickson et al. 2012, PLoS ONE.
- Faster than humans in water; zigzag running is a myth, run straight; crocs rarely pursue far on land — Queensland "Be Crocwise".
- Most attacks happen in or at the water's edge — Crocwise.
- Crocodiles watch and learn human routines (same spot, same time) — Crocwise / NT Parks guidance.

## Narration

```narration
[music: documentary_pulse] If you're swimming off northern Australia and the water looks completely empty, that's when you should worry.

The saltwater crocodile is the biggest reptile alive. Big males pass six metres and weigh a full tonne.

And it goes out to sea. Tagged crocs have ridden ocean currents for hundreds of kilometres between islands.

It can rest underwater for over an hour. Only its eyes and nostrils need to break the surface.

When it strikes, it closes with the strongest bite ever measured in a living animal. Strong enough to crack a sea turtle's shell. [sfx: impact]

Then it drags you under and spins, until you can't tell which way is up.

So, your options. Swim for the shore? In the water, it's faster than you will ever be.

Run zigzag on land? That one's a myth. Just run straight. Crocs rarely chase you far from the water.

Play dead? It was going to drag you under anyway.

The only option that works is the boring one. Stay away from the water's edge. That's where nearly every attack begins.

Here's what surprises people. The croc you can see isn't the problem. It's the one that has watched you for days, and learned exactly when you come down to the water. [sfx: stinger @0.2]

So next time the water looks empty, remember. Empty is what it looks like when a crocodile is doing its job. Ever swum somewhere with croc signs? Tell me where.
```
