# The Puddle That Stopped an Army (Short, story lane, v1)

v1 (2026-11-03): the anonymous Storm Oil form. The farmer, his knee gauge, the neighbour, the laughing general, the
vanishing horses and the stuck boats are invented and played for laughs; the only stated fact is the "You see" line:
in 1672 the Dutch flooded their own land against a French invasion and kept the water about knee-deep on purpose (too
deep to march horses and cannon through, too shallow for boats, the ditches hidden under it).

## Narration

```narration
[sfx: creak] He flooded his own farm, with an invading army so close he could hear their horses snorting.

[sfx: knock] His neighbour drops his pitchfork. "The enemy's coming, and you're watering the cabbages?"

[sfx: splash] He just props the floodgate open, rolls his trousers to the knee, and wades in.

[sfx: knock] "Not yet," he says, as the water creeps up his shin.

[sfx: crowd] By morning, the whole village is furious, and his neighbour is pumping it back out with a windmill.

[sfx: tick] Then the water touches his knee, and he slams the floodgate shut. "That'll do."

[sfx: drums] Soon the army reaches the flood, and their general laughs so hard he nearly falls off his horse.

[sfx: knock] "They're defending their country with puddles!"

[sfx: whoosh_long] The general sends his cavalry galloping across the shiny water.

[sfx: splash] Seconds later, the first horse vanishes, rider and all, leaving one hat floating. Then another.

[sfx: knock] The general sends boats to fish them out, and the boats scrape to a stop in the mud.

[sfx: tick] Back at the gate, the farmer checks the water against his knee, and his neighbour quietly stops pumping.

[sfx: whoosh] You see, the Dutch really did this in 1672 to stop a French invasion, keeping the water about knee-deep on purpose.

That's too deep to march horses and cannon through, but too shallow to float a boat.

And under it, every ditch between the fields disappears, so any step could be a plunge.

[sfx: impact] By sunset, the general who laughed at puddles is wading home, soaked to the knee, carrying his own boat.
```
