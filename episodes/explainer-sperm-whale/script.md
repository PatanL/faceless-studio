## Facts to verify (paraphrased; sources)

- Adult males up to ~16 m (52 ft); the head is about one third of the body length. NOAA Fisheries, sperm whale
  species profile. 16 m / 1.75 m = 9.1 people head to toe.
- The spermaceti organ holds up to ~1,900 litres of spermaceti oil ("nearly two thousand litres"); a household bath
  holds ~150-200 L, so ~10 baths. Clarke 1978; Cranford 1999 (Marine Mammal Science) on the nasal complex.
- The nasal complex (spermaceti organ + junk) forms the click and sends it forward as a narrow, very directional
  beam. Møhl et al. 2003, J. Acoust. Soc. Am. 114: 1143 ("The monopulsed nature of sperm whale clicks").
- Click source levels up to 236 dB re 1 µPa rms at 1 m, the loudest recorded animal sound. Møhl et al. 2003.
  Rivals (same underwater scale): blue whale calls ~188 dB re 1 µPa (Cummings & Thompson 1971); snapping shrimp
  ~185-190 dB p-p (Au & Banks 1998). On-screen bars: 236 / 189 / 188.
- Foraging dives typically 400-1,200 m and 45-60 min on one breath. Watwood et al. 2006, J. Anim. Ecol. Below ~1,000 m
  no sunlight is left (aphotic zone; NOAA Ocean Explorer).
- Echolocation: regular clicks while searching, then the click rate rises into a "buzz"/"creak" in the final
  approach to prey; squid are the main prey. Miller, Johnson & Tyack 2004, Proc. R. Soc. B.
- Swimmers and researchers in the water with sperm whales off Dominica describe feeling the clicks in their chest.
  Paraphrased, attributed ("say"); e.g. Shane Gero / Dominica Sperm Whale Project interviews.
- Eardrums: 236 dB re 1 µPa rms is ~630 kPa at 1 m, far above any human underwater exposure limit; no measurement
  on a person exists, hence "on paper" and "nobody's volunteered to check".
- Codas: patterned click sequences used socially at/near the surface, softer and less directional than echolocation
  clicks (Madsen et al. 2002); families (clans) off Dominica have distinct coda dialects (Gero, Whitehead & Rendell
  2016, R. Soc. Open Sci.).

## Narration

```narration
What would a sperm whale's click do to your body?

A big male is sixteen metres long, nine of you laid head to toe, and a third of it is head.

That head is a sound machine. Nearly two thousand litres of oil inside, ten bathtubs' worth, focus every click into a beam.

And those clicks hit over two hundred and thirty decibels. No animal on Earth is louder, not even the blue whale.

It needs that to hunt. On one breath it dives a kilometre down, where you couldn't see your own hand.

There it clicks, listens for the echo, and closes in. The clicks speed up into a buzz, and the squid is gone.

So what does that feel like to you? Swimmers off Dominica say the clicks thump through their chest like a drum.

And that's from a polite distance. On paper, at point blank and full power, one click could burst your eardrums. Nobody's volunteered to check.

Good news: full power is for hunting, a kilometre down. Near the surface, they mostly trade softer patterns called codas, and every family has its own dialect.

So if one swims up and starts clicking at you, relax. It's probably telling its family about you.
```
