## Facts to verify

- Each ~10 m of seawater adds ~1 atm — NOAA Ocean Service.
- At 10 m, gas spaces halve in volume (Boyle's law) — DAN / NOAA Diving Manual.
- Nitrogen narcosis from ~30 m on air; "rapture of the deep" (Cousteau) — DAN.
- Free divers reach 100 m+ on one breath (records > 200 m no-limits).
- Deepest scuba dive 332.35 m, Ahmed Gabr, 2014; descent ~12-15 min, ascent ~13-15 h — Guinness World Records.
- Sunlight effectively gone by ~1,000 m (aphotic zone) — NOAA.
- Sperm whales dive 1,000-2,000 m+ and can stay down 60-90 min — NOAA.
- Titanic wreck ~3,800 m; ~380 bar ≈ ~390 kg per cm² (grand piano 300-500 kg).
- Challenger Deep ~10,935 m, ~1,086 bar ≈ ~1.1 t per cm² (small car ~1 t).
- Snailfish filmed at ~8,336 m (2022, Izu-Ogasawara Trench) — Minderoo-UWA Deep Sea Research Centre, 2023.
- ~27 people have reached Challenger Deep vs 600+ who have been to space (2023 counts).

## Narration

```narration
[music: deep_ocean] Every ten metres you sink, the ocean piles another sky's worth of weight on top of you. Let's see how far you get. [sfx: bubbles]

Ten metres. The pressure has doubled. The air in your lungs is squeezed to half its size.

Thirty metres. Normal air starts to make you feel drunk. Divers call it the rapture of the deep.

One hundred metres. Eleven times the surface pressure. Free divers come back from here on one breath. Most people wouldn't.

Three hundred and thirty-two metres. The deepest scuba dive ever. Minutes to get down. About fifteen hours to come back up.

One thousand metres. The sunlight is gone for good. From here on, the only light is the kind animals make themselves. [sfx: underwater_rumble]

Two thousand metres. A sperm whale glides past on its way to hunt. It holds its breath for over an hour. You'd get about two minutes.

Three thousand eight hundred metres. This is where the Titanic rests. Every square centimetre of you now carries the weight of a grand piano.

Ten thousand nine hundred metres. The bottom of the Mariana Trench. Over a thousand times the pressure at the surface.

That's like balancing a car on your thumbnail. Anything in you that holds air gave up a very long way back. [sfx: impact]

And yet, fish have been filmed more than eight kilometres down, drifting around like it's nothing.

Fewer people have stood at the bottom of the ocean than have flown to space. Comment the depth where you'd have turned around.
```
