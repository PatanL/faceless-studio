# The Ancient Shield That Heard an Army Underground (Short, story lane)

**Premise.** About 2,500 years ago a Persian army sent by Aryandes, the governor of Egypt, besieges the Greek city of
Barca (in what is now Libya). For months the Persians assault the walls and, at the same time, dig mines toward
them. A bronze-worker inside the city carries a bronze shield along the inside of the wall and sets it against the
ground. Elsewhere it is silent; over the mines the bronze rings. The Barcaeans dig countermines there and kill the
diggers.

## Facts checked (2026-11-02, against the queue's sources)

- **Siege, assaults and mines at the same time.** Herodotus 4.200 (Macaulay, lexundria.com/hdt/4.200/mcly): the
  Persians besieged Barca for nine months, digging underground passages toward the wall *and* making vigorous
  assaults. The draft's "one week the attacks just stop … they had stopped attacking the wall because they were
  digging under it" contradicts this. **Fixed:** the attacks go on; the pivot is "while they hammered the walls, they
  were also digging under them".
- **The bronze-worker and the shield.** Herodotus 4.200: a worker in bronze carried a shield covered with bronze
  round inside the wall and set it against the ground; elsewhere it was silent, over the mines the bronze sounded.
  Aeneas Tacticus 37.6-7 (Loeb, penelope.uchicago.edu): a coppersmith carried a bronze shield round inside the wall
  and held it against the ground; where digging was going on beneath, it became resonant; the Barcaeans dug a
  countermine there and killed many of the miners; "even now men use this means". Two independent ancient sources.
  **Fixed:** "face-down" dropped (neither source says which face); "lying in the dirt" kept.
- **Countermines.** Both sources: the defenders dug countermines where it rang and killed the diggers. "Dug straight
  down and broke into the tunnel" is the countermine, said plainly; the killing is left out (lane tone).
- **Place.** Barca stood on the site of modern Marj, inland, with Ptolemais as its port (Wikipedia, Barca (ancient
  city)). **Fixed:** "on the coast of Libya" → "in what's now Libya".
- **Date (contested).** Livius.org c. 512 BC; Wikipedia 515 BC; the Colorado coin project 510 BC. Said as "about
  2,500 years ago" (the queue's safe wording).
- **Duration.** "For months" (Herodotus' nine months is single-source; no number used).
- **Single-source points flagged by the queue.** The nine months (Herodotus only) is not stated; the trench-and-oath
  ending (Herodotus 4.201, single source) is not used. The shield itself is in two sources.
- **Dramatised (T7), marked here:** "every day"; the guards' jeer; the kneeling and listening; "for days, nothing";
  the dust shivering on the bronze; "in secret". The draft's invented "corner tower" and "the Persian camp goes
  strangely quiet" are cut.
- **Physics line.** "every pick striking below makes it ring like a bell": Herodotus "the bronze sounded", Aeneas
  "resonant"; Vitruvius 10.16.9-10 describes bronze vessels ringing from miners' iron tools at Apollonia (same
  principle).
