# House style bible: "Lantern & Ink"

Our own house style. The bar is the Kurzgesagt **quality tier**, while the **look** stays clearly ours. Every number here comes from the three measured studies in this folder, `study-kurz-space.md` (**KS**), `study-kurz-bio.md` (**KB**) and `study-infographics.md` (**TIS**), or from our own orca pilot measured with the same tools (**ours**). Section numbers such as "KB §3" point into those studies.

- Judge with `JUDGE_RUBRIC.md` (/100, side by side against reference frames).
- Machine-readable palette: `house/palette.json`, generated by `tools/house/make_palette.py`.
- Automatic checks: `tools/house/judge_metrics.sh` (runs on the Spark).

**Why code can reach this tier.** Remotion, SVG, Canvas and WebGL can render everything After Effects renders: flat fills, clip paths, masks, gradients, blend modes, blur, particles and springs. Kurzgesagt's advantage is authoring: layered shading on every object, dense hand-placed micro-detail, consistent light, real depth planes and motion that never stops. This bible turns each of those into a recipe with numbers, so a component can be built to spec and a judge can check it from frames.

**Copying rule.** We study Kurzgesagt's methods and never copy what they made: their birds, duck, faced planets, cell faces, isometric lab floor, porthole HUD, compositions, logo, jingle or wording. The same applies to TIS's cast and sets. §1 lists the deliberate differences, and the rubric checks them as gates.

## 0. The look in ten lines

1. **One lantern, one ink.** Every scene has one warm key light (the lantern, `amber`) and one cool ambient (the ink, `ink`/`dusk`). Shadows lean toward the ink and highlights toward the lantern.
2. **A bounded palette.** 10 ramps (§2). Shading colours are OKLab mixes of ramp tokens, never free picks. At least 85 % of each frame's pixels must sit on the palette (check P1).
3. **No black, no grey, almost no white.** The deepest colour is `ink-0` (#01121a, a saturated teal-ink). Grey mid-tones stay at or below 4 % of the frame. Our white is `bone`; pure white is only for glints.
4. **Every hero object gets the 9-layer stack** (§4): base, core shadow, stipple terminator, bounce, highlight, specular, rim, micro-marks and contact shadow, all from one light vector.
5. **Diorama depth.** 4–5 named planes. Each plane casts a soft "plane-gap" shadow onto the plane behind it, far planes fade toward the haze colour, and an ink foreground plane is cropped by the frame edge and softly defocused.
6. **The stipple terminator** is our signature texture: the edge between light and shadow is a band of graded vector dots, not a hard line (Kurzgesagt) and not a flat split (TIS).
7. **The light is an actor.** Shading is computed from a light vector, so lights can move, sweep, flicker and switch on, and every object re-shades itself.
8. **Nothing is ever frozen.** At least 95 % of frames change. Something new arrives every 2.0–4.5 s and a new composition every ≤ 3.5 s, and the camera keeps drifting between moves.
9. **YOU is always in the picture** whenever scale matters: the bone-white figure on its `signal`-red pad is our scale anchor and brand mark.
10. **Sound has a bed and a ping.** The music bed never stops. A pitched sonar ping marks reveals and scale beats, and every arrival lands an SFX on the frame.

## 2. Palette system

### 2.1 The ramps

Defined in OKLCH (perceptual) and gamut-fitted to sRGB by `tools/house/make_palette.py`. Steps go from 0 (darkest) upward. Token names are `<ramp>-<step>`, e.g. `tide-3`.

| ramp | tokens (step 0 → n) | role |
|---|---|---|
| `ink` | `#01121a` `#01212d` `#013041` `#00445a` `#155770` | the ambient: darkest darks, shadows, haze in water, foreground silhouettes |
| `tide` | `#036b6e` `#0a8b8f` `#0eadb2` `#35cbd0` `#7fe4e7` `#bef4f5` | water, glass, cool rim light, cool emitters (bioluminescence) |
| `kelp` | `#073724` `#065539` `#0f7c55` `#29a072` `#62c598` | life, plants, secondary cool subjects |
| `dusk` | `#041531` `#0c284f` `#1a3f73` `#315a97` `#507bbb` | distance, night sky, space field (kept at H ≈ 216°, below the violet band) |
| `sky` | `#237aa6` `#4699c6` `#74b7df` `#a0d2f1` `#d1edfe` | daylight sky and air |
| `coral` | `#712615` `#a1331a` `#cb492d` `#ea6e52` `#fa9a84` | warm subjects: flesh, coral, warm animals |
| `amber` | `#814202` `#b36303` `#dd8809` `#faac27` `#ffd167` `#fff2c0` | the lantern: key light, fire, emitters, heat. Hue drifts toward yellow as it brightens |
| `ochre` | `#5b431b` `#84622a` `#ac8545` `#d2aa6a` `#eccc99` | earth, sand, rock, wood, paper |
| `bone` | `#c5bdac` `#ded7c6` `#f4eee1` | our white: bellies, teeth, label plates, the YOU figure |
| `signal` | `#9e1421` `#dd2534` | brand accent. **Only** the YOU pad, the one key number of a beat, and immediate threat |

### 2.2 How a frame uses it (per frame)

- **1 key ramp** for the field (30–60 % of the frame): `ink`, `dusk`, `tide`, `sky` or `kelp`.
- **1 subject ramp, complementary to the key**: a warm subject (`coral`, `amber`, `ochre`, `bone`) on a cool key, or a cool subject (`tide`, `kelp`) on a warm daylight key (`sky` + `ochre` ground).
- **1–2 accents**, each ≤ 5 % of the frame. `signal` is ≤ 2 % and appears at most once per frame.
- The result is 3–5 hue families per frame (P5 ≥ 3; ours today = 1) and 6–9 k-means clusters of at least 2 % each (KB §1.1: 7–8).

### 2.3 Shading colours are mixes, not picks

All derived colours are **OKLab mixes** of tokens. In TypeScript, use `culori` (`interpolate([a, b], 'oklab')(t)`), or port the 30-line `tools/house/oklab.py`.

| derived colour | formula | notes |
|---|---|---|
| core shadow | `mix(base, scene.ambient, 0.38)` | ambient = `ink-2` underwater and at night, `dusk-2` in space, `ochre-0` on sunlit ground. Value drops about 0.15–0.25 and hue rotates toward the ambient (KB measured 10–25°; ours rotates toward 200°, not 260°). |
| deep occlusion | `mix(base, scene.ambient, 0.60)` | creases, under-belly, contact |
| highlight plane | `mix(base, scene.key, 0.22)` | key = `amber-4` (lantern), `bone-2` (overcast daylight), `tide-5` (cold emitter) |
| specular glint | `bone-2`; pure `#ffffff` allowed only here | |
| rim | `tide-4` (cool back light) or `amber-4` (warm back light) | always the opposite temperature to the key |
| bounce | `mix(base, ground or water colour, 0.30)` at 12–18 % opacity | lower edge of the shadow side |
| haze (atmospheric) | `mix(fill, scene.haze, h)` with h per plane (§6) | haze = the key field colour at the horizon |
| glow | radial gradient from the emitter colour to transparent, `screen` blend | §5.3 |

`palette_check.py` accepts exactly these families: tokens, mixes between adjacent steps of one ramp (gradients), mixes of any token toward any `ink`/`dusk` token (shadow, haze, fade) and mixes toward `amber-4/5`, `bone-2` or `tide-5` (light). A colour outside them is a palette violation.

### 2.4 Hard rules

- No `#000000`, no greys (S < 0.2) for anything bigger than a glint. Unlit objects in dark scenes drop to `ink-1`/`ink-2` silhouettes (KB §4: lit vs. unlit).
- Near-white (V > 0.92, S < 0.15) ≤ 1.5 % of a frame and pure white ≤ 0.3 %. KS/KB measure 0.1–0.5 % and TIS 1.4 %.
- Background gradients use at least 3 stops with a slight hue shift. A dark gradient spanning more than 1000 px gets the ffmpeg dither (§7).

### 2.5 Colour script

The key ramp follows the story, as in KB §1.2, where the ocean video rotates about 40° in hue and loses 0.8 V on the way down.

| scene type | key → subject → accent | reference for tier |
|---|---|---|
| sunlit shallows | `sky`/`tide-3..5` field, `ochre-4` sand → `coral`, `amber` subjects → `kelp` plants | KB D 0:56, D 1:26 |
| open water, mid depth | `tide-0..2` → `ink` silhouettes in the haze; hero `bone`/`coral` | KB D 2:50 |
| deep / night | `ink-0..2` field; only lit things have colour → `tide-4` bioluminescence, `amber` lantern | KB D 5:28, D 7:06 |
| daylight land | `sky` + `ochre` ground → `kelp` → `coral` subject | KB W 4:10 |
| space | `dusk-0..1` field (never violet) → `amber`/`coral` star → `tide` cold stars | KS L 3:14, L 6:54 |
| body / macro | `coral-1..3` or `kelp` field → `tide` or `bone` agents | KB W 1:26, W 3:18 |
| data board / scale | `ink-1` or `dusk-1` plate → `bone` tags → one `signal` number | KB W 2:22 |

Consecutive scenes change their key ramp or step by at least 2 steps (TIS §1 "one key per scene, consecutive scenes change key").

## 3. Shape language

- **Geometric construction, organic silhouette.** Build from circles, capsules, rounded rectangles and constant-width round-capped strokes (KB §2). Limbs, tentacles and fins are strokes with round caps; tapered strokes are allowed (Kurzgesagt keeps constant width).
- **Our corner rule: a "soft chamfer".** Hard-surface props (subs, buildings, instruments) use chamfered corners with a 3–6 px round. Organic things have no corners. Kurzgesagt rounds everything; TIS uses sharp boxes.
- **Sharpness is semantic** (KB §2): triangles only for teeth, spines, fins and threat.
- **No outlines on silhouettes.** Separation comes from value, temperature and the rim light. Measured outline response must stay ≤ 0.05 (Kurzgesagt 0.004–0.025, TIS 0.05–0.10, ours 0.29; check D5). Internal detail lines are 2–4 px in a ramp tone, never black.
- **Line weights at 1080 px frame height:** leaders 3 px; detail lines 2–4 px; limbs 20–60 px; micro dashes 3–6 px; stipple dots 1.5–4 px radius. Scale by frame height for 9:16 (1920 px tall → ×1.78).
- **Our motif: the ring.** Spots, eyes, lures, the YOU pad and label leader ends are drawn as 2-tone rings (outer ring + inner disc). Kurzgesagt's concentric spots use 3 tones and fill the whole spot; ours are open rings.
- **Hierarchy by size** (KS §7): one hero at 25–55 % of frame height (16:9) or 30–60 % of frame width (9:16), 0–4 supporting objects at 5–25 %, and hundreds of micro elements at 0.2–1 %.

## 4. Shading recipe (as code)

### 4.1 One light model per scene

```ts
scene.light = {
  key:  { dir: [-0.6, -0.8], color: 'amber-4' },  // unit vector TOWARD the key, screen space (y down); default upper left
  rim:  { dir: [ 0.7,  0.7], color: 'tide-4'  },  // back light, roughly opposite the key
  ambient: 'ink-2', ground: 'ochre-2', haze: 'tide-1',
}
```

Every component reads `scene.light`. Light can animate (§9.5), and all objects re-shade together. This is the consistency rule (FEEDBACK A3): every shadow side in a shot agrees with the same key direction.

### 4.2 The crescent primitive

`crescent(body, dir, d)` = the body minus a copy of the body translated by `-dir * d`. The result is a hard-edged crescent on the `dir` side. In SVG it is a `<mask>`: a white body, plus a black translated body, inside a `clipPath` of the body.

### 4.3 Opaque hero: the 9-layer stack

R is the object's radius (or half its height). Every layer is clipped to the body path unless noted.

| # | layer | geometry | colour / blend | numbers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L0 | **base + form gradient** | body | radial gradient centred `0.25·R` toward the key: from `mix(base,key,0.06)` to `mix(base,ambient,0.08)` | ΔL across the body ≤ 0.06, so it still reads as flat (KB: 85–87 % flat-fill pixels) |
| L1 | **core shadow** | `crescent(body, -key.dir, 0.30–0.45·R)` | `mix(base, ambient, 0.38)`, normal blend, hard edge | covers 20–40 % of the object (KB §3) |
| L2 | **stipple terminator** | band `0.06–0.10·R` wide on the lit side of the L1 edge | dots in the L1 colour; radius falls from 4 px to 1 px across the band; jittered grid, seeded | 60–200 dots per hero (§7.1) |
| L3 | **deep occlusion** | creases, under-belly, where parts overlap | `mix(base, ambient, 0.60)`, hard edge or 4–8 px CSS blur on the group | |
| L4 | **bounce** | `crescent(body, ground.dir, 0.12·R)` on the lower shadow side | `mix(base, ground, 0.30)` at 12–18 % opacity | |
| L5 | **highlight plane** | body scaled 0.55–0.70, offset `0.15·R` toward the key | `mix(base, key, 0.22)`, hard edge | 10–20 % of the object |
| L6 | **specular glint** | 1–2 bean/ellipse shapes in the highlight, upper-key side | `bone-2` (glossy: eye, wet skin, glass); omit on matte | each 1–4 % of the object area |
| L7 | **rim** | `crescent(body, rim.dir, w)`, w = 3–8 px at 1080p | `rim.color` at 85–100 % | on the side away from the key |
| L8 | **micro-marks** | 12–40 seeded dash clusters, pores, scale arcs, spots (as rings) | base ±6–10 % L, shadow-side marks take the L1 colour | §8 |
| L9 | **contact / cast shadow** (outside the clip) | ellipse under the object, offset away from the key; size and opacity shrink as the object lifts (`s = 1 − 0.45·lift`) | `mix(ground, ambient, 0.55)`, radial gradient alpha 0.7 → 0 | grounded objects only |

**Supporting objects** need at least L0, L1, L5 and L7. **Far-plane objects** keep L0 and L1 only, flattened by haze (§6). **Hero objects** need all applicable layers: the judge checks shadow, highlight, rim and contact shadow (J2).

### 4.4 SVG skeleton (Remotion/React)

```tsx
// body: SVG path string; R: radius; L = scene.light; c(token) -> hex; mixOk(a, b, t) -> hex (OKLab)
const k = L.key.dir, r = L.rim.dir;
<svg>
  <defs>
    <clipPath id={`${id}-clip`}><path d={body} /></clipPath>
    <mask id={`${id}-core`}>   {/* crescent away from the key */}
      <path d={body} fill="white" />
      <path d={body} fill="black" transform={`translate(${k[0]*0.38*R} ${k[1]*0.38*R})`} />
    </mask>
    <mask id={`${id}-rim`}>    {/* crescent toward the rim light */}
      <path d={body} fill="white" />
      <path d={body} fill="black" transform={`translate(${-r[0]*w} ${-r[1]*w})`} />
    </mask>
    <radialGradient id={`${id}-form`} cx={0.5 + 0.25*k[0]} cy={0.5 + 0.25*k[1]} r="0.8">
      <stop offset="0" stopColor={mixOk(base, key, 0.06)} />
      <stop offset="1" stopColor={mixOk(base, ambient, 0.08)} />
    </radialGradient>
  </defs>
  <ContactShadow ... />                                                      {/* L9 */}
  <g clipPath={`url(#${id}-clip)`}>
    <path d={body} fill={`url(#${id}-form)`} />                               {/* L0 */}
    <path d={body} fill={mixOk(base, ambient, 0.38)} mask={`url(#${id}-core)`} /> {/* L1 */}
    <Stipple band={...} color={mixOk(base, ambient, 0.38)} seed={id} />       {/* L2 */}
    {occlusion} {bounce}                                                      {/* L3, L4 */}
    <ellipse ... fill={mixOk(base, key, 0.22)} />                             {/* L5 */}
    {glints}                                                                   {/* L6 */}
    <path d={body} fill={c(L.rim.color)} mask={`url(#${id}-rim)`} />           {/* L7 */}
    <MicroMarks seed={id} count={24} ... />                                    {/* L8 */}
  </g>
</svg>
```

**Render-cost rules** (measured in `docs/POLISH_IDEAS.md` on the Spark):
- Masks, clipPaths and gradients are about free.
- Use CSS `filter: blur()` on whole plane divs. SVG `feGaussianBlur` costs +54 %, and per-element blur on many nodes costs +83 %.
- Glows are `radialGradient` fills, not filters.
- Use at most 5 full-frame blurred layers per scene.
- Keep grain out of Chrome: Chrome grain costs +70–105 %, so add it in ffmpeg (§7.3).

### 4.5 Emissive bodies (lantern, lure, stars, bioluminescence)

Emitters are never darkened by L1. KS §3c/3d recipe, in our palette:
- **Body:** a radial gradient whose hue moves toward the edge while V stays ≥ 0.85: `amber-5 → amber-4 → amber-3` (warm) or `tide-5 → tide-4 → tide-2` (cold). Limb darkening is done by hue (KS §3c), not by value.
- **Lit-side double rim:** a 6–10 px bright crescent with a 3–6 px darker line inside it (KS §3c).
- **Halo:** a tight core falling to half intensity at `R + 0.08R` and a faint tail reaching the background by `R + 0.35R` (KS: 0.1 R + 0.3 R; TIS moon: half at +55 px on a 170 px disc). The halo hue rotates outward toward the ambient: amber → coral → ink. Blend is `screen`.
- **Rays (optional, heroes only):** 12–20 soft wedges reaching 1.3–1.5 R at 8–15 % opacity, slowly rotating (≤ 3°/s) (KS §4).
- **Sparkles:** 4-point crosses with 1–2 px arms (±30–80 px) and a soft core, 6–15 per frame in emissive scenes (KS §4).
- **Receivers:** every object within about 3 R of an emitter takes the emitter's colour as its key (§5.2).

### 4.6 Glass, water and translucent bodies

Translucent bodies (jellies, bubbles, glass) are a 40–60 % opacity fill (KB §3 layer 9), plus internal structure strokes, a hard `bone-2`/`tide-5` glare band at 10–15 % opacity across the body, and a rim. Bubbles are rings with a highlight crescent in the upper key corner.

## 5. Lighting

### 5.1 Setups

| setup | key | rim | ambient | use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lantern** (default) | `amber-4` from the upper left | `tide-4` from the lower right | `ink-2` | underwater, night, interiors |
| **Daylight** | `bone-2` from above | `sky-3` | `ochre-0` (ground bounce) / `dusk-2` (shadows) | land, surface, sky |
| **Cold emitter** | `tide-4` from the emitter | `amber-4` | `ink-1` | bioluminescence, deep sea |
| **Stellar** | the star (`amber` or `tide`) in or near frame | the opposite temperature | `dusk-1` | space |

One key per scene (KS §4). Opaque objects show it through the L1 side, the L5 position and the L7 side.

### 5.2 Diegetic light (the lit/unlit pass)

When a light source is in the scene (a lantern, sub light, lure or searchlight), draw the world twice:
- a **lit** pass with the full palette;
- an **unlit** pass in which every fill is `mix(fill, ink-1, 0.75)`.

Composite them through the light's mask: a cone or disc with a soft 40–120 px edge. Objects inside the cone are in full colour; outside they become ink silhouettes (KB §4: D 6:30, D 7:06). The cone's interior is lifted along its length and fades to the background at the tip. This is how a "lantern" sells darkness. Cost: two renders of the vector layer, which is cheap.

### 5.3 Glow and bloom

- Glow belongs only to emitters (KS §4, KB §4, TIS §4). Non-emitters stay razor-crisp.
- Halo numbers are in §4.5. Bloom never smears over a shape's interior; it sits behind the shape.
- At most one full-frame light event (flash, white-out, light leak) per 30 s. It lasts 0.3–1.5 s and lands on a narration beat.

### 5.4 Rays and caustics

- **Water rays:** 4–8 soft-edged wedges at 5–12 % `tide-5` with `screen` blend. Each sways ±2–4° with a period of 6–12 s, and its opacity is driven by independent noise (KB §4, POLISH 1d).
- **Caustics:** a net of rounded light cells in `tide-3/4` near the surface band, slowly morphing (period ≥ 4 s).
- **Atmosphere (land):** at most 2 long diagonal light sheets at 4–8 %.
- Merge rays, motes and glow into **one** screen-blended layer per scene (+28 % cost for each full-frame blended layer).

## 6. Depth planes

| plane | depth (parallax factor) | haze mix toward `scene.haze` | blur | content |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P0 field** | 0.05 | — | 0 | gradient field, light field, far caustics |
| **P1 far** | 0.25–0.35 | 55–70 % | 0 (2 px allowed in dark scenes) | silhouettes only: no L5–L8 |
| **P2 mid** | 0.5–0.65 | 25–40 % | 0 | supporting objects (L0, L1, L5, L7) |
| **P3 subject** | 1.0 | 0 | 0 | hero + labels + YOU |
| **P4 foreground** | 1.3–1.6 | toward `ink-0`: 70–90 % (dark silhouettes) | 6–14 px CSS blur | cropped by the frame edge: rocks, kelp, coral, debris, bubbles |

**Measurable rules:**
- **Planes:** ≥ 4 planes in every environment shot and ≥ 3 in close-ups and diagrams (J1). KS/KB use 3–5 and TIS 3–5; ours today has 1–2.
- **Haze:** each plane farther back is mixed ≥ 15 percentage points more toward the haze. Far planes keep saturation ≥ 0.45, because fade adds value and hue shift, not grey (KS §5: S stays 0.8–1.0; KB: far S 0.3–0.65).
- **Scale steps:** 2.5–5× between planes (KB §5).
- **Foreground:** a cropped P4 element appears in ≥ 60 % of environment shots (J6). KB: "most landscape shots".
- **Plane-gap shadow (ours):** P2, P3 and P4 each get one CSS `drop-shadow(0 6px 18px <ink-0 at 30–40 %>)` on the plane div, so every plane casts a soft shadow onto what is behind it. Use one per plane, never per element.
- **Parallax:** visible in every camera move. A move is lateral as well as zoom, because a pure push barely separates planes. Foreground/subject speed ratio is ≥ 1.3 (KS measured 1.11 for its nearest layer; we exaggerate on purpose).

## 7. Texture

### 7.1 Stipple terminator (signature)

- **Dots:** radius 1–4 px at 1080p, on a jittered grid with spacing 2.2–3× the max radius, seeded by object id so dots never swim between frames.
- **Gradient:** radius ramps down linearly with distance from the shadow edge (project the dot onto `key.dir`).
- **Placement:** use it on heroes and P2 supporting objects, never on P1/P0. The band is ≤ 10 % of R, so it reads as texture, not halftone.
- **Also allowed** on cast shadows (a stipple fringe 6–12 px wide) and on big background masses as a subtle tone-on-tone pattern at ≤ 6 % ΔL.

### 7.2 Micro-marks (vector, never bitmaps)

- **Mark vocabulary:** dash clusters (3–5 round-capped dashes), pore dots, scale arcs, ring spots, barnacle rings, rivets on props, and growth lines on shells and rock.
- **Tone:** base ±6–10 % L (KB §3 layer 7).
- **Placement:** concentrated on the hero and at the frame edges. The centre background stays calm (KB §7).

### 7.3 Grain and dither

- Kurzgesagt has no measurable grain (KS §6, KB §6). We add only a **dither**: ffmpeg `noise=c0s=4:c0f=t+u` after the render, which hides banding in dark gradients (POLISH 3a/3b).
- No paper textures, no film grain, no bitmap overlays.
- The flat-region residual must stay ≤ 1.5 (frame_stats `grain`; Kurzgesagt 0.0, codec floor about 0.6).

### 7.4 Background patterns

Large blobs or our ring motif at 3–5 % ΔL on big flat fields (KB §6: W 1:24, W 4:32). They animate slowly, ≤ 5 px/s.

## 8. Detail density

Video medians over the 2 s grid. The Kurzgesagt values come from `judge_metrics.sh` on KB videos W and D, so they include title cards and data screens.

| measure (tool) | Kurzgesagt W / D | TIS | ours (orca) | **target** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| shapes ≥ 0.03 % of frame (`tis/density.py`) | 137 / 112 | 142 | 116 | **≥ 110** (D1) |
| micro-details 4–149 px (`tis/density.py`) | 633 / 658 | 843 | 584 | **≥ 600** (D2) |
| small specks 6–400 px brighter than local background (KB `particles.py`, cv2 port) | 124 / 134; 165–371 on environment stills | — | 63 | **≥ 120** video median (D3); ≥ 150 in environment frames (J4) |
| edge density (Canny px / px, `tis/frame_metrics.py`) | 0.047 / 0.042 | 0.040–0.051 | 0.026 | **0.040–0.08** (D4) |
| outline response (`tis/frame_metrics.py`) | 0.004 / 0.025 | 0.05–0.10 | 0.29 | **≤ 0.05** (D5) |
| named objects per environment frame (by eye) | 15–60 (KB §7) | 5–20, up to 40 | 5–8 | **≥ 15** (J4) |
| surface micro-marks on the hero (by eye) | dozens to hundreds (KS §7) | ~0 | 0–3 | **≥ 12** (J4) |

The shape and micro-detail counts barely separate Kurzgesagt from our pilot (116 vs 112–137 shapes), so they are floors only. What separates them is specks (2×), edge density (1.7×), outline response (10×+) and the hero's marks, which the judge counts by eye.

**Hierarchy:**
- One hero at 25–55 % of frame height carries the densest detail.
- A calm field covers 30–50 % of the frame (KB median top-cluster share 33–41 %).
- A micro layer (motes, marine snow, dust, bubbles, stars) is present in **every** frame at 150–350 specks. Marine snow and dust are bean-shaped specks of about 7 × 7 px in 2–3 tints of the field (KB §7). Star fields are 170–270 specks per megapixel, with the largest 10 % softly glowing (KS §5).
- **Close-ups and data boards** may drop to about 60 shapes, but they keep the micro layer and the hero's marks (TIS alternates sparse close-ups with dense establishing frames).

## 9. Motion

### 9.1 Frame rate

Render at 30 fps (production default) with every frame unique: 0 % duplicate frames, no stepped holds. Use 60 fps only for showcase pieces, because it doubles render cost.

### 9.2 Easing tokens (add to `core/anim.ts` when adopted)

| token | definition | use | evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `travel` | ease-out quad, or `bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` for faster arrivals | object travel, pans, landings: 50 % at 0.3 s, 90 % at 0.8–1.1 s for a 0.5-frame-width move | KS cube 1.1 s out-quad; TIS landings out-quad/out-quart, 90 % in 0.9–1.6 s |
| `drift` | sine in-out, ramps 0.6–1.1 s | camera drift between moves, ambient sways | KB ease-in 0.6–1.1 s |
| `settle` | spring `{damping 14, stiffness 180, mass 0.6}` | heavy arrivals: overshoot ≤ 7 %, 2 visible cycles, period about 0.35 s | KS settle −6.6 % → −4 %, period 0.35–0.4 s |
| `pop` | spring `{damping 10, stiffness 220, mass 0.5}` | labels, icons, bubbles: overshoot 10–25 %, settled in 0.25–0.5 s | KB pops 10–30 % |
| `exit` | `bezier(0.7, 0, 0.84, 0)` | exits; 60 % of the entrance duration | POLISH 4a |
| `anticipate` | a 6–10 % dip opposite to the move over 4–6 frames before `travel` | character and creature actions | POLISH 4b |

Nothing moves linearly, with one exception: a held establishing shot may use a slow linear pull or push (TIS: 8–10 %/s; ours ≤ 4 %/s).

### 9.3 Ambient layer (always on)

| element | rule |
|---|---|
| motes, marine snow, dust, stars | drift 2–12 px/s with per-particle noise; stars twinkle at 0.2–0.6 Hz with random phase |
| creatures | breathing scale ±1.5 % (period 3–4 s); fins, tails and tentacles get a sine sway with a 0.1–0.3 phase offset per segment (KB §9.2); blink every 2–5 s, never in sync across creatures |
| light | ray sway (§5.4); emitter pulse ±5–10 % (a lub-dub for living lights); caustics morph |
| environment | kelp and coral sway (period 4–8 s); bubbles rise 40–120 px/s with a sideways wobble |
| camera | drift between moves (§9.4) |

**Targets:** ≥ 95 % of frames show a pixel change (M1; KB 97–98 %; the floor is 80 %). The longest near-still run is ≤ 2.5 s (M2), except one deliberate breather of ≤ 6 s per video (KB D 9:50).

### 9.4 Camera

- **Drift:** between moves, the camera always drifts at 0.5–2 %/s zoom or 3–30 px/s lateral (KS +0.76 %/s; KB drift active 43–55 % of the time). It locks only on data boards, for ≤ 6 s.
- **Moves:** `travel` landings that reach 90 % in 0.9–1.6 s (TIS). Every move has a lateral component so parallax shows (§6).
- **Hand-held noise:** ≤ 6 px amplitude and 0.5° roll on noise. Turn it off while a label or number is being read.
- **Shake:** impacts only, ≤ 0.6 s, decaying.
- **Rack focus:** animate blur between P3 and P4 over 10–14 frames as an alternative to a cut between similar silhouettes.

### 9.5 Light as an actor (ours)

Because shading is computed from `scene.light`, use it:
- a lantern switching on reveals the scene through the §5.2 mask;
- the key sweeps ≤ 15° over a held shot, so highlights and shadows crawl;
- a passing emitter relights the hero;
- a lure pulse re-tints nearby receivers.

Aim for at least one light event per 30 s.

### 9.6 Cadence and transitions

- **Something new every 2.0–4.5 s** (M4; FEEDBACK B5; KS 2.4 s, KB 2.2–2.9 s, TIS 2.0–2.3 s). It can be an element, label, move, morph, cutaway or light event.
- **A new composition every ≤ 3.5 s** (M3; TIS 2.8–3.2; ours 10.6). Get it from camera moves through one large set, cutaways and overlays, not from cuts.
- **Hard cuts ≤ 2 per minute** (M5; KB 0.4–1.8 per minute), reserved for scale jumps and punchlines.
- **Transition library (build ours):**

  | transition | duration | reference |
  |---|---|---|
  | bubble/particle wipe | 0.6–0.9 s | KB T1 |
  | iris on our ring motif | 0.4–0.5 s | KB T4 |
  | diagonal wipe into schematic mode | 0.75 s | KB T2 |
  | push-in through the subject | 1.0–1.5 s | KB T3 |
  | light reveal (lantern on) | 2–5 s | KB T5 |
  | in-place morph | 0.5–1 s | |
  | whip pan with directional blur | 0.25–0.35 s | TIS |
  | depth descent with live counter | — | KB, FEEDBACK B6 |

  Use at least 3 different kinds per video.

### 9.7 Secondary motion and acting

- **Follow-through:** every appendage lags its parent by 3–6 frames (KB §9.2).
- **Morph, don't cut:** a state change is a morph with a ≤ 1.3× overshoot over 0.5–1 s (KB W 3:17).
- **Counters and bars** tick continuously; they never jump.

## 10. Characters and creatures

- **Real animals stay natural** (KB §8): correct silhouette and proportion, no human mouths. Personality comes from **eyes** and pose.
  - Our eye: a `bone-1` sclera ring around a coloured iris ring, an `ink-0` pupil (never black), and 1 large + 1 small `bone-2` glint toward the key.
  - Lids carry the emotion.
- **No faced objects** (no planets, cells or stars with faces). That is a Kurzgesagt signature (KS §8, KB §8). Agents that need personality get eyes only, and only when the script makes them characters.
- **No birds as mascots, no duck shapes, no bean-blob mascots.**
- **The YOU figure** (brand, `brand/avatar`):
  - a bone stick figure with round caps and a ring-shaped head, standing on a `signal` ring pad;
  - its height defines "human scale" in every scale shot;
  - it gets the same shading stack scaled down (L1 + L7 at least), a contact shadow, and idle sway;
  - it must never be a silhouette-free sticker.
- **Construction:** 5–15 rounded primitives plus pattern marks (KB §8), shaded with the §4 stack. Creatures and their colours stay identical across shots (FEEDBACK A3): keep one component per creature with props, never redraw per scene.

## 11. Typography and on-screen information

| role | font | size (cap height) at 1920×1080 / 1080×1920 | colour and treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| title / chapter card | **Anton** (condensed, heavy), tracking −0.01 em, 1–2 lines | 7–9 % of frame height / 4.5–6 % | `bone-2` on an `ink-0` plate at 85 % with a soft plane-gap shadow; enters by mask wipe, never wide-tracked thin caps |
| big number (the proof) | Anton, tabular figures | 9–14 % / 6–9 % | `bone-2`, unit in Montserrat 700 at 45 % size in `tide-4`; the key number may use `signal` |
| label ("lantern tag") | Montserrat 800, sentence case | ≥ 32 px / ≥ 34 px | `ink-1` text on a `bone-2` pill (radius = 50 % of height), soft plane-gap shadow (0 4px 12px `ink-0` at 35 %) |
| leader line | — | 3 px | `bone-2`, round caps, ending in a 2-tone **ring** on the object (our motif) |
| sub-label (mass, length) | Montserrat 600 | 55 % of the label size | `tide-4` or `bone-0` |
| captions (Shorts) | Montserrat 900 (pipeline default) | ≥ 44 px / ≥ 52 px | `bone-2` with a 6–8 px `ink-0` stroke; whole phrases; safe area (FEEDBACK B8) |
| progress device (FEEDBACK B6) | Anton numerals | 4–5 % | a vertical **depth/scale gauge** on one edge: `bone-1` ticks, a `signal` ring marker, a counter that ticks continuously |

- **Pacing:** at most 2 labels plus captions on screen at once. A label stays up only while its object is discussed (KS §10, KB §10).
- **Literal numbers:** numbers on screen use the narration's figures ("1,000 m", "30 tonnes").
- **Not ours:** wide-tracked thin caps (Kurzgesagt section titles), hard offset-block tag shadows (Kurzgesagt data tags), corner-bracket frames, logo bugs.

## 12. Sound design

| measure | Kurzgesagt | TIS | **target** |
|---|---|---|---|
| integrated loudness | −15.1 to −16.9 LUFS | −12.7 to −15.3 | **−14 ± 1 LUFS** (pipeline delivery target for Shorts and YouTube) (A1) |
| loudness range | 2.3–4.2 LU | 2.1–2.6 | **≤ 4 LU** (A2) |
| silence (< −45 dBFS for ≥ 0.4 s) | 0–1 per video | 0.1 % of windows | **0** (A3) |
| music bed under voice | 5–7 dB (gap p50), 14–16 dB (p10) | 12–20 dB | **12–18 dB under the voice**, ducked |
| narration | 150–170 wpm | 175–185 | follow `research/corpus/explainer/PLAYBOOK.md` pacing; natural voice first (FEEDBACK A1) |

**SFX rules:**
- Every hero entrance, transition, morph and light event gets a sound that lands within ±2 frames of the visual event (FEEDBACK A2). Target: ≥ 70 % of these events carry one; check by ear on the motion clip (rubric I).
- Most SFX body sits in 120–600 Hz, so they read on phone speakers (POLISH: [ME]).
- **Our sound vocabulary:**
  - a pitched **sonar ping** in the bed's key on reveals, scale beats and the YOU marker;
  - water and bubble foley; a low pressure thrum (sub ≤ 60 Hz, short) on depth beats;
  - soft wooden or marimba knocks for pops;
  - airy filtered whooshes for wipes.
- **Avoid** music-box or glockenspiel plinks as the default pop sound; that is the Kurzgesagt palette.
- **Sonic logo:** 2–3 s, a descending ping plus a low swell. It is ours; never their jingle.
- **Breather:** one music-only breather of ≤ 6 s per long video after the hook or at the bookend (KS I 0:15; KB D 9:29). None in Shorts.
